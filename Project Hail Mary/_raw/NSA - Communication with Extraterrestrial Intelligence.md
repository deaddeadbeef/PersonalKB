# NSA — Communication with Extraterrestrial Intelligence

## What This Source Is

Declassified NSA technical paper (originally 1966) by Lambros Callimahos, a legendary cryptanalyst. Explores how an intercepted extraterrestrial signal could be decoded using cryptanalytic techniques, treating the problem as a special case of breaking an unknown code with no crib.

## Why It Matters to PHM

Grace and Rocky's communication process mirrors real thinking about how first contact might work. Callimahos argues that mathematics is the most likely starting point, that signal structure itself encodes information about the sender, and that the problem is tractable using techniques from cryptanalysis. The novel's math-first bootstrapping approach is directly aligned with this framework.

## Key Takeaways

- An alien signal can be treated as a cryptanalysis problem: the "plaintext" is the intended meaning, the "ciphertext" is the received signal, and the "key" is the unknown encoding scheme.
- Mathematics is the safest first bridge because any technological civilization must understand arithmetic, prime numbers, and basic logical relations.
- Signal structure (repetition, entropy patterns, mathematical regularities) provides meta-information even before content is decoded.
- The author argues that decoding is harder than encoding: a civilization that wants to be understood would build redundancy and mathematical scaffolding into its transmissions.
- The paper anticipates many ideas later formalized in SETI/METI research, including Freudenthal's Lincos system.
