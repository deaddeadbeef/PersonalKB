# Frontiers 2024 — Synthetic Torpor Review

## What This Source Is

Review article in *Frontiers in Physiology* (2024) surveying the state of research on synthetic torpor — artificially inducing hibernation-like metabolic suppression in non-hibernating mammals. Covers recent breakthroughs in rodent models and the gap to human application.

## Why It Matters to PHM

The *Hail Mary* crew survives a multi-year interstellar journey in medically induced comas. This review is the most current assessment of how close real science is to that capability. The answer: promising but early. Torpor has been induced in mice and rats via targeted brain stimulation, but human application remains speculative and multi-year durations are far beyond any demonstrated capability.

## Key Takeaways

- Synthetic torpor has been demonstrated in rodents by activating specific hypothalamic neurons, achieving core temperature drops to ~25°C and metabolic suppression to ~30% of baseline.
- The mechanism appears to involve a central neural switch rather than purely pharmacological cooling, which is encouraging for translational potential.
- Duration remains limited: sustained torpor in rodents is measured in hours to days, not months or years.
- Human application faces major unknowns: immune suppression during torpor, muscle/bone atrophy, cardiovascular stability, and neurological recovery after extended states.
- Early evidence suggests torpor may reduce radiation damage (lower metabolic rate = fewer reactive oxygen species), but this is preliminary.
