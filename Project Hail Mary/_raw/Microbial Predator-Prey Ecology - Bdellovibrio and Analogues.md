## What This Source Is

## Why It Matters to PHM

Taumoeba's fictional role as an Astrophage-specific predator rests on the real scientific principle that microbial predation can be host-specific, ecologically stable, and population-suppressing rather than population-exterminating. *Bdellovibrio* is the canonical example. This source provides the evidentiary backbone for the `verified` and `plausible` confidence levels assigned to the Taumoeba-related chunks.

## Key Takeaways

- *Bdellovibrio bacteriovorus* preys on gram-negative bacteria with measurable host-range specificity — some strains exhibit narrow prey preferences.
- Under controlled laboratory conditions, *Bdellovibrio* can reduce target bacterial populations by 99%+ without eliminating them entirely; predator-prey cycles emerge.
- Long-term co-culture experiments show predator-prey coexistence through arms-race co-evolution: prey resistance mutations arise; predator counter-resistance follows.
- The lifecycle (attack phase → periplasmic growth phase → lysis → release of daughter cells) is well-characterized and takes roughly 4 hours per cycle at 28°C.
- Related organisms (*Vampirovibrio chlorellavorus*, *Micavibrio aeruginosavorus*) extend the predatory-prokaryote niche across multiple clades, suggesting convergent evolution of microbial predation.
- Lotka-Volterra models fit microbial predator-prey data moderately well; spatial heterogeneity and resistance mutations introduce deviations.
