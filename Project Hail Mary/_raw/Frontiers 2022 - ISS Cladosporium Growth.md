# Frontiers 2022 — ISS Cladosporium Growth

## What This Source Is

Peer-reviewed paper in *Frontiers in Microbiology* (2022). Reports results of growing melanized *Cladosporium sphaerospermum* aboard the International Space Station, measuring its growth rate and radiation-attenuation properties.

## Why It Matters to PHM

Astrophage is a radiation-eating organism. This paper provides the strongest real-world evidence for radiotrophic biology: a melanized fungus that not only survives ionizing radiation but grows ~21% faster in space-station radiation levels compared to ground controls. It also demonstrates that a thin fungal layer can slightly attenuate incoming radiation, suggesting living organisms could serve as biological shielding.

## Key Takeaways

- *C. sphaerospermum* grew approximately 21% faster aboard the ISS than matched ground controls, consistent with radiation-enhanced metabolism via melanin.
- A ~1.7 mm fungal layer provided measurable (though modest) radiation attenuation beneath it.
- The mechanism is consistent with melanin converting ionizing radiation into chemical energy, extending the "radiotrophic fungi" hypothesis from Chernobyl observations.
- The fungus is self-replicating and self-repairing, making it a candidate for passive bio-shielding on long-duration missions.
