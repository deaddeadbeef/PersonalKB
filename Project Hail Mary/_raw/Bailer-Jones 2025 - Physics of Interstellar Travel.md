# Bailer-Jones 2025 — Physics of Interstellar Travel

## What This Source Is

Technical review paper covering the physics of interstellar travel: the relativistic rocket equation, photon rocket performance limits, mass-ratio constraints, and the deceleration problem. Authored by Coryn Bailer-Jones, a well-known astrophysicist.

## Why It Matters to PHM

The Hail Mary's drive is essentially a photon rocket fueled by a fictional energy source. Bailer-Jones provides the rigorous framework for understanding why photon rockets are the theoretical ceiling for reaction drives, why they still require enormous power per newton of thrust, and why deceleration doubles the mass-ratio problem. This is the physics backdrop against which Astrophage's fictional energy density becomes necessary.

## Key Takeaways

- Photon rockets achieve the maximum possible exhaust velocity (c), giving the best theoretical specific impulse of any reaction drive.
- Even so, thrust = Power / c means ~300 MW per newton — photon drives trade enormous power for tiny thrust.
- The relativistic rocket equation shows mass ratios grow exponentially with target velocity. Reaching 0.1c and stopping requires a mass ratio far worse than 0.1c one-way.
- Including deceleration roughly squares the one-way mass ratio, which is why interstellar round-trips are so punishing.
- No known energy source other than matter-antimatter annihilation comes close to the density needed. Astrophage is the novel's way of granting this.
