# Artificial Gravity and Induced Torpor

> **One-line summary** — The *Hail Mary* keeps its crew alive during a multi-year interstellar voyage using two key technologies: artificial gravity and induced torpor (medical coma).

## 🎯 Intuition
**The Core Idea:** The *Hail Mary* keeps its crew alive during a multi-year interstellar voyage using two key technologies: artificial gravity and induced torpor (medical coma).
**Why It Matters:** Both are grounded in real research but extrapolated well beyond current capability. Artificial gravity determines whether the crew can function during thrust and coast, while induced torpor is what makes a multi-year voyage remotely practical in the novel.

## ⚙️ Core Mechanics
### Artificial Gravity
The ship generates gravity-like conditions through two mechanisms:

#### Thrust Gravity
During acceleration and deceleration, the ship's thrust provides a constant pseudo-gravitational force (equivalence principle). This is **completely real physics** — Einstein's insight that acceleration and gravity are locally indistinguishable.

#### Spin Gravity (During Coast)
During the unpowered cruise phase, the ship would need rotation to maintain gravity. The physics is straightforward (centripetal acceleration = ω²r), but practical constraints are significant:

- **Radius**: Small radius → high rotation rate → Coriolis effects that cause nausea and disorientation.
- **Comfort threshold**: Studies suggest a minimum radius of ~50–100 m and rotation under ~2 rpm for long-term habitability.
- **The *Hail Mary***: The novel doesn't deeply specify the coast-phase gravity arrangement, which is a minor gap.

### Induced Torpor
The crew spends most of the journey in medically induced comas—a technology that extrapolates from real **therapeutic hypothermia** research.

#### What's Real
- **Therapeutic hypothermia**: Cooling patients to ~32–34°C for 24–72 hours is standard medical practice for brain injury and cardiac arrest.
- **NASA STASH study**: NASA funded research into "Torpor Inducing Transfer Habitat" concepts for Mars missions, exploring hypothermia for weeks-to-months duration.
- **Hibernation biology**: Bears, ground squirrels, and other mammals naturally suppress metabolism for months. Active research aims to induce similar states in non-hibernating species.

#### What's Extrapolated
- **Multi-year duration**: Current medical practice supports days of induced hypothermia, not years. Muscle atrophy, bone loss, immune suppression, and neurological degradation would be severe challenges.
- **Reliable recovery**: The novel assumes clean wake-up after years of coma. In reality, even weeks of immobility cause significant deconditioning.
- **Metabolic suppression**: True hibernation-level metabolic reduction (~5% of normal) has never been achieved in humans.

> [!note] Plausibility Rating
> Artificial gravity via thrust: **fully grounded**. Spin gravity: **grounded with engineering caveats**. Multi-year torpor: **extrapolated far beyond current medicine** — optimistic but not physically impossible.

## 🔬 Deep Dive
### Scientific Accuracy
Artificial gravity via thrust is completely real physics, and spin gravity is grounded with engineering caveats. Multi-year torpor is extrapolated far beyond current medicine, but the note treats it as optimistic rather than physically impossible.

### Narrative Analysis
Artificial gravity and torpor are what let the novel stage an interstellar mission with a tiny crew and a limited habitable volume. They also make Grace's awakening credible: the mission only works dramatically because most of the voyage happens with the crew asleep and the ship still physically survivable.
