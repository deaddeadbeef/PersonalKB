# Antarctic nuclear detonations release subglacial methane as extreme planetary triage

## Context

In Chapter 13's flashback, Dr. François Leclerc presents climate-cascade projections to Stratt's science team: Astrophage-induced solar dimming will trigger ice-albedo feedback, frozen agriculture, and biome collapse beyond any conventional mitigation. Leclerc argues that the only available counter-forcing intervention is releasing methane trapped in Antarctic ice sheets — triggered by controlled nuclear detonations. Stratt authorises the operation. The team watches the controlled ice-shelf collapse from an aircraft carrier. Stratt acknowledges she will be prosecuted: "If I have to be the world's whipping boy to secure our salvation, then that's my sacrifice to make."

## Why It Matters

This is one of the novel's most ethically extreme decisions and the clearest illustration of Stratt's utilitarian calculus under existential pressure. Nuclear weapons used on Earth's own ice sheets, with deliberate greenhouse-gas release, is the kind of decision that would be unthinkable under normal governance. Stratt makes it unilaterally. The scene grounds the novel's ethics theme in a concrete, scientifically motivated scenario — Leclerc's projections are based on real climate-feedback mechanisms — rather than abstract philosophy.
