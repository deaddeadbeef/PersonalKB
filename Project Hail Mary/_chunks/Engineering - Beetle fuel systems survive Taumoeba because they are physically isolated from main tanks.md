# Beetle fuel systems survive Taumoeba because they are physically isolated from main tanks

## Context

In Chapter 21, Taumoeba consumes all accessible Astrophage aboard the Hail Mary, causing a complete power-loss cascade. Grace discovers that the Beetle probes' fuel systems are unaffected: each Beetle has a self-contained, physically isolated fuel supply with no shared plumbing to the ship's main tanks. The Beetles become Grace's only remaining source of Astrophage power. Rocky later modifies the Beetles to serve as the primary propulsion system, and their isolation from the contaminated main tanks is what makes the rest of the mission possible.

## Why It Matters

This is a textbook example of how engineering redundancy — originally designed for a different purpose (autonomous probe return) — saves the mission when an unforeseen threat appears. The physical isolation is not a clever hack but a natural consequence of the Beetles' design as independent vehicles. The scene also sets up the critical plot point that Beetle fuel remains clean even after the xenonite-permeability discovery in Chapter 27: the Beetles' Eridian-steel mini-farms are equally isolated from the contamination pathway. See [[The Hail Mary Drive]] for the propulsion context and [[Taumoeba and the Biological Solution]] for the contamination arc.
