# Relativistic mass ratios explode when deceleration is included

## Context

The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation relates final velocity to exhaust velocity and the mass ratio (initial mass / final mass). For relativistic speeds, the relationship becomes exponential. Critically, a ship that must decelerate at its destination needs to carry fuel for both acceleration and deceleration. Since the deceleration fuel must also be accelerated, the mass ratio approximately squares: if one-way requires a mass ratio of R, a stop-at-destination mission requires roughly R². A round trip requires roughly R⁴.

## Why It Matters

This is the "known soft spot" in the novel's physics. The *Hail Mary* must accelerate to a significant fraction of c, decelerate at Tau Ceti, and (in principle) return. Independent analyses have noted that the stated ship mass and fuel load may not fully close the loop for this profile. Weir acknowledged the tension. The mass-ratio problem is not a minor bookkeeping issue — it is the fundamental constraint that makes interstellar travel so difficult even with perfect fuel.
