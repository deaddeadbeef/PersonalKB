# Tape-measure melt is the first direct evidence of Rocky's extreme-temperature environment

## Context

In Chapter 10, Rocky installs a small square airlock in the tunnel wall, allowing objects to be transferred between the two environments. Grace sends a metal tape measure through as the first exchange. It returns with the rubber grip pads melted and the graduated ink markings unreadable: Rocky's side is above the melting point of rubber (roughly 120–180°C depending on compound) and hot enough to destroy standard printing ink. Grace also observes that Rocky cannot see the visible-light graduated markings — a separate discovery (Rocky's blindness) that compounds the communication problem. The tape measure result gives Grace his first hard upper-bound data point on Rocky's environmental temperature, moving the characterisation of Eridian biology from inference to direct measurement.

## Why It Matters

This is the moment speculation becomes physical evidence. Grace knew from Rocky's biochemistry and the Astrophage context that Eridians probably live at high temperatures, but the melted tape measure is the first object that comes back with material proof. It also reveals a practical communication obstacle: standard human measurement tools and visual markings are useless in Rocky's environment, forcing Grace to redesign every physical exchange that follows. The incident is a miniature lesson in empirical method — an object sent into an unknown environment and the state in which it returns is itself a measurement.
