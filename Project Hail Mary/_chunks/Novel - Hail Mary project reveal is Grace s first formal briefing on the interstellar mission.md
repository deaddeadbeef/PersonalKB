# Hail Mary project reveal is Grace's first formal briefing on the interstellar mission

## Context

In Chapter 4's flashback, Stratt brings Grace into a multinational briefing where the Hail Mary mission is laid out: Tau Ceti is the only nearby star not showing Astrophage dimming, making it the obvious target for investigation. Dimitri Komorov, a Russian physicist, demonstrates that controlled Astrophage combustion produces 60,000 N of thrust — vastly exceeding chemical rockets and proving interstellar transit is feasible. Grace is granted clearance instantly, over the objections of at least one national representative, illustrating Stratt's absolute authority over normal procedural constraints.

## Why It Matters

This is the moment where the novel's flashback strand shifts from "mysterious scientific anomaly" to "active mission planning." The briefing establishes the interstellar scope of the story, the international scale of the response, and the engineering foundation (Astrophage propulsion) that makes everything possible. Dimitri's thrust demonstration is also the first empirical validation that the drive concept works — prior to this, Astrophage propulsion is theoretical. See [[The Hail Mary Drive]] for the drive physics and [[Arc - Astrophage Crisis Escalation]] for the escalation trajectory.
