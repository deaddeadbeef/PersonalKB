# Redell's blackpanel design is scalable Sahara Astrophage production infrastructure

## Context

In a Chapter 12 flashback, Stratt and Grace visit Robert Redell in Auckland's Pare Prison, where he has been quietly developing a concept for industrial Astrophage production. Redell's blackpanel is elegantly simple: black anodised metal foil (high solar absorptivity, low emissivity) placed under a glass sheet with a sealed air gap. In direct equatorial sunlight, the trapped heat raises the foil surface above 96.415°C — Astrophage's critical temperature threshold — maintaining the conditions in which Astrophage can survive, feed, and reproduce. The design uses only foil, glass, and ceramic spacers: all materials that can be manufactured at industrial scale from common inputs. Installed across North Africa in numbers reaching into the trillions, the panels become a continental-scale bioreactor. Stratt immediately recognises the value and agrees to reduce Redell's sentence in exchange for him running the programme.

## Why It Matters

The blackpanel concept is the production backbone of the entire Hail Mary mission. Without a scalable method to cultivate Astrophage in the quantities required for interstellar propulsion, the mission cannot be fuelled. Redell's design is significant precisely because it is not complex: it exploits Astrophage's own precise temperature requirements as a passive thermostat, requiring no active temperature control or monitoring beyond placing the panels correctly. The concept also illustrates a recurring theme in the novel — that the most effective solutions to the Astrophage crisis are those that work with the organism's biology rather than against it.
