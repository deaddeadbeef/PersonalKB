# Astrophage reproduction and lifecycle involve binary fission and stellar residence

## Context

The novel depicts Ryland Grace's laboratory work revealing that Astrophage behaves like a single-celled organism using stellar energy to fuel cellular reproduction. The in-universe biology posits a life cycle anchored to the host star: cells absorb radiation at the photosphere, reach an energy threshold, divide, and the resulting daughter cells either remain in the star's outer layers or migrate outward. This is a fictional construct with no direct real-world parallel — no known organism reproduces at stellar-photosphere temperatures or scales.

## Why It Matters

The lifecycle framing is load-bearing for the novel's plot. Astrophage's ability to accumulate in enormous numbers explains the measurable stellar dimming detected as the Petrova Line. Binary fission as the reproduction model aligns the organism with familiar microbiology, making it legible to Grace (a biologist) and to the reader, even though the thermodynamic impossibility is vast. The lifecycle concept also sets up the biological solution: if Astrophage reproduces, it can be preyed upon. See [[Astrophage Life Cycle and Migration]] and [[Taumoeba and the Biological Solution]].
