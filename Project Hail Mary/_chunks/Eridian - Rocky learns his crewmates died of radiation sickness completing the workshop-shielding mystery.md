# Rocky learns his crewmates died of radiation sickness completing the workshop-shielding mystery

## Context

In Chapter 13, Grace concludes the radiation-teaching arc begun in Chapter 12. When Rocky finally grasps what ionising radiation does to biological tissue, his response is immediate and devastating: "Thank. Now I know how my friends died." Rocky's twenty-two crewmates died during the interstellar transit because Eridian science — shaped by a planet with 25× Earth's magnetic field and 29× the atmospheric shielding — had no concept of radiation as a biological threat. Rocky survived only because he spent most of his time in his workshop, which happened to be the most heavily shielded compartment on the Blip-A.

## Why It Matters

This is the emotional complement to the crew-disclosure scene in Chapter 11 (where Rocky first revealed his dead crew). There, the fact of death was shared; here, the cause is understood. The moment works on multiple levels: it demonstrates how Eridian evolutionary history created a lethal knowledge gap, it deepens the Grace-Rocky bond through shared grief, and it completes the radiation-mystery subplot. Rocky's tone — "low, sad" — is one of the novel's most emotionally effective uses of the musical-language system.
