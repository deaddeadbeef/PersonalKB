# Alien signals can be treated as cryptanalysis problems

## Context

Callimahos (NSA, 1966) argued that decoding an extraterrestrial signal is structurally analogous to breaking a cipher with no crib. The "plaintext" is the sender's intended meaning, the "ciphertext" is the received signal, and the "key" is the unknown encoding scheme. Crucially, even before the content is decoded, the signal's statistical properties — entropy, repetition patterns, mathematical regularities — reveal information about the sender's communication system. This is the same principle cryptanalysts use when attacking unknown ciphers.

## Why It Matters

Grace's approach to understanding Rocky mirrors this framework. He begins by analyzing patterns — tapping sequences, counting, looking for mathematical structure — before attempting to decode semantic content. The novel doesn't cite Callimahos, but the methodology is remarkably aligned. This chunk validates the novel's first-contact communication approach as being rooted in serious (if classified) thinking about the problem.
