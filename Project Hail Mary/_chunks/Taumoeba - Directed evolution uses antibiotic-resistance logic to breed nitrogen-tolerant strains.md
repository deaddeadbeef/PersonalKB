# Directed evolution uses antibiotic-resistance logic to breed nitrogen-tolerant strains

## Context

After discovering nitrogen's lethality, Grace reframes the problem using an antibiotic-resistance analogy: just as bacteria evolve to survive the chemicals that kill them, Taumoeba can be bred for nitrogen tolerance through iterative selective pressure. Rocky immediately grasps the concept and fabricates ten smaller breeding tanks from xenonite. This initiates the months-long breeding programme whose success is confirmed in Chapter 24.

## Why It Matters

The directed-evolution strategy is the novel's most pedagogically effective science beat. The antibiotic-resistance analogy makes a sophisticated evolutionary biology concept immediately accessible. The approach is scientifically sound — iterative selective pressure is how real strain-development works. The xenonite tanks chosen here also set up the unintended consequence discovered in Chapter 27 (xenonite permeability). See [[Taumoeba and the Biological Solution]].
