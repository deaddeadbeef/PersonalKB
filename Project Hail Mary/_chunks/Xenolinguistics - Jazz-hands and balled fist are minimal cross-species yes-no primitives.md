# Jazz-hands and balled fist are minimal cross-species yes-no primitives

## Context

In Chapter 11's concentrated vocabulary-building session, Grace and Rocky establish a binary confirmation system: Grace uses jazz-hands (all five fingers spread flat against the tunnel wall, making maximum contact area) for "yes" and a balled fist for "no." Rocky can feel both through the xenonite wall with his claws and adopts corresponding equivalents on his side. The yes/no pair is the smallest possible semantic vocabulary — two symbols with opposite meanings — but it is foundational. Every subsequent word introduced in the session can be confirmed or rejected by either party, creating a closed feedback loop that prevents ambiguous terms from accumulating in the shared lexicon.

## Why It Matters

Yes/no confirmation primitives are a well-documented requirement in monolingual fieldwork — linguists establishing vocabulary with a speaker of an undocumented language must first secure a reliable yes/no channel before they can bootstrap nouns and verbs. Grace intuitively rediscovers this. The jazz-hands/fist pair is also notable for being physical rather than acoustic: it uses the same tactile channel Rocky uses for spatial navigation, meaning the primitive fits Rocky's sensory architecture rather than being imported from Grace's visual-gesture system. This is a small but telling example of the communication methodology adapting to the asymmetry between the two parties. See [[Xenolinguistics and First Contact]] for the broader communication arc.
