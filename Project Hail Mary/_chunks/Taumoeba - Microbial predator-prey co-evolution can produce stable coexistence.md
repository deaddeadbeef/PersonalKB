# Microbial predator-prey co-evolution can produce stable long-term coexistence

## Context

Classical Lotka-Volterra population dynamics — where predator and prey populations cycle in counterphase — apply at microbial scales, with some modifications. Studies of phage-bacteria, *Bdellovibrio*-bacteria, and protozoan-bacteria systems show that predators rarely drive prey to full extinction; instead, prey resistance mutations arise, predator counter-resistance evolves in response, and a fluctuating arms race emerges. Long-term coexistence of predator and prey populations is a common ecological outcome, especially in spatially structured or nutrient-limited environments. This dynamic is well-established in microbial ecology.

## Why It Matters

This provides the ecological plausibility for the Tau Ceti system having both Astrophage and Taumoeba co-exist without one exterminating the other. The novel's depiction of Taumoeba controlling — but not eliminating — Astrophage in the Tau Ceti system is consistent with known microbial ecology. The same logic implies that deploying Taumoeba against the Sun's Astrophage population might achieve population suppression without complete sterility, which fits the novel's resolution. The principle is real; the specific application to stellar-dwelling organisms is fiction. See [[Taumoeba and the Biological Solution]].
