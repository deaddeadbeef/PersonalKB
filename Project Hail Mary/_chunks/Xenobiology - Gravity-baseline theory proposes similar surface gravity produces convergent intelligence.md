# Gravity-baseline theory proposes similar surface gravity produces convergent intelligence

## Context

In Chapter 20, Grace and Rocky discuss why their species are comparably intelligent despite radically different biochemistry (water vs. ammonia), sensory systems (vision vs. echolocation), and environments (Earth vs. Erid). Grace proposes that similar surface gravity is the key variable: both planets have roughly Earth-level gravity, which constrains body size, locomotion, and the biomechanical challenges that intelligence evolves to solve. Similar gravitational constraints produce similar scales of tool use, similar body-plan ranges, and — by extension — similar levels of neural complexity.

## Why It Matters

The gravity-baseline theory is one of the novel's most speculative scientific ideas, but it serves an important narrative function: it explains why Grace and Rocky can work together as equals rather than one species being vastly more advanced. The theory is grounded in real evolutionary biology (gravity constrains body plans) even if the intelligence extrapolation is fictional. It also connects to the Goldilocks-civilisation argument from Chapter 13 — both species are in the right technological and biological window for interstellar collaboration.
