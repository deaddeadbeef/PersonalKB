# Grace deduces from the Beetle probes that the Hail Mary has no return trajectory

## Context

While exploring the ship's storage area in Chapter 3, Grace finds four small automated probes — the Beetles — each equipped with 5 terabytes of data storage. His reasoning is straightforward: if the mission designers built dedicated return vehicles for the scientific findings, they did not build a return trajectory for the ship itself. The Hail Mary is a one-way vessel. Grace reaches this conclusion alone, still recovering from amnesia, and the realisation transforms his understanding of his situation from "stranded" to "sacrificed."

## Why It Matters

The one-way revelation is the novel's first personal-stakes escalation. Until this point, Grace's problem is survival and orientation; after it, his problem is mortality and meaning. The deduction is also a narrative-structure pivot: it motivates Grace's Chapter 5 acceptance of death and his commitment to finding a solution worth transmitting. The Beetle probes themselves become critical later — they are the mechanism by which Taumoeba-82.5 reaches Earth. See [[The Hail Mary Drive]] for the propulsion constraints that make a return trajectory impossible, and [[Arc - Astrophage Crisis Escalation]] for the mission-design context.
