# Torpor may reduce radiation damage but evidence is early

## Context

One speculated benefit of torpor for spaceflight is radiation protection. The reasoning: lower metabolic rate means fewer reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated during normal cellular processes. Since ROS amplify radiation damage, suppressing metabolism could reduce the biological impact of a given radiation dose. Some animal studies show reduced DNA damage markers during hibernation, but the data is limited and has not been tested under spaceflight radiation conditions.

## Why It Matters

The *Hail Mary* largely sidesteps its radiation problem — the crew is in torpor for most of the journey, and the novel doesn't deeply address shielding against relativistic interstellar medium. If torpor genuinely reduces radiation vulnerability, it would be a convenient double benefit: preserving the crew from aging *and* from radiation. But the evidence is too early to call this more than an interesting hypothesis. Confidence: uncertain.
