# Grace deduces Rocky's blindness to visible light through systematic object-exchange experiments

## Context

In Chapter 10, Grace and Rocky have progressed beyond initial communication to active information exchange. Grace systematically presents objects through their shared mini-airlock — varying color, texture, shape, and material — and observes Rocky's responses. Rocky identifies objects flawlessly by shape, size, and material composition, but shows zero response to any visual property: color differences, light sources, and visual displays produce no reaction. Grace eliminates alternative hypotheses (different visible spectrum, low-resolution vision) before concluding that Rocky perceives the world entirely through non-visual means. The later identification of echolocation confirms the hypothesis.

## Why It Matters

This scene is one of the novel's purest demonstrations of the scientific method: observation, hypothesis, controlled experiment, elimination of alternatives, and conclusion. It also fundamentally redefines the reader's understanding of Rocky — everything about Eridian technology, communication, and engineering must be reinterpreted through the lens of acoustic rather than visual perception. The discovery drives multiple subsequent plot points: the vibration-based language, the tactile popsicle-stick symbols, and Rocky's inability to read visual displays.
