# Improvised Petrovascope search repurposes attitude thrusters as rotating Petrova-band illumination

## Context

In Chapter 28, after deciding to rescue Rocky, Grace faces the problem of finding a dead ship in interstellar space. The Blip-A has no drive signature, no radio, and no active emissions — it is a dark object against a dark background. Grace's solution: use the attitude-control thrusters (which burn small amounts of Astrophage) as a light source in the Petrova frequency, then sweep the Petrovascope through three-dimensional arcs looking for reflected signal from the Astrophage coating Rocky's ship. The technique is an improvised active radar using Astrophage physics as both the illumination source and the detection medium.

## Why It Matters

The Petrovascope search technique is the novel's final major engineering improvisation. It closes the loop on the Petrova-frequency physics that opened the story (Petrova's email in Chapter 1) by repurposing the same phenomenon for active search rather than passive observation. The technique is also a character beat: Grace's scientific creativity — first demonstrated with the pendulum in Chapter 2 — here operates at its highest stakes, with Rocky's life and Erid's survival depending on whether the improvisation works. It does, in Chapter 29. See [[Ryland Grace]] for Grace's engineering pattern and [[Arc - Grace and Rocky]] for the rescue arc.
