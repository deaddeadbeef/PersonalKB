# CO2 emission spectroscopy explains how Astrophage navigates to Venus for reproduction

## Context

In Chapter 4, Grace works through the navigation question systematically: why does Astrophage go to Venus specifically? He tests his three samples (nicknamed Larry, Curly, and Moe) against various wavelengths and narrows on the infrared spectral emission bands of CO2 as the key attractant. Venus's atmosphere is 96.5% carbon dioxide, producing an overwhelming spectral signature that Astrophage can detect and follow. The complete mechanistic picture emerges: Astrophage absorbs energy at the Sun, detects Venus via CO2 spectroscopy, reproduces in the Venusian upper atmosphere, and both parent and daughter cell return sunward. Chapter 2 first surfaces the CO2 connection; Chapter 4 synthesizes the full mechanism with experimental confirmation.

## Why It Matters

This discovery is the novel's first complete scientific explanation — a full cycle from observation through hypothesis to experimental confirmation. It explains the Petrova Line's shape (the arc follows the Sun-Venus path), grounds Astrophage biology in a plausible sensory mechanism, and demonstrates Weir's characteristic hard-SF pedagogy: the reader works through the logic alongside Grace.
