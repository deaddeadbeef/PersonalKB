# Global average sunlight is one quarter of the solar constant

## Context

The solar constant (~1361 W/m²) measures radiant flux hitting a surface perpendicular to the Sun at Earth's distance. But Earth is a sphere, not a flat disk. The cross-sectional area intercepting sunlight (πr²) is one quarter of the total surface area (4πr²), so the global average over all latitudes and day/night is ~340 W/m². After albedo reflection (~30%), the absorbed energy is ~240 W/m².

## Why It Matters

This factor-of-four relationship is critical for understanding the Astrophage crisis. When Astrophage reduces the solar constant by a few percent, the impact on global-average absorbed energy is still measured in watts per square meter — but Earth's climate is so finely balanced that even 5 W/m² of reduction (about 2% of the solar constant) would overwhelm the current greenhouse imbalance in the opposite direction. The novel's premise that a modest percentage of solar dimming could end civilization is quantitatively sound.
