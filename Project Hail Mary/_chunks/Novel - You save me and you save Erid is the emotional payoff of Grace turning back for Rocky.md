# "You save me and you save Erid" is the emotional payoff of Grace turning back for Rocky

## Context

Chapter 29 opens three months into the search. Grace has been methodically sweeping three-dimensional arcs with his improvised Petrovascope radar. A faint return signal appears. He closes the range, matches velocity, and confirms it is the Blip-A — dead in space, no drive signature. Grace EVAs across on a tether and knocks on the hull with a wrench until Rocky hears him. Rocky's physical condition tells the story: burns, injured arms, weeks of failed attempts to stop the Taumoeba infestation. His emotional response is immediate: "You save me and you save Erid!" Grace explains he knew about xenonite permeability — information Rocky had no way to discover — and came specifically because of it.

## Why It Matters

This is the novel's emotional peak. Everything converges here: Grace's rescue decision (Chapter 28), the Petrovascope search technique (Chapter 28), the xenonite-permeability knowledge (Chapter 27), and the entire Grace-Rocky partnership built across twenty chapters of shared problem-solving. Rocky's statement carries double weight: Grace saves Rocky personally and, by delivering the xenonite warning, saves Erid as a civilisation. The reunion inverts the novel's original framing — Grace was forced onto the mission against his will, and here he has voluntarily chosen to stay in space forever for friendship.
