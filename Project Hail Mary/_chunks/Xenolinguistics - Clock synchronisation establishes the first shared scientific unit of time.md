# Clock synchronisation establishes the first shared scientific unit of time

## Context

In Chapter 9, after the vibration-language breakthrough, Rocky holds up a clock with raised symbols on three concentric rotating rings — a physical clock that Grace can observe. Grace had already taped his own clock to his side of the tunnel wall. Both time one full rotation of the Eridian clock and arrive at the same result: one Eridian second equals approximately 2.366 Earth seconds. The exchange works because both parties can independently measure the same physical duration and compare their readings numerically — a calibration that requires only counting ability and a timing device, not any shared language.

## Why It Matters

The clock exchange is the model for how all subsequent quantitative science between Grace and Rocky proceeds. Numbers and physical measurements are the bridge: both parties can observe the same physical event, time it independently, and verify the ratio of their measurement units. This is more powerful than pure mathematics (which is abstract) because it grounds the shared system in a physically reproducible standard. Rocky's clock is also revealing: three concentric rings encode a base-6 positional number system, reflecting the six-limbed anatomy of Eridians. The calibration is the first piece of mutually verified, quantitatively precise knowledge between the two species.
