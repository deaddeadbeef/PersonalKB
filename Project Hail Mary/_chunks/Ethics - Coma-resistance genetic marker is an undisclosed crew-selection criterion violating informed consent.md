# Coma-resistance genetic marker is an undisclosed crew-selection criterion violating informed consent

## Context

Chapter 14's flashback introduces the coma-resistance screening programme: Dr. Lamai administers tests while Stratt withholds the full context. In Chapter 15, DuBois directly tells Grace that his rare genetic marker — not his scientific expertise alone — is why Stratt selected him. Grace was never informed of this criterion. The one-in-seven-thousand rarity means the selection pool is vanishingly small.

## Why It Matters

This chunk captures one of the novel's sharpest ethical tensions: Stratt's utilitarian calculus overrides individual autonomy under existential necessity. Grace's consent was never sought for the criterion that most determined his fate. The revelation recontextualises his entire presence on the mission. See [[Eva Stratt and the Ethics of Existential Response]] for the broader coercion analysis and [[Ryland Grace]] for Grace's character arc.
