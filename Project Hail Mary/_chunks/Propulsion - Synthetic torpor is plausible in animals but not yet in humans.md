# Synthetic torpor is plausible in animals but not yet in humans

## Context

Recent research (2020–2024) has demonstrated that targeted stimulation of specific hypothalamic neurons (notably in the preoptic area) can induce torpor-like states in mice and rats — species that do not naturally hibernate. Core body temperature drops to ~25°C, metabolic rate falls to ~30% of baseline, and the animals recover without apparent harm. However, the durations are hours to days, and the translational gap to humans is large: different brain architecture, different thermoregulatory physiology, unknown long-term effects.

## Why It Matters

The *Hail Mary* crew spends years in medically induced comas. Current science has demonstrated the principle — metabolic suppression can be artificially triggered — but the gap between rodent experiments lasting hours and human torpor lasting years is enormous. The novel's torpor technology is "extrapolated" rather than "grounded": the direction of research is real, but the destination is speculative. This is one of the more honest extrapolations in the book.
