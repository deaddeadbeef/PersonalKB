# Melanized fungi accelerate under ionizing radiation

## Context

The ISS experiment by Shunk et al. (2022) placed *Cladosporium sphaerospermum* samples in the ISS environment alongside matched ground controls. Over a 30-day growth period, the space-grown samples showed ~21% greater biomass accumulation. The elevated growth tracks with the hypothesis that melanin pigments transduce ionizing radiation into metabolic energy, first proposed after observations of melanized fungi thriving in the Chernobyl reactor.

## Why It Matters

This is the single strongest empirical anchor for Astrophage's radiation-eating biology. Weir's premise — that an organism could use radiation as a primary energy source — is not pure invention. Real fungi demonstrably grow better when exposed to ionizing radiation, and the mechanism (melanin-mediated energy transduction) has a plausible biophysical pathway. The scale difference between fungal radiotrophism and Astrophage's stellar feeding is immense, but the qualitative principle is grounded.
