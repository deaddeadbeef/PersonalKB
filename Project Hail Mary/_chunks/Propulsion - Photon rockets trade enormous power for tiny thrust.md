# Photon rockets trade enormous power for tiny thrust

## Context

A photon rocket produces thrust by emitting directed electromagnetic radiation. Since photons travel at c, the exhaust velocity is the maximum physically possible. But thrust equals power divided by c: T = P/c. For 1 N of thrust, you need P = c × 1 N ≈ 3 × 10⁸ W — roughly 300 MW. For comparison, a conventional rocket engine producing 1 N might need only a few watts of chemical energy release.

## Why It Matters

The Hail Mary drive is a photon rocket. This chunk explains why such a drive is simultaneously the best and worst propulsion concept: best because nothing can beat lightspeed exhaust, worst because the power-to-thrust ratio is absurdly unfavorable. The novel resolves this by giving Astrophage an energy density approaching mass-energy equivalence. Without that fictional element, photon rockets are a theoretical curiosity with no practical path to interstellar travel.
