# Stratt's pre-emptive pardons demonstrate extraordinary cross-jurisdictional legal authority

## Context

In a Chapter 10 flashback, Stratt appears at a court hearing captioned "Intellectual Property Alliance v. Project Hail Mary" — a consolidated IP lawsuit presumably targeting the Project's use of proprietary technologies without licence. Stratt produces pardons pre-emptively signed by multiple heads of state, covering multiple jurisdictions. The case is immediately dismissed. The scene is presented almost as a comic beat: the legal machinery of international commerce grinds forward, and Stratt dissolves it in moments with a stack of papers she already had ready.

## Why It Matters

The pardon scene is the clearest single illustration in the novel of how Stratt's authority operates. She does not negotiate, delay, or appeal: she pre-empts. The pardons were already in hand before the lawsuit was filed, implying that Stratt (or her legal team) anticipated the action and secured multi-sovereign cover in advance. This demonstrates not just the breadth of her authority (spanning multiple jurisdictions simultaneously) but its proactive nature — she shapes the legal landscape ahead of events rather than reacting to them. This is the concrete instance behind the abstract claim that she holds "unchecked emergency powers." See [[Eva Stratt and the Ethics of Existential Response]] for the broader ethics analysis of what her authority means for accountability and proportionality.
