# Predatory bacteria demonstrate host-specific population suppression

## Context

*Bdellovibrio bacteriovorus* is a gram-negative proteobacterium that preys on other gram-negative bacteria by attaching to the prey cell wall, penetrating the periplasm, and consuming cell contents before releasing daughter cells. Studies have shown that *Bdellovibrio* can reduce host populations by 99% or more under controlled conditions, yet has a defined prey range — it attacks particular gram-negative species effectively but shows poor predation of others. Related predatory bacteria (*Vampirovibrio*, *Micavibrio*) display similar narrow host ranges. This is a well-documented phenomenon in microbiology and ecology.

## Why It Matters

This is the primary real-world anchor for Taumoeba's fictional role in *Project Hail Mary*. The novel's solution depends on the plausibility of an organism that *specifically* targets Astrophage without causing ecological collateral damage. *Bdellovibrio*'s demonstrated host-specificity makes that premise scientifically grounded at the conceptual level, even though Taumoeba operates in conditions (stellar-temperature environments, extreme energy densities) no real predatory bacterium has ever faced.
