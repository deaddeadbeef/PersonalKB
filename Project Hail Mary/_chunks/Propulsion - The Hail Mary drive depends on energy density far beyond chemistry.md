# The Hail Mary drive depends on energy density far beyond chemistry

## Context

Chris West's analysis works backward from the novel's stated performance numbers (mass, acceleration, cruise velocity, mission duration) to calculate the energy density the fuel must provide. The result: Astrophage must store energy at densities comparable to matter-antimatter annihilation (~9 × 10¹⁶ J/kg). Chemical fuels top out around 10⁷ J/kg; nuclear fission around 10¹⁴ J/kg. Only complete mass-to-energy conversion approaches the needed density.

## Why It Matters

This chunk is the quantitative backbone for understanding why Astrophage is the novel's one essential impossibility. Everything else — the photon-rocket drive, the interstellar trajectory, the mission timeline — follows logically *if* you accept the fuel. Weir built the story by turning one dial to its maximum setting and then rigorously working out the consequences. West's analysis confirms both that the dial is set to "impossible" and that the consequences are internally consistent.
