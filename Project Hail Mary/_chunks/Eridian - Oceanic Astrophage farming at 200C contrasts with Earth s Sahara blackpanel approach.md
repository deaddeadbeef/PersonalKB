# Oceanic Astrophage farming at 200°C contrasts with Earth's Sahara blackpanel approach

## Context

In Chapter 17, Rocky describes how Eridians harvest Astrophage: their oceans are approximately 200°C (Erid's surface temperature is far higher than Earth's), and Astrophage accumulates naturally in this warm water. Eridians collect it directly from oceanic sources. By contrast, Earth's approach is Redell's blackpanel design — trillions of manufactured black-foil panels across the Sahara that absorb and concentrate Astrophage from sunlight. Both species need Astrophage fuel for their interstellar missions, but their harvesting methods reflect their home planets' radically different environments.

## Why It Matters

The contrast between oceanic farming and desert blackpanels is one of the novel's best examples of convergent engineering under divergent conditions: same goal, different starting conditions, different solutions. It deepens the portrayal of Eridian civilization as genuinely alien — not just humans in different bodies — and grounds the science in environmental logic rather than arbitrary invention. The oceanic temperature also reinforces Rocky's extreme-temperature biology (his ship is at 200°C+).
