# Astrophage interstellar migration is driven by IR-photon emission and charge gradients

## Context

The novel's in-universe explanation for how Astrophage spreads between star systems involves directional photon emission: cells store energy at near-E=mc² density (see [[Astrophage Energy Physics]]) and then release it as targeted IR radiation, producing a reaction force. Magnetic or charge-based orientation is implied as the steering mechanism. The result is that individual cells — or more plausibly, vast swarms — traverse interstellar distances over geological timescales. This is the mechanism that seeds Tau Ceti and threatens 40 Eridani, and is the same principle the Hail Mary drive exploits in reverse.

## Why It Matters

Interstellar migration is what makes Astrophage an *existential* threat rather than merely a local solar parasite. It also elevates the story from a single-system crisis to a multi-species one: Rocky's civilization faces the same threat from the same organism. The migration mechanism directly connects to [[The Hail Mary Drive]] (which uses Astrophage as fuel) and to the detection method at [[Stellar Dimming and the Petrova Line]]. Physically, the thrust from IR photon emission is real in principle but insufficient to propel cells to significant fractions of *c* on biological timescales — this is one of the novel's load-bearing fictional extrapolations.
