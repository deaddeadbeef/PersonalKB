# Eridian radiation blindness is a species-specific knowledge gap from extreme planetary shielding

## Context

In Chapter 12's present-timeline strand, Grace begins teaching Rocky radiation science — cosmic rays, high-speed hydrogen atoms, ionising particles. Rocky has no cultural framework for any of it. The explanation: Erid's magnetic field is twenty-five times stronger than Earth's, and its atmosphere is twenty-nine times as thick. Together these provide essentially perfect shielding from all ionising radiation at the planetary surface. Eridians evolved in an environment where radiation simply did not exist as an observable phenomenon. They never developed the concept, never built detectors, and never included it in their physics. The knowledge gap is not a failure of intelligence but a consequence of evolutionary and environmental history.

## Why It Matters

This is one of the novel's most elegant illustrations of how environment shapes knowledge. Eridians are clearly highly intelligent — Rocky independently developed interstellar propulsion and materials science — but their science has a systematic blind spot determined entirely by what their planet made observable. Earth's scientific tradition developed radiation physics partly because radiation is directly hazardous here (at altitude, during solar events, and in geological contexts like radon). Erid's shielding removed all of that evolutionary and empirical pressure. The irony is that Rocky's crewmates died of radiation sickness en route — killed by the one phenomenon their civilization's entire scientific tradition had never had reason to study.
