# Neutrino production is the mechanism for Astrophage's 96.415 C temperature lock

## Context

In a Chapter 12 flashback, Dr. Lokken presents a CERN advance paper whose finding is simple and startling: Astrophage stores energy by producing neutrinos. Because neutrinos carry energy away from the cell at a rate that scales precisely with the organism's temperature, the system reaches a stable equilibrium at exactly 96.415°C — a thermodynamic fixed point where energy input from absorbed stellar radiation exactly balances energy loss via neutrino emission. This is why every measurement of Astrophage temperature, regardless of its environment, returns the same number. Lokken also notes a secondary implication: Astrophage's interaction cross-section with electromagnetic radiation is extremely high ("super cross-section"), meaning a hull lined with live Astrophage will absorb essentially all incoming cosmic radiation — turning the fuel into a structural radiation shield.

## Why It Matters

The neutrino mechanism is one of the novel's most creative pieces of fictional physics. It provides a precise, mechanistically grounded explanation for an anomaly that had been treated as unexplained data — why does Astrophage always sit at 96.415°C? The answer ties together energy storage, temperature regulation, and a real particle physics concept (neutrino emission) in a way that is internally consistent. It also produces a useful engineering consequence (radiation shielding) as a free corollary, which the novel immediately exploits. For real physics context, neutrinos do carry energy away from nuclear reactions (this is real, e.g., in stellar nucleosynthesis), but no biological process produces neutrinos, and the temperature-lock mechanism is fictional extrapolation.
