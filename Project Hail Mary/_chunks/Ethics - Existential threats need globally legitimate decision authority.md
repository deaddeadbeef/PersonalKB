# Existential threats need globally legitimate decision authority

## Context

Policy discussions on planetary defense, climate tipping points, and pandemic response consistently identify a governance gap: existential threats (those risking human extinction or civilizational collapse) demand coordinated, rapid, high-authority responses, but no existing institution has the mandate, enforcement capability, or democratic legitimacy to deliver one. The Responsibility to Protect (R2P) doctrine allows sovereignty override when a state fails to protect its population, but R2P has never been applied at species scale and lacks institutional machinery for existential scenarios.

## Why It Matters

Eva Stratt is the novel's answer to this governance gap: a single administrator with essentially unlimited authority, no democratic accountability, and no sunset clause. PHM uses her character to dramatize the tension between what an existential crisis demands (speed, unity, ruthlessness) and what legitimate governance requires (consent, accountability, proportionality). The novel is remarkably honest about this tension — Stratt is effective *because* she is unaccountable, and the story never fully resolves whether that trade-off is justified.

- Q: Is there a real-world governance framework for existential threats?
- Q: How does Stratt's authority compare to real emergency powers?
