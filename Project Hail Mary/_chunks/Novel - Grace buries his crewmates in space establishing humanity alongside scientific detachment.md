# Grace buries his crewmates in space establishing humanity alongside scientific detachment

## Context

In Chapter 3, Grace discovers that his two crewmates — Yáo and Ilyukhina — are dead, their bodies mummified in their medical beds. As fragmentary memories surface, he recalls Ilyukhina's humor ("she could have you laughing your butt off within thirty seconds") and Yáo's quiet reserve. Rather than treating the bodies as obstacles, Grace uses the airlock system to give each a deliberate space burial. The scene is the first time the novel pauses its problem-solving momentum for an emotional beat.

## Why It Matters

The burial sequence establishes a character dimension that the purely scientific scenes cannot: Grace cares about people, not just problems. This is narratively essential because his later decision to rescue Rocky — sacrificing his own return — must feel emotionally authentic, not just logically motivated. The scene also introduces Yáo and Ilyukhina as presences in the story despite their deaths, giving weight to the crew-selection ethics explored in later flashbacks. See [[The Hail Mary Crew - Yao and Ilyukhina]] for the crew's role and [[Arc - Grace and Rocky]] for how Grace's capacity for personal attachment develops through the novel.
