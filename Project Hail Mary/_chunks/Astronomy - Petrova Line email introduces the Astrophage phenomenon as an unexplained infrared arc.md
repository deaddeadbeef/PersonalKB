# Petrova Line email introduces the Astrophage phenomenon as an unexplained infrared arc

## Context

In a fragmentary flashback during Chapter 1, Grace recalls sitting in a diner reading an email from astronomer Dr. Irina Petrova addressed to an astronomy mailing list. She has noticed a thin infrared arc between the Sun and Venus and is soliciting theories from colleagues. At this point in the narrative, neither Grace nor the reader knows what the arc is. The email is presented without explanation — a scientific anomaly from an ordinary professional channel.

## Why It Matters

This email is the novel's inciting scientific observation. Everything that follows — Astrophage identification, the Hail Mary mission, the interstellar journey — traces back to Petrova noticing the arc. Weir chooses to introduce the existential crisis not as a dramatic announcement but as a puzzling email from a working scientist, grounding the novel's tone in scientific process from the first page. The arc will later be explained as the Astrophage migration path (see [[Stellar Dimming and the Petrova Line]]). The email also establishes the flashback-strand convention that structures the entire novel (see [[Arc - Astrophage Crisis Escalation]]).
