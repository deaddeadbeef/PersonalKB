# Pendulum gravity calculation demonstrates empirical reasoning under cognitive impairment

## Context

In Chapter 2's present timeline, Grace — still amnesiac and barely able to stand — rigs a tape measure as a pendulum and times its oscillations to derive the local gravitational acceleration. Using the period formula, he calculates that producing this gravity through centrifugal force would require a radius of approximately 1,280 metres, far beyond any constructed structure. He rules out the centrifuge hypothesis definitively, concluding he is not on Earth and not in the solar system. He is alone, recovering from torpor, with no memory of how he got here.

## Why It Matters

This is the novel's first "show, don't tell" demonstration of Grace as a scientist. Rather than exposition about his credentials, Weir lets Grace solve a physics problem with improvised tools while cognitively impaired. The pendulum test is real physics — any reader can verify the math — and it establishes the hard-SF contract: solutions in this novel will be derived from first principles. The scene also seeds the dual-timeline structure, as Grace's present-day reasoning intercuts with flashbacks of his lab work. See [[Ryland Grace]] for the character arc and [[Weir's Hard SF Method]] for the novel's pedagogical approach.
