# Acoustic sensing provides high-bandwidth environmental perception in dark or turbid environments

## Context

Dolphin biosonar operates effectively in conditions that defeat vision: dark deep water, turbid coastal shallows, environments with high visual scattering. During prey interception, dolphins enter a "buzz" phase in which click intervals shorten to 1–2 ms — a spatial update rate exceeding 500 Hz. The auditory brainstem shows hypertrophied processing nuclei (cochlear nucleus, inferior colliculus) dedicated to submillisecond timing discrimination: the neural hardware is explicitly specialized for extracting information from rapid pulse-echo sequences. In terms of environmental modeling throughput, cetacean biosonar in the buzz phase rivals or exceeds the spatial update rate of vertebrate vision in equivalent tasks.

## Why It Matters

Rocky's home world orbits 40 Eridani A, and primary-mode chapter notes confirm it is a high-pressure, high-temperature world with an opaque atmosphere — a classic vision-poor environment. The key question for Eridian sensory biology is whether an acoustic-primary sense could support not just navigation but engineering cognition: perceiving material structure, design geometry, and spatial relationships with sufficient resolution to build complex technology. Real cetacean biosonar shows that acoustic sensing can achieve spatial update rates and discrimination resolution comparable to vision. In dense, dark environments, acoustic perception is not a degraded sense — it is the appropriate primary sense, fully capable of supporting sophisticated spatial reasoning.
