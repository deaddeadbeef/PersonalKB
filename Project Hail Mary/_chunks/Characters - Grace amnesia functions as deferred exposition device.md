# Grace's amnesia functions as a narrative device for deferred exposition

## Context

The novel opens with Ryland Grace waking in an unfamiliar environment with no memory of his identity, the mission, or his crewmates (who are dead). Rather than an info-dump, Weir uses Grace's amnesia to drip-feed backstory through alternating timeline chapters: present-day scenes aboard the Hail Mary, where Grace must rediscover what he knows through objects and observations, intercut with flashback sequences showing his recruitment by Stratt, his role in the Astrophage crisis response, and the mission preparation. Memory returns piecemeal and non-linearly, mirroring the reader's own gradual orientation.

## Why It Matters

The amnesia device is primarily a narrative-craft choice that serves multiple functions: it prevents an exposition wall, it forces Grace to *demonstrate* his intelligence by reasoning from first principles (establishing his scientist persona), and it sustains dramatic tension about the mission's stakes. The structure also means the novel can withhold information about the crew's fate and Grace's coerced selection until emotionally optimal moments. For KB purposes, this is the lens through which all character analysis of Grace should be filtered — his arc is one of recovery, not just discovery. See [[Ryland Grace]].
