# PHM Novel - Chapter 14

← [[PHM Novel - Chapter 13]] | [[Chapter Index]] | [[PHM Novel - Chapter 15]] →

> **One-line summary** — Rocky finally enters the Hail Mary, and the wonder of direct collaboration is matched by flashback evidence of how secretly Grace was chosen for the mission.

## 🎯 Intuition
**The Core Idea:** The partnership becomes physically real just as Grace's lack of agency in the mission becomes harder to ignore.
**Why It Matters:** This chapter is a major structural transition: Grace and Rocky stop being separated pen pals and become co-present collaborators. At the same time, the flashback confirms that Grace's survival on the ship depended on a hidden biological criterion, sharpening the novel's ongoing questions about consent and necessity.

## ⚙️ Core Mechanics
### Summary

Grace watches through the porthole as the Blip-A's hull robot installs an entirely new tunnel to replace the old one — Rocky can modify infrastructure from the outside, maintaining his own atmospheric pressure throughout. Rocky then enters the Hail Mary for the first time: a xenonite sphere (Rocky is "as heavy as a motorcycle") is guided through the new airlock by Grace. The two tour the ship together. Rocky is astounded by Grace's laptop and other human technology; he anchors himself with pentagon-face magnets to navigate the Hail Mary's metal surfaces in zero g. In the flashback strand, Stratt introduces Grace to Dr. Lamai and reveals the coma-resistance screening programme: only roughly one in seven thousand people carry the genetic marker that makes long-duration induced torpor survivable; astronauts are tested but there are nowhere near enough. Grace is included in the tests without being given the full picture of why. Back in the present, the chapter closes with a dramatic disclosure: Rocky reveals he has been alone at Tau Ceti for forty-six Earth years — longer than Grace has been alive. He is 209 years old; average Eridian lifespan is 689 years. He also reveals that the Blip-A's sample-collection device fell off the ship during the journey, leaving him unable to collect Astrophage from near Tau Ceti — but Grace has the External Collection Unit on the Hail Mary.

## 🔬 Deep Dive
### Science and Technology

- Xenonite pressure sphere as engineering solution to incompatible atmospheric environments (Rocky at 29 atm ammonia vs Grace at 0.2 atm oxygen) — see [[Rocky and the Eridians]].
- Rocky's magnetic anchoring using pentagon-face magnets: pragmatic solution to zero-g mobility on metal surfaces — see [[Rocky and the Eridians]].
- Induced-torpor coma-resistance genetic marker: one in ~7,000 people; required for surviving the multi-year journey — see [[Artificial Gravity and Induced Torpor]] and [[Ryland Grace]].
- Rocky's 46-year solitary vigil at Tau Ceti: contextualises his patience, capability, and the depth of his isolation — see [[Rocky and the Eridians]].
- Rocky's lost sample-collection device and Grace's External Collection Unit as the complementary asset that makes their partnership scientifically complete.

### Narrative Analysis
Weir balances novelty and unease by pairing the delight of Rocky exploring human space with the cold procedural logic of coma screening. The chapter's big reveals arrive through conversation, which keeps the tone intimate even when the implications are unsettling.
