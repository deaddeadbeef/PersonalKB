# PHM Novel - Chapter 20

← [[PHM Novel - Chapter 19]] | [[Chapter Index]] | [[PHM Novel - Chapter 21]] →

> **One-line summary** — Grace finally identifies Taumoeba while flashbacks to Baikonur reveal the catastrophe that forced him onto the Hail Mary crew.

## 🎯 Intuition
**The Core Idea:** The novel's biological answer appears at the same time Grace's last major backstory gap closes.
**Why It Matters:** This chapter fuses the two halves of the book: the flashback arc explains exactly why Grace is here, and the present arc discovers the organism that may save both worlds. It also ends on a sharp systems-failure cliffhanger, immediately converting scientific success into the next crisis.

## ⚙️ Core Mechanics
### Summary

The chapter interweaves two timelines. In the **flashback** (nine days before Hail Mary launch at Baikonur Cosmodrome), Grace meets privately with the three-person crew to document their preferred methods of death. DuBois presents a meticulous written plan for nitrogen asphyxiation via his EVA suit — carefully engineered, with a backup involving the airlock. Ilyukhina wants a medically supervised heroin overdose: maximum pleasure from the first doses, then a lethal final dose. Commander Yao requests a Type-92 handgun and volunteers to die last so he can assist the other two if anything goes wrong. Shortly after, a massive explosion destroys the Baikonur research centre, killing both DuBois and Dr. Annie Shapiro (the mission's science specialist). Metal condensate at the scene indicates extreme temperatures — the only source aboard is Astrophage. A generator experiment gone wrong. Stratt slaps Grace out of his grief: nine days remain to find a replacement science specialist, and that search is how Grace ends up as a crew member.

In the **present strand**, Rocky regains consciousness and corrects a near-fatal misunderstanding: the black substance Grace blew from his carapace vents was not soot but Rocky's own biological scab material — protective tissue his body generates to cover burn damage during healing. Because Grace waited until Rocky had healed sufficiently, the accidental removal was ultimately survivable rather than lethal. Rocky, still weak, builds a sealed xenonite extraction box — with externally controlled armatures and dual-environment valves — so Grace can safely sample cells from the Adrian air container without cross-atmospheric contamination. Off the painkillers and rested, Grace uses the box to prepare a microscope slide, and almost immediately observes an amoeba-like organism engulf and kill a clump of Astrophage. He names it "Taumoeba." He and Rocky set up two atmospheric simulation experiments — replicating Venus's upper atmosphere (−100 °C, 0.02 atm CO₂) and Threeworld's upper atmosphere (−82 °C, mixed CO₂/N₂/SO₂) — to test whether Taumoeba can survive in the planets where Astrophage is breeding out of control. The chapter ends: "Then the lights go out."

## 🔬 Deep Dive
### Science and Technology

- Rocky's biological scab material: protective tissue (not soot) secreted to cover burns during healing; premature removal is lethal — Grace survived his mistake only because Rocky had healed enough before the intervention — see [[Rocky and the Eridians]].
- "Gravity baseline" theory of comparable intelligence: animal reflexes are calibrated to local gravity; sentient species evolve to outpace their planet's animals; Earth and Erid have similar surface gravities, producing similar intelligence levels — see [[Rocky and the Eridians]].
- Xenonite sample-extraction box: sealed dual-environment container with external-control armatures; allows Grace to gather Adrian microbes without cross-atmospheric exposure — see [[Taumoeba and the Biological Solution]].
- Taumoeba discovery: amoeba-like organism from Adrian engulfs and kills Astrophage clumps within seconds; the prey are paralysed (unable to accelerate) before encirclement completes, suggesting a chemical exretion — see [[Taumoeba and the Biological Solution]].
- Venus atmospheric simulation (−100 °C, 0.02 atm, CO₂-dominant) and Threeworld simulation (−82 °C, 0.02 atm, CO₂/N₂/4% SO₂): first test of Taumoeba survivability outside Adrian — foundational step toward a planetary delivery solution — see [[Taumoeba and the Biological Solution]].
- Baikonur explosion: metal condensate at site indicates Astrophage-temperature heat (~3000 °C); a generator experiment caused the detonation; 16 staff killed including DuBois and Shapiro — see [[Eva Stratt and the Ethics of Existential Response]].

### Plot Connections

- Previous chapter: [[PHM Novel - Chapter 19]]

- The "Then the lights go out" cliffhanger is the direct inciting event for Chapter 21's total-power-loss crisis (Taumoeba fuel contamination).
- Taumoeba's naming here is the pivotal scientific moment: from candidate predator cells first seen in Chapter 16 to a confirmed, named, and actively breeding organism with a path toward planetary deployment.
- DuBois and Shapiro's deaths in the flashback explain the chain of events that made Grace an involuntary crew member — completing the backstory thread begun in earlier flashback chapters.
- Rocky's scab-biology correction continues the theme of well-meaning cross-species assumptions going wrong; Grace's intervention was nearly lethal despite good intent.
- The Venus/Threeworld atmospheric experiments establish the directed-evolution research arc that dominates Chapters 22–27. See [[Taumoeba and the Biological Solution]].

### Narrative Analysis
The structure lets breakthrough and tragedy illuminate each other: Taumoeba's discovery feels even bigger because it is set against the loss that made Grace the accidental crew member. The final blackout is effective because the chapter first creates a sense of hard-won stability and progress.
