# Paper Trading Lab

## One-Line Summary

Paper trading is a simulator for decision quality, not proof that a strategy will work with real money.

## Purpose

This lab turns the first Stock Trading pass into ten written, reviewable paper trades. It exists to test whether the learner can connect filings, price action, account mechanics, order type, position size, and post-trade review without risking live capital.

## Lab Rules

1. Use no live capital.
2. Use no margin, short selling, options, leveraged ETFs, or intraday frequent-trading strategy.
3. Trade only after the thesis is written before entry.
4. Size every trade from planned risk, not conviction.
5. Record the intended order type and the risk chosen by that order type.
6. Review process quality separately from profit or loss.

## Setup

Before the first paper trade, complete:

- [[Stock Trading - Learning Path]] through Pass 4.
- [[Company Filing Worksheet]] for at least one company.
- [[Order Types and Execution]] enough to explain market, limit, stop, and stop-limit behavior.
- [[Accounts Settlement and Margin]] enough to explain T+1 settlement and why margin is excluded.
- [[Position Sizing and Trade Journaling]] enough to calculate `1R`.

## Process Grade

Grade each paper trade on process, not outcome.

| Grade | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A | Thesis, order, risk, size, invalidation, exit, and review were written before entry and followed |
| B | One minor process miss, but the planned risk and invalidation were clear |
| C | Trade was understandable after the fact, but one key decision was not written before entry |
| F | No written invalidation, no planned risk, unclear order type, or rule violation |

## Batch Review

After ten trades, answer:

1. How many trades had a complete pre-trade plan?
2. How many trades violated the planned order, invalidation, or size?
3. Which mistakes were market understanding errors?
4. Which mistakes were account mechanics errors?
5. Which mistakes were emotional or discipline errors?
6. Which setup type should be banned from the next batch?
7. Which single rule would have prevented the largest avoidable loss?

## Advancement Rule

Do not move from this lab to live capital. The next step is another paper batch with one narrower setup and one explicit rule improvement.
