# Price Action Momentum and Volatility

## One-Line Summary

Price action shows what buyers and sellers are doing, but it does not explain by itself why a move is happening or whether it is sustainable.

## Price Action Vocabulary

| Term | Working meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Trend | Sustained directional movement over a chosen timeframe |
| Range | Price oscillating between approximate support and resistance areas |
| Breakout | Price moving beyond a prior range or level |
| Volume | Shares traded; often used to judge participation |
| Volatility | Magnitude and speed of price swings |
| Beta | Historical sensitivity to a benchmark index |
| Momentum | Persistence of recent direction over a short period |

These terms are descriptive. They do not guarantee future movement.

## Momentum

FINRA describes momentum investing as a strategy that tries to exploit short-term price movements in stocks that are moving rapidly higher or lower. FINRA also warns that momentum can reverse quickly, indicators can give false signals, and even experienced investors using sophisticated tools cannot predict unexpected shocks.

For a beginner, momentum belongs in the vocabulary layer before the strategy layer. Learn what it means; do not assume it is an edge.

## Volatility

Volatility is the degree of price movement. FINRA describes higher volatility as larger price swings and higher potential risk. Volatility can create opportunity for traders, but it also increases slippage, emotional pressure, stop-outs, gaps, and position-sizing errors.

Beta is one common relative-volatility measure. A stock with beta above 1 has historically moved more than its benchmark for a given benchmark move; beta below 1 has historically moved less. Beta is historical, not a guarantee.

## Technical Analysis Boundary

Technical analysis studies price, volume, trends, and related indicators. It can help structure entries, exits, and risk levels, but it cannot replace business analysis, account mechanics, or position sizing. In this wiki, technical tools are only allowed after the learner can explain:

1. What the company is.
2. What the account rules are.
3. What the order type does.
4. What amount is at risk.
5. What invalidates the trade.

## Chart Journal Prompt

For any chart observation, write:

- Ticker and date.
- Timeframe.
- Trend, range, or breakout.
- Volume context.
- Volatility context.
- Fundamental or news context if known.
- Planned invalidation point.
- What would prove the visual read wrong.
