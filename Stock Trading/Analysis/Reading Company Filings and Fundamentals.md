# Reading Company Filings and Fundamentals

## One-Line Summary

Fundamental stock analysis starts with how the company makes money, what risks it faces, what its filings disclose, and what price investors are paying for the business.

## First Source: EDGAR

EDGAR is the SEC's public database for company filings. It is the first source to check for U.S. public-company financial and operating information. Investor.gov explains that EDGAR can be used to review registration statements, prospectuses, 10-K annual reports, 10-Q quarterly reports, 8-K current reports, proxy statements, and related disclosures.

## What to Read in a 10-K

Investor.gov's 10-K guidance identifies several beginner-critical sections:

| Section | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Business | What the company sells and how it operates |
| Risk Factors | What could materially hurt the company |
| MD&A | Management's explanation of operating results and financial condition |
| Financial Statements | Income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, and notes |

Read filings as evidence, not marketing. The goal is not to find one attractive sentence; it is to understand the business model, risk map, financial condition, and management's own explanation of change.

## Due Diligence Questions

FINRA's stock-evaluation guidance suggests starting with basic operating and financial questions:

- How does the company make money?
- Are its products or services in demand?
- How has the company performed?
- Is management experienced?
- Is the company positioned for growth and profitability?
- How much debt does it carry?
- What industry and macro risks affect it?

## Core Ratios

| Ratio | Basic use |
| --- | --- |
| EPS | Earnings per share; a per-share profit measure |
| P/E | Price divided by EPS; a common valuation multiple |
| P/S | Market capitalization divided by revenue; sometimes used when earnings are weak or negative |
| D/E | Debt-to-equity; a leverage measure |

Ratios are comparison tools, not final answers. Compare them against the company's history, peers, industry economics, growth rate, margins, debt load, and risk profile.
