# How Stock Markets Work

## One-Line Summary

Stock markets match buyers and sellers through brokers, exchanges, market centers, quotes, and rules; the visible price is only a snapshot of executable supply and demand.

## The Basic Flow

1. The investor submits an order to a brokerage account.
2. The broker routes the order according to its routing system, regulatory duties, and available market centers.
3. A market center attempts to execute the order against available bids or offers.
4. The trade execution creates a transaction price.
5. Clearing and settlement transfer money and securities after the trade.

The beginner should separate order entry from execution and settlement. Clicking buy is not the same event as receiving the exact price, and trade date is not the same event as settlement date.

## Quotes

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Bid | Highest displayed price buyers are currently willing to pay |
| Ask | Lowest displayed price sellers are currently willing to accept |
| Spread | Difference between ask and bid |
| Last price | Most recent trade price, not a promise of the next execution price |
| Volume | Number of shares traded over a period |
| Liquidity | Depth and ease of trading without large price impact |

A tight spread and deep liquidity usually make execution more predictable. A wide spread, thin volume, or fast-moving price makes execution less predictable.

## Market Safeguards

U.S. markets include volatility controls. Investor.gov describes market-wide circuit breakers that can halt trading after large single-day declines in the S&P 500 and single-stock Limit Up-Limit Down mechanisms that pause trading when prices move outside specified bands.

These safeguards do not remove risk. They can stop trading temporarily, interrupt exit plans, and remind the trader that liquidity can change abruptly.

## First Mental Model

Think of the market as an order book and routing system, not a single price oracle. The current price is the result of the most recent matched transaction. The next execution depends on the order type, order size, visible and hidden liquidity, routing, volatility, and competing orders.
