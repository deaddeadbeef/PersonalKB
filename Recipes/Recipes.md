## How To Use This Library

Use the recipe vault as a meal system, not a cookbook to browse endlessly. Start from the protein target and the day shape. If lunch needs to travel, choose bentos, wraps, subs, or crunchwraps. If dinner can be assembled hot, choose bowls, skillets, or stir-fries. If calories are tight, adjust the starch and sauce first before cutting protein.

Each recipe page is intended to answer four practical questions: how much protein it delivers, whether it survives as lunch, what equipment it needs, and how much active time it costs. Those fields matter more than novelty. A recipe earns its place when it is repeatable on a normal week and clear enough to cook without rereading a long story.

## Maintenance Rules

After cooking, record the real-world outcome. Keep the score simple: repeat, revise, or retire. Add one note about texture, reheating, packing, or satiety. When several recipes in a category fail the same way, fix the category pattern rather than each page separately. The goal is a small dependable menu that can be scaled, not a large collection of untested ideas.
