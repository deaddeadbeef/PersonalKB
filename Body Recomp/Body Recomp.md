### The Big Picture

I'm a **returning lifter** who came back from a long detraining period. At 107.5 kg and 32% body fat, I could barely squat 40 kg. Nine months later, I've lost nearly 15 kg of mostly fat while rebuilding my squat to 100 kg for working sets — textbook **muscle memory recomposition**.

My stored myonuclei from previous training allowed rapid muscle regrowth even in a caloric deficit. Combined with high protein intake (2.0 g/kg) and progressive overload, ~85-90% of weight lost was fat.

## 🎯 Recommendations — Next Steps

### Immediate (This Week)

> [!tip] **ACTIVE: Diet Break in Progress (April 13–27, 2026)**
> - **Priority #1:** Protein 180-200g/day from whole foods (non-negotiable)
> - **Priority #2:** Carbs — increase via rice, oats, potatoes, fruit (glycogen replenishment)
> - Calories: ~2,000-2,300 kcal/day (don't force junk to hit the top end)
> - Fat: ~60-70g/day
> - **Rule:** Only eat clean — if you land at 2,000 with 180g protein, that's a success
> - Training: unchanged — maintain current program
> - Expected scale impact: +1-2 kg (water + glycogen, NOT fat)
> - After break: resume deficit, MF algorithm will have recalibrated

1. **Break the MacroFactor budget spiral.**The app's expenditure algorithm has been steadily cutting your calorie target because it estimates your TDEE is dropping. This has dragged protein from 211g → 144g — a serious risk to muscle retention. Two immediate actions:
   - **Set a protein floor**: In MacroFactor's macro settings, lock protein at ≥180g (or ≥2.0 g/kg). Let carbs and fat absorb any budget changes — never let the algorithm touch your protein.
   - **Manually override if needed**: If the app gives you a daily target below ~1,800 kcal, override it. At your current activity level and size, going below 1,800 consistently will cost you muscle.

2. **Add 5-10g fiber.** Easiest fix: 1 tablespoon of psyllium husk in water (5g fiber, 15 kcal). Or add a cup of broccoli to one meal.

3. **Get a body fat check.** You're likely at 22-24% based on your strength gains and weight loss trajectory, but a visual assessment or DEXA scan would anchor the estimates.

### Short-Term (Next 2-4 Weeks)

5. ~~**Take a diet break.**~~ ✅ **Started April 13.** Eating at or near maintenance (~2,000-2,300 kcal) for 1-2 weeks with protein locked at 180-200g from whole foods. Prioritizing protein and carbs; NOT forcing junk calories to hit a number. Resume deficit around April 27.

5. **Audit your training volume.** You have workout data in MacroFactor. Review whether you're hitting 10-20 sets per muscle group per week. Volume is the key signal for muscle preservation.

### Medium-Term (Next 2-3 Months)

6. **Continue cutting to 20% body fat.** Based on your current lean mass (~70-72 kg), hitting 20% BF means a target of ~87-90 kg. At your current rate (0.38 kg/week), that's roughly 2-3 months away.

7. **Progress the squat past bodyweight.** You're at 1.08× BW now. Pushing toward 1.25-1.5× BW (115-135 kg) would be the next strength milestone. Don't expect fast gains during a deficit — maintaining is winning.

8. **Track all main lifts.** Squat is the headline number, but also track bench press, deadlift, overhead press, and row to get a complete picture of muscle retention.

### Phase Transition (When You Hit 20% BF)

9. **Reverse diet to maintenance.** Increase calories by 100-150/week until at TDEE. Hold maintenance for 4-8 weeks to let hormones fully normalize.

10. **Decide: continue cutting or lean bulk.** At 20% BF with a solid strength base, you'll be in a great position for either:
    - Cut to 15-17% for visible abs
    - Lean bulk (+200-300 kcal) to build more muscle, then cut again later
