# Llama 4 and Open Model Evolution

## Summary

Meta's Llama family has been a cornerstone of the open-weights model ecosystem. Llama 3.1 (July 2024) scaled to 405B parameters with competitive frontier performance. Llama 3.2 (November 2024) added multimodal capabilities (vision). Llama 4 (2025) adopted a mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture, achieving competitive performance with closed frontier models while maintaining open weights. The Llama ecosystem has spawned extensive fine-tuning, quantisation, and derivative model development.

## Key Claims

1. Llama 4's MoE architecture enables frontier capability at reduced inference cost
2. Open weights under Meta's license enable community fine-tuning and derivative models
3. The gap between open and closed frontier models has narrowed significantly
4. Multimodal capabilities (Llama 3.2 onward) bring vision understanding to open models
5. The Llama ecosystem (fine-tuning tools, quantisation, deployment) is the most mature open model stack

## Atomic Facts

1. Llama 3.1 405B released July 2024
2. Llama 3.2 added multimodal (vision) capabilities, November 2024
3. Llama 4 adopted mixture-of-experts architecture in 2025
4. Weights released under Meta's community license (permissive with usage limits)
5. Extensive derivative model ecosystem (fine-tuned, quantised variants)
6. Competitive with closed models on many standard benchmarks

## Significance

Llama's continued evolution demonstrates that open-weights development can keep pace with closed frontier models, ensuring that advanced AI capabilities remain accessible to the broader research and developer community.
