# Distillation and Model Compression

> **Distillation makes a smaller model learn a larger model's behavior, while compression techniques like pruning try to keep capability while reducing cost.**

## 🎯 Intuition
**The Core Idea:** A smaller student model can inherit much of a larger teacher model's capability by learning from its richer outputs instead of only from hard labels.
**Analogy:** Distillation is like a student learning a master's intuitions, not just memorizing the final answers on an exam.
**Why It Matters:** Distillation produces smaller models that are faster and cheaper to run while retaining much of the teacher's capability. A 1.5B distilled model can approach the quality of a 7B teacher while being 4-5× faster, enabling real-time applications or deployment on smaller GPUs. At the same time, pruning and synthetic-data training offer other routes to compression, each with trade-offs in quality, efficiency, and legality.

---

## ⚙️ Core Mechanics
### How It Works
- Knowledge distillation trains a smaller "student" model to mimic the behavior of a larger "teacher" model.
- The classic formulation uses the teacher's output distribution as a soft target—rather than training only on the hard label (the correct answer), the student learns from the full probability distribution over all tokens.
- This captures richer information about relationships between outputs: if the teacher assigns 0.4 probability to "happy," 0.3 to "joyful," and 0.2 to "glad," the student learns these words are similar, improving generalization beyond what sparse hard labels provide.
- Temperature scaling makes the teacher's distribution "softer" (less peaky) by dividing logits by a temperature T>1 before softmax.
- This amplifies small probabilities, giving the student more nuanced information about the teacher's uncertainty.
- The student is trained on a combination of the soft teacher distribution (with temperature) and the hard ground-truth labels.
- Beyond output distillation, feature distillation matches intermediate representations—aligning the student's hidden states or attention maps with the teacher's, though this requires architectural compatibility and adds complexity.
- Modern "distillation" for LLMs often means something different: training a smaller model on synthetic data generated by a larger model, without necessarily matching distributions.
- This resembles the original distillation idea but is technically just supervised fine-tuning on teacher-generated data.
- Pruning complements distillation—removing parameters (unstructured) or entire channels/layers (structured) to shrink models.
- Structured pruning maintains hardware efficiency but requires careful calibration to avoid accuracy collapse.
- **Soft targets**: Student learns from teacher's full output distribution, not just argmax.
- **Temperature scaling**: Divide logits by T>1 before softmax to soften distribution.
- **Loss function**: `L = α * L_soft(student, teacher) + (1-α) * L_hard(student, labels)`.
- **Response distillation**: Match output token distributions (most common).
- **Feature distillation**: Match intermediate hidden states or attention weights.
- **Pruning (unstructured)**: Remove individual weights below threshold (irregular, hard to accelerate).
- **Pruning (structured)**: Remove entire neurons, heads, or layers (maintains hardware efficiency).
- **Synthetic data training**: Generate large dataset from teacher, train student on it.

### Key Specifications

| Concept | What It Is | What It's Not |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Soft Targets vs Hard Labels** | Full probability distribution from teacher | One-hot encoded ground truth |
| **Response Distillation vs Feature Distillation** | Match output logits | Match intermediate layer activations |
| **Distillation vs Training on Synthetic Data** | Distillation matches distributions | Synthetic training uses teacher outputs as labels |
| **Structured vs Unstructured Pruning** | Structured: remove entire components | Unstructured: remove individual weights |
| **Distillation vs Quantization** | Trains new smaller model | Compresses existing model |
| **Pruning vs Shrinking Architecture** | Remove params from trained model | Train smaller model from scratch |

### Key Facts
- Distillation quality depends heavily on data: the student can only learn what is represented in the training set.
- Domain-specific distillation therefore requires domain-specific data.
- Pruning is less commonly used for LLMs than distillation because unstructured pruning does not accelerate inference on GPUs.
- Structured pruning often degrades quality more than training a smaller model from scratch.
- Licensing is a real concern: training a model on proprietary teacher outputs may violate the teacher's terms.

## 🔬 Deep Dive
### Technical Details
The central technical insight of distillation is that the teacher's output distribution contains more information than a hard label alone. Instead of learning only which token was "correct," the student also learns which alternatives the teacher considered plausible, and by how much. Temperature scaling is what makes this especially useful: raising the temperature before softmax softens the distribution and amplifies otherwise tiny probabilities, exposing structure in the teacher's uncertainty.

Feature distillation goes further by matching internal representations such as hidden states or attention maps. This can transfer richer internal behavior, but it introduces practical complications because teacher and student architectures must be compatible enough for those intermediate targets to line up. In modern LLM practice, many systems labeled "distilled" are really trained on synthetic teacher-generated data rather than true distribution matching.

Pruning attacks the same efficiency problem from a different angle. Unstructured pruning removes individual weights but typically yields irregular sparsity that is hard to accelerate on common hardware. Structured pruning removes larger coherent units such as heads, neurons, or layers, which is friendlier to hardware but riskier for quality. Both distillation and pruning also raise legal and licensing questions when the source teacher is proprietary.

### Limitations and Criticisms
- Distillation can only transfer behavior present in the teacher outputs and the chosen training distribution, so gaps in data become gaps in the student.
- Feature distillation is more complex than response distillation because it requires architectural compatibility and extra alignment choices.
- Compression methods can run into legal or licensing issues when the teacher model is proprietary and the student is trained on its outputs.

### Impact and Legacy
Distillation and model compression are foundational for turning powerful but expensive models into practical systems. They enable faster inference, cheaper deployment, and smaller hardware footprints without requiring the student to match the teacher's size. In LLMs, the term "distillation" has also broadened to include synthetic-data training, showing how the original teacher-student idea evolved as model-serving costs became a central concern.
