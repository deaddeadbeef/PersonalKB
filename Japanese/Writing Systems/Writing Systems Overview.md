# Writing Systems Overview

Japanese uses three writing systems simultaneously — a feature unique among modern languages.

## The Three Scripts

### Hiragana (ひらがな ![[writing-001-hiragana.mp3]])
- **Purpose:** Native Japanese words, grammatical particles, verb/adjective endings
- **Count:** 46 base characters + combinations (dakuten, handakuten, combo kana)
- **Learn first:** This is your foundation — everything else builds on it
- **Deep dive:** [[Hiragana Complete Guide]]

### Katakana (カタカナ ![[writing-002-katakana.mp3]])
- **Purpose:** Foreign loanwords, onomatopoeia, emphasis, scientific terms
- **Count:** 46 base characters (mirrors hiragana)
- **Priority:** Learn alongside or immediately after hiragana
- **Deep dive:** [[Katakana Complete Guide]]

### Kanji (漢字 ![[writing-003-kanji.mp3]])
- **Purpose:** Content words — nouns, verb stems, adjective stems
- **Count:** 2,136 jōyō kanji (daily-use) / ~3,000 for full literacy
- **Challenge:** Each kanji has multiple readings (on'yomi + kun'yomi)
- **Deep dive:** [[Kanji — How Kanji Work]]

## How They Work Together

A typical Japanese sentence mixes all three:

> 私は毎日コーヒーを飲みます。 ![[writing-004-watashi-wa-mainichi-kohii-o-nomimasu.mp3]]
> Watashi wa mainichi kōhī o nomimasu.
> "I drink coffee every day."

| Part | Script | Role |
|------|--------|------|
| 私 ![[writing-005-watashi.mp3]] (watashi) | Kanji | "I" — content word |
| は ![[writing-006-particle-wa.mp3]] (wa) | Hiragana | Topic particle |
| 毎日 ![[writing-007-mainichi.mp3]] (mainichi) | Kanji | "Every day" — content |
| コーヒー ![[writing-008-kohii.mp3]] (kōhī) | Katakana | "Coffee" — loanword |
| を ![[writing-009-particle-o.mp3]] (o) | Hiragana | Object particle |
| 飲みます ![[writing-010-nomimasu.mp3]] (nomimasu) | Kanji + Hiragana | Verb stem (kanji) + ending (hiragana) |

## Learning Order
1. **Week 1-2:** Master hiragana (reading + writing)
2. **Week 2-3:** Master katakana
3. **Week 3+:** Begin kanji (never stops — lifelong learning)
