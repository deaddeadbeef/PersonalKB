# Grammar Overview

Japanese grammar is fundamentally different from English. Understanding the core patterns unlocks the entire language.

## Core Principles

### SOV Word Order
Japanese is Subject-Object-Verb (English is SVO):
> 私は 本を 読みます。
> ![[gramov-001-watashihahonwoyomimasu.mp3]]
> Watashi wa / hon o / yomimasu.
> I / book / read. → "I read a book."

### Basic Copula Examples
> 私は学生です。 (watashi wa gakusei desu) — I am a student.
> ![[grammar-001-watashi-wa-gakusei-desu.mp3]]
>
> これは本です。 (kore wa hon desu) — This is a book.
> ![[grammar-002-kore-wa-hon-desu.mp3]]

### Particles Mark Roles
Words are tagged with particles that mark their grammatical role — word order is flexible:
- は (wa) — topic marker
  ![[gramov-002-ha.mp3]]
- が (ga) — subject marker
  ![[gramov-003-ga.mp3]]
- を (o) — object marker
  ![[gramov-004-wo.mp3]]
- に (ni) — direction/time/indirect object
  ![[gramov-005-ni.mp3]]
- で (de) — location of action/means
  ![[gramov-006-de.mp3]]

### Verb-Final
The verb always comes at the end. Everything else is relatively free.

### Conjugation-Heavy
Verbs and adjectives conjugate for tense, negation, formality, and mood. Nouns do not conjugate.
