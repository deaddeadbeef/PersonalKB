# Vocabulary Overview

Building vocabulary is the single biggest factor in reading comprehension. Japanese has three major word origins with distinct patterns.

## Word Origins

### Wago (和語) — Native Japanese
- Oldest layer of vocabulary
- Usually written in hiragana or with kun'yomi kanji readings
- Examples: 山 (yama, mountain), 食べる (taberu, eat), 美しい (utsukushii, beautiful)
![[nontbl-026-yama.mp3]]
![[nontbl-027-taberu.mp3]]

### Kango (漢語) — Chinese-Origin
- ~60% of vocabulary in formal/academic contexts
- Usually use on'yomi kanji readings
- Examples: 図書館 (toshokan, library), 科学 (kagaku, science), 経済 (keizai, economy)
![[nontbl-031-toshokan.mp3]]
![[nontbl-032-kagaku.mp3]]
![[nontbl-033-keizai.mp3]]

### Gairaigo (外来語) — Foreign Loanwords
- Mostly from English, written in katakana
- Growing rapidly in modern Japanese
- Examples: コンピューター (konpyuutaa, computer), アルバイト (arubaito, part-time job [from German])

## Learning Strategy
1. **Core frequency first** — the top 1,000 words cover ~85% of daily conversation
2. **Thematic clusters** — learn related words together (all food words, all travel words)
3. **Kanji compounds** — once you know component kanji, compounds become guessable
4. **Context over lists** — always learn words in example sentences, never in isolation
