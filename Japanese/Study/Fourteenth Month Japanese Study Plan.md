# Fourteenth Month Japanese Study Plan

> Use this for the fourth month of [[Phase 5 — Advanced]]. The job is to turn advanced work into a repeatable benchmark and refinement loop: every week needs one JLPT-style listening item or diagnostic task, one real native input segment on the same topic, role, or function, one Block 4 local diagnostic drill set, one named bottleneck, and one next-source decision recorded in [[Phase 5 Weekly Review]].

## Purpose

This page is the Month 14 execution path. It follows [[Thirteenth Month Japanese Study Plan]] and wraps Block 4 of [[Phase 5 Audio Assignment Ladder]].

Use it only after the [[Thirteenth Month Japanese Study Plan#Month-Thirteen Success Criteria|Month 13 success criteria]] are real. If nuance work still depends on isolated clips instead of native/tutor/course context, repeat the relevant Week 53-56 work before starting this page.

## Before Week 57

Do this once:

1. Open [[Phase 5 Audio Assignment Ladder]] and copy Block 4 into [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log#Current Assignment]].
2. Choose one JLPT-style listening item, mock section, course diagnostic, or timed listening task from [[JLPT Overview — N5 to N1]] or your current N2/N1 resource.
3. Choose one real native input segment on the same topic, role, or function from [[Phase 5 Authentic Audio Spine]], [[Advanced Listening Resources]], news, podcast, interview, lecture, workplace audio, drama, documentary, tutor recording, or course audio.
4. Open [[Phase 5 Local Audio Practice]] Block 4, [[Phase 5 Audio Coverage Map]], [[Phase 5 Weekly Review]], [[Advanced Output and Register Feedback Log]], [[Study Roadmap — Intermediate to Advanced]], and [[Pronunciation and Audio Accuracy]].
5. Decide the first bottleneck label before the second study day: speed, vocabulary, grammar, register, pitch, stamina, topic knowledge, or transfer from test audio to real audio.
6. Name the proof route: score/error log, transcript/subtitle comparison, shadowing, summary, bottleneck table, tutor/native feedback, or next-source decision.

Default route: use one JLPT-style listening question or short diagnostic, then one native segment on the same function. Keep both short enough to compare actively on at least three days.

## Month-Fourteen Rules

| Rule | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| One Block 4 assignment | Keep the month on Phase 5 Block 4 until the benchmark/refinement loop is real |
| Pair test and reality | Every JLPT-style task must be paired with real native input on the same topic, role, or function |
| Bottleneck before resources | Name the bottleneck before adding new materials |
| Local clips are diagnostics | Use [[Phase 5 Local Audio Practice]] Block 4 to isolate the bottleneck, not to replace real audio |
| Native transfer is the proof | Improvement counts when the same skill transfers from test audio to native input |
| Next segment is required | End each week by naming the next exact source segment |
| Feedback is logged when output matters | Use [[Advanced Output and Register Feedback Log]] if the bottleneck includes summary, argument, register, pitch, or spoken output |
| Weekly proof is required | End each week with [[Phase 5 Weekly Review]] and one row in [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]] |

If a local clip or diagnostic points to pronunciation, pitch, or reading uncertainty, check [[Pronunciation and Audio Accuracy]] and record suspect clips in [[Pronunciation Correction Log]].

## Four-Week Path

| Week | Main content | Local audio | Authentic model | Output proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 57 | [[JLPT Overview — N5 to N1]] and benchmark setup | Block 4 diagnostic clips from [[Phase 5 Local Audio Practice]] | One JLPT-style listening item plus one real segment on the same topic/function | Name the bottleneck and record the first comparison |
| 58 | Speed, vocabulary, and stamina diagnosis | Block 4 speed/media-vocabulary clips or weak Block 1/3 clips | Same native segment or one shorter related segment with text support | Shadow or summarize one short section and mark whether speed, vocabulary, or stamina is limiting |
| 59 | Register, pitch, and grammar transfer | Block 4 register/pitch/grammar diagnostic clips from [[Phase 5 Local Audio Practice]] | JLPT-style item plus workplace, interview, conversation, tutor, course, or native segment with a matching function | Check one register, pitch, or grammar issue through a source-backed route |
| 60 | [[Study Roadmap — Intermediate to Advanced]] and next-cycle decision | Only the local clips that expose the active bottleneck | One final paired test/native comparison | Name the next four-week target, exact source segment, and review cadence |

If a week is overloaded, repeat it. Month 14 is about building an honest refinement loop, not finishing a mock test.

## Week 57

Goal: create a benchmark that transfers to real native listening.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[JLPT Overview — N5 to N1]].
3. Use the Block 4 diagnostic prompts in [[Phase 5 Local Audio Practice]].
4. Work one JLPT-style listening item or short diagnostic.
5. Replay one real native segment on the same topic, role, or function.
6. Name the bottleneck and write the first comparison.

Minimum proof: one paired JLPT-style/native comparison and one bottleneck label.

If the test item and native segment are unrelated, the week does not prove transfer.

## Week 58

Goal: separate speed, vocabulary, and stamina problems.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Reuse the same paired source or choose one shorter related native segment.
3. Use only Block 4 local clips that expose speed, media vocabulary, or discourse timing.
4. Shadow or summarize one short section.
5. Mark whether the failure point was speed, unknown words, weak topic knowledge, or fatigue.

Minimum proof: one short shadowing or summary target with the bottleneck named.

If vocabulary is the bottleneck, do not call it a listening failure until the words are known in context.

## Week 59

Goal: test whether register, pitch, or grammar nuance transfers from controlled audio to native input.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Choose one JLPT-style item or course diagnostic with a clear function.
3. Pair it with one workplace, interview, conversation, tutor, course, drama, or native source segment that uses the same function.
4. Use Block 4 local clips only to isolate register, pitch, or grammar.
5. Check one issue through [[Pronunciation and Audio Accuracy]], tutor/native feedback, course audio, transcript/subtitles, OJAD, NHK accent dictionary, Forvo, or a native recording.

Minimum proof: one source-backed check for register, pitch, grammar, or reading.

If output is part of the fix, log it in [[Advanced Output and Register Feedback Log]].

## Week 60

Goal: turn the benchmark month into a next-cycle plan.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[Study Roadmap — Intermediate to Advanced]].
3. Repeat only local clips that expose the active bottleneck.
4. Re-run one paired test/native comparison.
5. Choose the next four-week target: repeat Block 4, deepen native-source work, repair register/output, repair pitch, repair grammar nuance, or begin a new N2/N1 resource.
6. Name the exact next source segment and review cadence.

Minimum proof: one next-cycle decision with exact source, bottleneck, and review cadence.

If the decision is vague, repeat Week 60 with a smaller source pair.

## Weekly Handoff

At the end of each week:

1. Fill [[Phase 5 Weekly Review]].
2. Add one summary row to [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]].
3. Keep or update the Block 4 assignment from [[Phase 5 Audio Assignment Ladder]].
4. Add an [[Advanced Output and Register Feedback Log]] entry if summary, argument, register, pitch, or spoken output produced a carry-forward fix.
5. Decide whether any suspect local clips need [[Pronunciation Correction Log]].
6. Name the exact next JLPT-style item and native segment before the second study day of the new week.

The weekly review decides the next action. Do not add new N2/N1 material just because the current benchmark feels uncomfortable.

## Minimum Day

Use this on busy days:

- 5 minutes due reviews
- 5 minutes current Block 4 benchmark or roadmap page
- 1-3 diagnostic clips from [[Phase 5 Local Audio Practice]]
- 1 sentence from the JLPT-style item or native source
- 1 bottleneck note or next-source decision

This preserves continuity. It does not replace the weekly paired comparison.

## What Not To Do

- Do not let JLPT practice replace native input.
- Do not add resources before naming the bottleneck.
- Do not diagnose everything as speed.
- Do not count local clips as advanced listening proof.
- Do not move into a new phase without [[Phase 5 Weekly Review]] evidence and a next-source decision.

## Month-Fourteen Success Criteria

Month fourteen counts when all of these are true:

- [[Phase 5 Weekly Review]] has evidence for the Block 4 month;
- [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]] names the exact Block 4 JLPT-style item or diagnostic and native segment pairs;
- Block 4 local diagnostic clips from [[Phase 5 Local Audio Practice]] were used on at least three days per week;
- at least one bottleneck was named each week: speed, vocabulary, grammar, register, pitch, stamina, topic knowledge, or test-to-native transfer;
- suspect local clips, if any, were recorded in [[Pronunciation Correction Log]];
- one JLPT-style listening task was paired with real native input on the same topic, role, or function;
- one speed, vocabulary, or stamina issue was separated from general listening difficulty;
- one register, pitch, grammar, or reading issue was checked through a source-backed route;
- one next-cycle target names the exact source segment, bottleneck, and review cadence;
- the next long-term target is chosen from [[Study Roadmap — Intermediate to Advanced]] or [[Phase 5 Audio Assignment Ladder]].

## Next Cycle

After these success criteria are true, continue to [[Fifteenth Month Japanese Study Plan]]. It turns the next-cycle decision into a four-week refinement cycle: one bottleneck, one native source, one local diagnostic set, one proof type, and one weekly decision. Do not reset to broad study.

Good next cycles:

- N2/N1 listening stamina with news or lectures;
- workplace/register output with tutor/native feedback;
- pitch and reading repair through reference audio;
- grammar nuance through native/course/tutor examples;
- native media comprehension with weekly summary and transcription proof.
