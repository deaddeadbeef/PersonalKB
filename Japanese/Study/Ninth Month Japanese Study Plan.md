# Ninth Month Japanese Study Plan

> Use this for the third month of [[Phase 4 — Intermediate Mastery]]. The job is to make keigo, business phrases, and cultural register usable without trusting local clips as social authority: every week needs one formal role-play, service/workplace audio, business course dialogue, tutor model, or native-speaker recording; one Block 3 local drill set; one explicit role/direction check; and one short register-appropriate role-play or checked sentence.

## Purpose

This page is the Month 9 execution path. It follows [[Eighth Month Japanese Study Plan]] and wraps Block 3 of [[Phase 4 Audio Assignment Ladder]].

Use it only after the [[Eighth Month Japanese Study Plan#Month-Eight Success Criteria|Month 8 success criteria]] are real. If N3 kanji, adult-life vocabulary, and source-backed summaries are still unstable, repeat the relevant Week 33-36 work before starting this page.

## Before Week 37

Do this once:

1. Open [[Phase 4 Audio Assignment Ladder]] and copy Block 3 into [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log#Current Assignment]].
2. Choose one exact formal role-play, service/workplace audio, business course dialogue, tutor model, or native-speaker recording with transcript, subtitles, lesson notes, role notes, or feedback route.
3. Open [[Phase 4 Authentic Audio Spine]], [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3, [[Phase 4 Audio Coverage Map]], [[Phase 4 Weekly Review]], [[Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist]], and [[Pronunciation and Audio Accuracy]].
4. Name the first role-play situation before the second study day.
5. Write the role map before choosing phrases: who is in-group, out-group, superior, customer, colleague, stranger, or yourself?

Default route: use a business course dialogue, Irodori B1-style workplace/service unit, tutor role-play, native-speaker correction, formal phone script, meeting scene, or customer-service segment where the role and social direction are visible.

## Month-Nine Rules

| Rule | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| One Block 3 assignment | Keep the month on Phase 4 Block 3 until register-sensitive output has real evidence |
| Human model first | Use tutor, native, workplace/service, or official-course audio before producing keigo freely |
| Role before phrase | Decide who is elevated, who is lowered, and who belongs to your in-group before drilling forms |
| Local clips are mouth practice | Use [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3 only after the social role is known |
| Checklist gates production | Use [[Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist]] before every role-play, phone line, meeting line, or email sentence |
| Cultural phrases need context | Seasonal greetings, idioms, and number culture require source-backed fit, not isolated memorization |
| Weekly proof is required | End each week with [[Phase 4 Weekly Review]] and one row in [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]] |

If a local clip disagrees with the human/course/tutor model, quarantine it in [[Pronunciation Correction Log]] and keep using the authentic model.

## Week 37

Goal: hear and produce sonkeigo as elevation of the other person, not as generic politeness.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[Keigo — Sonkeigo (Honorific)]].
3. Use the Block 3 sonkeigo clips in [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] only after naming the role.
4. Replay the same formal role-play, service audio, course dialogue, tutor model, or native segment.
5. Complete the role and direction rows in [[Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist]].
6. Say one sentence that elevates the other person or a respected third party.

Minimum proof: one sonkeigo sentence with the elevated person named and the human/course/tutor model identified.

If the form is correct but the social direction is unclear, stop and return to the checklist.

## Week 38

Goal: hear and produce kenjōgo as lowering yourself or your in-group.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[Keigo — Kenjōgo (Humble)]].
3. Use Block 3 kenjōgo clips from [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]].
4. Replay a client, workplace, tutor, or course segment where the speaker lowers self or in-group.
5. Complete the role, direction, form, and human-model rows in [[Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist]].
6. Produce one sentence about your own action or your in-group's action.

Minimum proof: one kenjōgo sentence with the lowered speaker/in-group named and the model identified.

If you are talking about your boss to a client, check the uchi-soto direction before choosing the verb.

## Week 39

Goal: make business phrases usable in a bounded role-play instead of as isolated formulas.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[Business Japanese — Workplace Communication]].
3. Use Block 3 workplace phrase clips from [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]].
4. Replay a workplace, service, phone, meeting, tutor, or business course segment.
5. Complete [[Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist]].
6. Produce one phone line, meeting line, request, apology, or email sentence and compare it to the model.

Minimum proof: one checked business sentence or 20-60 second role-play segment.

If the phrase feels memorized but socially risky, ask for tutor/native feedback or keep it as recognition-only.

## Week 40

Goal: use cultural expressions with context and know when not to use them.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[Seasonal Greetings and Cultural Expressions]], [[Idioms and Proverbs — ことわざ]], or [[Numbers and Superstitions]].
3. Use only the relevant Block 3 cultural clips from [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]].
4. Replay or read a tutor/native/course explanation that gives the season, situation, or social risk.
5. Say one expression with its context, or explain why one expression should be avoided.

Minimum proof: one cultural expression used with context, or one risky expression avoided with the reason explained.

If no context is available, do not produce the phrase. Keep it as recognition-only until a source explains its fit.

## Weekly Handoff

At the end of each week:

1. Fill [[Phase 4 Weekly Review]].
2. Add one summary row to [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]].
3. Keep or update the Block 3 assignment from [[Phase 4 Audio Assignment Ladder]].
4. Attach the role/direction evidence from [[Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist]].
5. Decide whether any suspect local clips need [[Pronunciation Correction Log]].
6. Name the exact next formal role-play, service/workplace audio, business course dialogue, tutor model, or native-speaker recording before the second study day of the new week.

The weekly review decides the next action. Do not advance to Block 4 because the pages were read once.

## Minimum Day

Use this on busy days:

- 5 minutes due reviews
- 5 minutes current Block 3 page
- 1-3 Block 3 local clips from [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]]
- 1 line from the named human/course/tutor model
- 1 role or direction decision in [[Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist]]

This preserves continuity. It does not replace the weekly proof.

## What Not To Do

- Do not produce keigo freely from local clips alone.
- Do not choose a phrase before deciding the role and social direction.
- Do not treat sonkeigo, kenjōgo, and polite business formulas as interchangeable.
- Do not use idioms or seasonal expressions without context.
- Do not advance to Block 4 without [[Phase 4 Weekly Review]] evidence and checklist proof.

## Month-Nine Success Criteria

Month nine counts when all of these are true:

- [[Phase 4 Weekly Review]] has evidence for the Block 3 month;
- [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]] names the exact Block 3 human/course/tutor/native segment or segments;
- Block 3 local clips from [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] were used on at least three days per week;
- [[Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist]] was used before each register-sensitive output proof;
- suspect local clips, if any, were recorded in [[Pronunciation Correction Log]];
- one sonkeigo sentence can be produced with the elevated person named;
- one kenjōgo sentence can be produced with the lowered speaker or in-group named;
- one business phone, meeting, request, apology, or email sentence can be compared against a human/course/tutor model;
- one seasonal, idiom, or number-culture expression can be used or avoided with context explained.

## Next Month

After these success criteria are true, continue to [[Tenth Month Japanese Study Plan]]. It wraps Block 4 of [[Phase 4 Audio Assignment Ladder]] and moves into native-speed support, discourse timing, filler words, source labels, and short shadowing, transcription, or summary proof.

Before starting Block 4, choose one short native-speed segment with transcript, subtitles, article text, or notes, and decide whether the output proof will be shadowing, transcription, or summary.
