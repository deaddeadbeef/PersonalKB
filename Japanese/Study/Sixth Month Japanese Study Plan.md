# Sixth Month Japanese Study Plan

> Use this for the third month of [[Phase 3 — Expansion]]. The job is to make practical-domain vocabulary audible and usable: every week needs one transcripted practical segment, one Block 3 local drill set, one source-backed reading or rhythm check, and one spoken role-play or summary target.

## Purpose

This page is the Month 6 execution path. It follows [[Fifth Month Japanese Study Plan]] and wraps Block 3 of [[Phase 3 Audio Assignment Ladder]].

Use it only after the [[Fifth Month Japanese Study Plan#Month-Five Success Criteria|Month 5 success criteria]] are real. If longer N4 sentences, potential forms, or summaries are still readable but not speakable, repeat the relevant Week 17-20 work before starting this page.

## Before Week 21

Do this once:

1. Open [[Phase 3 Audio Assignment Ladder]] and copy Block 3 into [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log#Current Assignment]].
2. Choose one exact practical dialogue, short article/audio pair, course segment, tutor recording, or native-speaker recording with transcript, subtitles, lesson text, or tutor-provided script.
3. Open [[Phase 3 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3, [[Phase 3 Audio Coverage Map]], [[Phase 3 Weekly Review]], and [[Pronunciation and Audio Accuracy]].
4. Name the first practical situation you will role-play before the second study day.
5. Pick one N4 kanji reading to verify through a source-backed route before the end of Week 23.

Default route: use Genki II, Irodori A2/B1, a graded N4 practical dialogue, a transcripted article/audio pair, or a tutor/native recording where travel, health, dates, onomatopoeia, or N4 kanji vocabulary appears in context.

## Month-Six Rules

| Rule | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| One Block 3 assignment | Keep the month on Block 3 until practical-domain proof is real |
| Situation before list | Hear the vocabulary inside a travel, health, scheduling, or descriptive context before drilling isolated words |
| Local clips isolate the words | Use [[Phase 3 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3 for travel, health, date/time, onomatopoeia, and N4 kanji prompts |
| Authentic audio is the model | Compare rhythm, politeness, and word choice against course, tutor, or native audio |
| Kanji are spoken words first | Check N4 readings inside real vocabulary before testing isolated characters |
| Weekly proof is required | End each week with [[Phase 3 Weekly Review]] and one row in [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]] |

If a local clip disagrees with the authentic model, quarantine it in [[Pronunciation Correction Log]] and keep using the authentic model.

## Week 21

Goal: turn travel vocabulary into usable station, route, and lodging language.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[Thematic Vocabulary — Travel and Transportation]].
3. Use the Block 3 travel clips in [[Phase 3 Local Audio Practice]].
4. Replay the same travel, route, station, lodging, or tutor dialogue with text visible.
5. Role-play one short exchange: ticket, route, transfer, airport, hotel, or inn.

Minimum proof: role-play buying a ticket or asking about a route, then name the exact authentic model used.

If travel words stay isolated, reduce the target to one exchange and repeat it across more days.

## Week 22

Goal: make health and scheduling vocabulary speakable under practical pressure.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[Thematic Vocabulary — Body and Health]] or [[Thematic Vocabulary — Numbers, Time, and Dates]].
3. Use Block 3 health and date/time clips from [[Phase 3 Local Audio Practice]].
4. Replay a clinic, pharmacy, appointment, scheduling, or tutor segment with text visible.
5. Say one symptom, one request, and one date/time sentence aloud.

Minimum proof: say one symptom, one request, and one date/time sentence without reading.

If numbers or dates collapse under speed, slow down and compare only one date/time sentence against the authentic model.

## Week 23

Goal: hear onomatopoeia and N4 kanji vocabulary in context instead of treating them as dictionary entries.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Study one small section from [[Onomatopoeia — Sound and State Words]] or [[Kanji N4 Essentials]].
3. Use Block 3 onomatopoeia and N4 kanji clips from [[Phase 3 Local Audio Practice]].
4. Replay a native, tutor, course, or graded segment where sound/state words or N4 kanji vocabulary appear in context.
5. Check one kanji reading or sound/state word through [[Pronunciation and Audio Accuracy]].

Minimum proof: use two onomatopoeia in short sentences and check one N4 kanji reading through a source-backed route.

If an onomatopoeia word feels vague, do not add more. Find one sentence where the context makes the state or sound clear.

## Week 24

Goal: consolidate Block 3 into a practical-domain summary and role-play routine.

Daily shape:

1. Finish due reviews.
2. Repeat only the weakest Block 3 local clips.
3. Replay the same practical segment or one shorter related segment.
4. Shadow one short line from the authentic model.
5. Summarize the segment or role-play the situation using five practical-domain words.

Minimum proof: summarize one short segment and reuse five practical-domain words aloud.

If the summary is too hard, keep the same segment and produce a shorter role-play instead.

## Weekly Handoff

At the end of each week:

1. Fill [[Phase 3 Weekly Review]].
2. Add one summary row to [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]].
3. Keep or update the Block 3 assignment from [[Phase 3 Audio Assignment Ladder]].
4. Decide whether any suspect local clips need [[Pronunciation Correction Log]].
5. Name the exact next practical segment before the second study day of the new week.

The weekly review decides the next action. Do not advance to Block 4 because the pages were read once.

## Minimum Day

Use this on busy days:

- 5 minutes due reviews
- 5 minutes current Block 3 vocabulary or kanji page
- 1-3 Block 3 local clips from [[Phase 3 Local Audio Practice]]
- 1 sentence from the named authentic segment
- 1 spoken practical-domain sentence from memory

This preserves continuity. It does not replace the weekly proof.

## What Not To Do

- Do not treat practical vocabulary as silent list memorization.
- Do not trust local TTS for onomatopoeia nuance, kanji readings, or connected-speech rhythm without comparing it to the authentic model.
- Do not add new kanji before checking how at least one current kanji appears in spoken vocabulary.
- Do not let travel, health, or date/time pages replace the named practical audio segment.
- Do not advance to Block 4 without [[Phase 3 Weekly Review]] evidence.

## Month-Six Success Criteria

Month six counts when all of these are true:

- [[Phase 3 Weekly Review]] has evidence for the Block 3 month;
- [[Authentic Audio Evidence Log]] names the exact Block 3 authentic segment or segments;
- Block 3 local clips from [[Phase 3 Local Audio Practice]] were used on at least three days per week;
- at least one rhythm, date/time, onomatopoeia, kanji-reading, or suspect-clip issue was checked each week;
- suspect local clips, if any, were recorded in [[Pronunciation Correction Log]];
- one travel exchange can be role-played without reading;
- one symptom, one request, and one date/time sentence can be said aloud;
- two onomatopoeia can be used in context;
- one N4 kanji reading has been checked through [[Pronunciation and Audio Accuracy]];
- one practical segment can be summarized or role-played using five practical-domain words.

## Next Month

After these success criteria are true, continue with [[Phase 3 Block 4 Japanese Study Plan]]. It wraps Block 4 in [[Phase 3 Audio Assignment Ladder]] and moves into pitch-accent checks, filler/discourse timing, intermediate listening, media rhythm, and register recognition.

Before starting Block 4, open [[Phase 3 Pitch Accent Practice Path]] and choose the exact intermediate or native segment plus the pitch/accent reference route that will anchor the month.
