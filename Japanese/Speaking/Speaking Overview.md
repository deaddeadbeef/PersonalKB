# Speaking Overview

Speaking Japanese requires mastering pronunciation, pitch accent, appropriate formality, and conversation flow.

## Core Skills

### Pronunciation
- [[Pronunciation — Difficult Sounds for English Speakers]]
- Japanese has only 5 vowels: a, i, u, e, o (pure vowels, no diphthongs)
- The れ (r) sound is between English l and r ![[speakov-001-re.mp3]]
- Long vowels and double consonants change meaning

### Pitch Accent
- [[Pitch Accent — Introduction]] — what it is and why it matters
- [[Pitch Accent — Common Patterns]] — the four accent patterns
- Japanese is NOT a stress language — it uses pitch (high/low) patterns
- Wrong pitch = foreign accent or wrong word entirely

### Fluency Tools
- [[Common Filler Words and Discourse Markers]]
- Aizuchi (相槌) — back-channel responses (うん, そうですね, なるほど) ![[speakov-002-aizuchi.mp3]]
- These are ESSENTIAL — silence in Japanese conversation is uncomfortable

## Formality Levels

| Level | When | Example | Audio |
|-------|------|---------|-------|
| Casual | Friends, family | 食べる? (taberu?) | ![[speakov-003-taberu.mp3]] |
| Polite (です/ます) | Default with strangers | 食べますか? (tabemasu ka?) | ![[speakov-004-tabemasu-ka.mp3]] |
| Formal (keigo) | Business, service | お召し上がりになりますか? | ![[speakov-005-omeshiagarini-narimasu-ka.mp3]] |

See: [[Culture Overview]] and [[Keigo — Overview and Register System]]

## Speaking Practice Methods
1. **Shadowing** — repeat immediately after native audio ([[Shadowing — Technique and Practice Guide]])
2. **Language exchange** — HelloTalk, Tandem, iTalki
3. **Self-narration** — describe your day in Japanese (alone)
4. **Role-play** — practice situational dialogues
