# Phase 4 Keigo and Register Production Checklist

> Use this during [[Phase 4 — Intermediate Mastery]] when you need to produce keigo, business Japanese, or other register-sensitive output. Local clips are drills. A tutor, native speaker, course dialogue, workplace/service recording, or other human model is the authority.

## Source Rule

Use sources in this order:

| Need | Use first | Use local clips for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social direction | Tutor/native feedback, course role-play, or a clear service/workplace model | [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3 after the role is known |
| Sonkeigo form | [[Keigo — Sonkeigo (Honorific)]] plus a human/register model | [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3 for form recall and mouth practice |
| Kenjōgo form | [[Keigo — Kenjōgo (Humble)]] plus a human/register model | [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3 for form recall and mouth practice |
| Business phrase rhythm | Workplace/service audio, course dialogue, or tutor role-play | [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3 for phrase repetition inside Obsidian |
| Uncertain register | [[Pronunciation and Audio Accuracy]] and [[Phase 4 Authentic Audio Spine]] | Nothing until the model is checked |

Do not produce keigo freely from a local TTS clip alone.

## Production Checklist

Use this before recording, role-playing, or sending a register-sensitive sentence.

| Check | Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role | Who is in-group, out-group, superior, customer, colleague, or stranger? |  |
| Direction | Am I elevating the other person, lowering myself, or just being polite? |  |
| Form | Is this sonkeigo, kenjōgo, teineigo, or business formula language? |  |
| Human model | What native/course/tutor segment did I compare against? |  |
| Local drill | Which page clip helped me repeat the form after the source check? |  |
| Output | What sentence, role-play, phone line, meeting line, or email sentence did I produce? |  |
| Feedback | What correction or uncertainty goes into [[Phase 4 Weekly Review]]? |  |

## Ten-Minute Role-Play Loop

Use this with [[Phase 4 Authentic Audio Spine]], [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]], and [[Phase 4 Audio Coverage Map]].

1. Choose one situation: visitor reception, client phone call, meeting opening, request, apology, follow-up email, or reporting your boss's words to a client.
2. Choose the social direction before choosing the verb.
3. Listen to one human or official-course model for the situation.
4. Drill one local clip from [[Phase 4 Local Audio Practice]] Block 3, then expand into [[Keigo — Sonkeigo (Honorific)]], [[Keigo — Kenjōgo (Humble)]], or [[Business Japanese — Workplace Communication]] if needed.
5. Record one 20-60 second role-play or write one short email sentence.
6. Compare it against the model or ask for tutor/native feedback.
7. Log the result in [[Phase 4 Weekly Review]].

## Common Stop Signals

Stop and check a model when:

- You are unsure whether to elevate the other person or lower yourself.
- You are talking about your boss to someone outside your company.
- The phrase is for a customer, interview, apology, or formal request.
- A local clip sounds natural but the social role is unclear.
- You can say the form but cannot explain why it fits the situation.

Clip-quality problems go to [[Pronunciation Correction Log]]. Normal learner corrections go to [[Phase 4 Weekly Review]].

## Practice Targets

| Target | Good source | Local companion | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Client phone greeting | Tutor/native role-play or business course audio | [[Business Japanese — Workplace Communication]] | Say the first 20 seconds smoothly |

| Asking a customer to wait | Service audio or tutor model | [[Business Japanese — Workplace Communication]] | Use the phrase without sounding casual |
| Describing a superior's action | Course/tutor example | [[Keigo — Sonkeigo (Honorific)]] | Elevate the superior, not yourself |
