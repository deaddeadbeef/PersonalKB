# Chunk NES 119 — NES Cartridge as Extension Architecture

The NES cartridge connector exposed raw CPU and PPU address and data bus lines, enabling cartridge-mounted hardware to intercept and extend both processors. This revolutionary design meant the console could grow in capability without hardware revisions. Early games used simple NROM with no additional hardware. Later cartridges added bank switching logic (UxROM, MMC1), scanline counting IRQ generators (MMC3), extra audio channels (VRC6, VRC7, N163), additional RAM, and even coprocessors. This extensibility gave the NES a 12-year commercial lifespan with late-era games vastly exceeding early titles in complexity.
