## Summary

SpaceX's Starship/Super Heavy launch system has undergone a rapid series of Integrated Flight Tests (IFTs) beginning in April 2023, embodying the company's iterative "test, fail, learn, fly again" development philosophy. Rather than pursuing traditional aerospace certification-before-flight approaches, SpaceX treats each flight as a data-collection opportunity, accepting vehicle loss as an acceptable cost of accelerating the learning curve. The program has progressed from a catastrophic first launch to the historic first-ever tower catch of a Super Heavy booster within roughly 18 months—an unprecedented pace for a vehicle of this scale.

## Key Claims

- IFT-1 (April 20, 2023) ended when the Flight Termination System was activated at approximately T+4 minutes after the vehicle failed to achieve stage separation; multiple Raptor engines were lost during ascent and the launch pad suffered significant damage.
- IFT-2 (November 18, 2023) successfully demonstrated hot-stage separation for the first time, but the Super Heavy booster experienced a rapid unscheduled disassembly (RUD) during boostback, and the Ship reached space before being lost near its planned engine relight.
- IFT-3 (March 14, 2024) achieved Ship coast through space and a booster splashdown attempt in the Gulf of Mexico, though the Ship was lost during atmospheric reentry due to heat shield and attitude control issues.
- IFT-4 (June 6, 2024) marked the first flight where both stages completed controlled splashdowns—Super Heavy in the Gulf and Ship in the Indian Ocean—validating guidance, navigation, and Raptor relight sequences.
- IFT-5 (October 13, 2024) achieved the first-ever tower catch of the Super Heavy booster using the launch tower's "chopstick" mechanical arms, a critical milestone for the rapid reuse architecture.

## Atomic Facts

1. The Starship IFT campaign launched five major test flights between April 2023 and October 2024, with IFT-6 and subsequent flights planned for 2025.
2. Hot-stage separation, first attempted on IFT-2, involves igniting the Ship's Raptor engines before physical separation from the booster, improving payload performance by approximately 10%.
3. The IFT-1 launch caused substantial damage to the Orbital Launch Mount at Starbase, leading SpaceX to install a steel-plate water-deluge flame deflector system before subsequent flights.
4. IFT-3 was the first flight to attempt a controlled booster splashdown targeting a specific area in the Gulf of Mexico, collecting data later used for the IFT-5 tower catch.
5. Each IFT has carried instrumentation and telemetry packages rather than operational payloads, maximizing engineering data return per flight.
6. The FAA licensing process for each IFT required environmental reviews and mishap investigations from prior flights, creating schedule pressure that SpaceX has publicly criticized.

## Significance

The Starship IFT campaign represents the most aggressive iterative flight-test program in the history of super-heavy-lift launch vehicles. Each flight built directly on lessons from the previous one, compressing a development timeline that would traditionally span a decade into under two years. The successful tower catch on IFT-5 validated the core reusability architecture that underpins SpaceX's cost targets for Starship operations.
