# Reusability Economics Analysis

## Summary

The Falcon 9 first stage constitutes approximately 60-70% of total vehicle cost (~$40-45M of $67M new-build price). Recovering and reflying it transforms the economics from expendable to an airline-like model where the capital asset amortizes across many flights. Marginal reflight cost is estimated at $15-20M (refurbishment, consumables, propellant, second stage, fairing, range fees), creating ~$47-52M gross margin per commercial flight against the $67M published price. Internal Starlink launches benefit further since no commercial margin is needed — marginal cost may be as low as $15-20M per mission. The Starlink constellation (6,000+ satellites requiring continuous replenishment) would be economically unviable at expendable pricing; reusability is the enabling condition for the entire business case. The airline analogy is structurally accurate: a Boeing 737 ($100M) flies ~30,000 flights, amortizing capital to ~$3,300/flight; a Falcon 9 booster ($40-45M) flying 20+ times amortizes to ~$2M/flight. At 90+ missions per year with predominantly reused boosters, SpaceX's annual savings relative to expendable operations are estimated at $3-4 billion.

## Key Claims

- The first stage represents 60-70% of vehicle cost, making its recovery the highest-leverage reusability target
- Marginal reflight cost of $15-20M vs $67M new creates ~$47-52M gross margin per commercial flight
- Starlink's business case collapses at expendable pricing — reusable launch is the enabling condition
- The airline analogy is structurally accurate: capital asset amortization across many flights transforms unit economics
- Cumulative savings from booster and fairing reuse since 2017 likely total multiple billions of dollars

## Atomic Facts

1. The Falcon 9 first stage (nine Merlin engines, tankage, avionics, landing hardware) constitutes 60-70% of total vehicle cost, estimated at $40-45M
2. Marginal reflight cost is estimated at $15-20M per mission covering refurbishment, consumables, propellant, second stage, fairing, and range fees
3. SpaceX's published Falcon 9 launch price is $67M, though actual contract prices vary by mission complexity, orbit, and customer
4. Starlink requires continuous replenishment of 6,000+ satellites; at expendable pricing (~$67M per 60 satellites), launch cost alone would exceed revenue potential
5. A Boeing 737 ($100M) amortizes capital to ~$3,300/flight over ~30,000 flights; a Falcon 9 booster flying 20+ times amortizes to ~$2M/flight
6. At 90+ missions per year with predominantly reused boosters, annual savings relative to expendable operations are estimated at $3-4 billion

## Significance

Reusability economics drive SpaceX's competitive dominance and enable projects (Starlink, Starship) that would otherwise be financially impossible. The structural cost advantage over expendable competitors is difficult to replicate without a comparable reuse capability, reshaping the entire launch industry's competitive landscape.
