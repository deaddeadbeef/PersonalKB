## Summary

NASA's Human Landing System (HLS) program selected SpaceX's Starship variant as the crewed lunar lander for the Artemis campaign, awarding the initial Option A contract valued at $2.89 billion in April 2021. The selection was immediately controversial, as NASA chose a single provider rather than the expected two, prompting formal protests from Blue Origin and Dynetics that were ultimately denied by the Government Accountability Office. The Starship HLS variant represents a significant departure from traditional lunar lander designs, leveraging the Starship platform's massive payload capacity while requiring substantial modifications for the lunar surface environment. Subsequent contract extensions have expanded the program scope to include sustainment missions beyond Artemis III.

## Key Claims

- NASA awarded the HLS Option A contract to SpaceX on April 16, 2021, for $2.89 billion—significantly less than competing bids from Blue Origin's National Team (~$5.99B) and Dynetics (~$8.5B in the evaluated cost range).
- Blue Origin and Dynetics filed formal protests with the GAO, arguing NASA's single-selection approach deviated from the original solicitation's intent; the GAO denied both protests in July 2021.
- The Starship HLS variant omits the standard thermal protection system since it will not perform Earth atmospheric reentry, instead featuring high-thrust RCS landing thrusters positioned high on the vehicle to minimize lunar regolith disruption.
- The HLS design includes a crew elevator system to transport astronauts from the pressurized cabin to the lunar surface (approximately 50 meters below the crew compartment) and a cargo airlock for deploying equipment.
- An uncrewed demonstration landing on the lunar surface is required before NASA will authorize the crewed Artemis III mission, serving as a critical risk-reduction milestone.

## Atomic Facts

1. The HLS Option A contract covered development, an uncrewed lunar landing demonstration, and one crewed landing mission (Artemis III) with two astronauts.
2. The Option B sustainment contract, awarded in 2023, extends SpaceX's HLS role to additional Artemis missions beyond the initial demonstration, with a contract value of approximately $1.15 billion.
3. The Starship HLS variant must be compatible with NASA's Gateway lunar orbital station for crew transfer operations on later Artemis missions, though Artemis III uses a direct Orion-to-HLS docking approach.
4. Landing thrusters on HLS are positioned well above the base of the vehicle to prevent high-velocity exhaust from excavating lunar regolith into ballistic trajectories that could damage hardware or obscure visibility.
5. The crew elevator concept addresses the unprecedented height of the Starship HLS—the tallest lunar lander ever designed—requiring a mechanical system to safely lower suited astronauts to the surface.
6. NASA's source selection statement cited SpaceX's technical approach, lower price, and higher self-funding commitment as key discriminators in the competitive evaluation.

## Significance

The HLS contract represents NASA's largest single investment in commercial lunar landing capability and ties the Artemis program's crewed surface operations directly to Starship's development success. The selection established SpaceX as the sole provider of crewed lunar landing services for the near-term Artemis campaign, making Starship's technical maturation a pacing item for American return-to-the-Moon objectives.
