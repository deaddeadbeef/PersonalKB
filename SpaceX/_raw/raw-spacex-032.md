## Summary

SpaceX's founding traces to Elon Musk's post-PayPal ambitions. After eBay acquired PayPal in October 2002, Musk received approximately $165 million from his 11.7% stake. His initial concept, "Mars Oasis," envisioned sending a greenhouse to Mars to reignite public interest in space. To find an affordable launcher, Musk made trips to Russia in 2001–2002 to buy refurbished ICBMs, but sellers repeatedly raised prices. On the flight home, Musk's spreadsheet showed rocket raw materials were ~3% of launch price, confirming room for disruption. SpaceX was incorporated May 6, 2002, and officially founded June 28, 2002, with $100 million of Musk's personal funds. The first headquarters was a warehouse at 1310 East Grand Avenue in El Segundo, California. The critical early hire was Tom Mueller, a TRW propulsion engineer who had tested a 13,000 lbf LOX/kerosene engine as a hobby. Mueller became VP of Propulsion and architect of the Merlin engine family. The initial team of ~12 included Chris Thompson, Hans Koenigsmann, and other TRW and Boeing veterans.

## Key Claims

- Musk received ~$165 million from the eBay/PayPal acquisition, providing SpaceX's personal capital base.
- Mars Oasis — a greenhouse on Mars — was Musk's original plan before he pivoted to building rockets.
- Two Russia trips (2001–2002) to buy ICBMs failed due to escalating prices, catalyzing the build-in-house decision.
- SpaceX incorporated May 2002, founded June 28, 2002, with $100 million personal commitment.
- Tom Mueller (TRW) was the foundational propulsion hire who designed the Merlin engine family.

## Atomic Facts

1. Musk's PayPal ownership stake was approximately 11.7% at the time of the eBay acquisition in October 2002, yielding roughly $165 million pre-tax.
2. Mars Oasis aimed to land a small greenhouse on Mars carrying seeds in dehydrated nutrient gel, producing the first photo of plant life on another planet.
3. During the Russia trips, Musk met with NPO Lavochkin and Kosmotras representatives; accounts describe dismissive treatment that reinforced his decision to build in-house.
4. The El Segundo warehouse at 1310 East Grand Avenue was ~75,000 sq ft — cramped but affordable and close to aerospace suppliers.
5. Tom Mueller had built and hot-fired a 13,000 lbf thrust LOX/kerosene engine as part of the Reaction Research Society amateur rocketry group before joining SpaceX.
6. The initial core team of approximately 12 engineers included Chris Thompson (structures), Hans Koenigsmann (avionics), and several recruits from TRW, Boeing, and Northrop Grumman's aerospace divisions.

## Significance

The founding story crystallizes the thesis that aerospace pricing reflected institutional inertia, not physical cost limits. Musk's spreadsheet became the intellectual foundation for vertical integration, Mueller's hire established propulsion as the core competency, and the El Segundo culture set the template that scaled to Falcon 9, Dragon, and Starship.
