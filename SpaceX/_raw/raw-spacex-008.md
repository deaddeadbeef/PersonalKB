# Crew Dragon Design and Certification

## Summary
This source documents the design features and NASA certification process for SpaceX's Crew Dragon spacecraft under the Commercial Crew Transportation Capability (CCtCap) contract. It covers the abort system design, cockpit interface, life support, and the critical demonstration missions that validated the vehicle for operational crew flights. Crew Dragon's certification restored independent U.S. crew access to the ISS for the first time since the Space Shuttle retired in 2011.

## Key Claims
- Crew Dragon's integrated launch escape system uses 8 SuperDraco engines embedded in the capsule walls, capable of activating at any point from pad to orbit
- The all-glass touchscreen cockpit replaced traditional mechanical switches and dials, representing a fundamental departure from prior crewed spacecraft interface design
- NASA's CCtCap requirements mandated a loss-of-crew probability no greater than 1 in 270 over the mission lifecycle
- Three demonstration missions—DM-1, in-flight abort, and DM-2—were required to validate vehicle safety before operational certification
- The Mark 3 parachute system uses four main chutes with a redundancy design allowing safe recovery even with one parachute failure

## Atomic Facts
1. Each SuperDraco engine produces approximately 73 kN (16,400 lbf) of thrust, giving the abort system a combined 584 kN to pull the capsule away from a failing rocket in under 8 seconds
2. DM-1 (Demo Mission 1) launched uncrewed on March 2, 2019, successfully docking with the ISS autonomously and returning via splashdown in the Atlantic
3. The in-flight abort test was conducted on January 19, 2020, demonstrating capsule separation and crew survival during the period of maximum aerodynamic pressure (Max-Q)
4. DM-2 launched on May 30, 2020, carrying NASA astronauts Doug Hurley and Bob Behnken to the ISS—the first crewed orbital launch from U.S. soil since STS-135 in July 2011
5. The Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) maintains cabin pressure at 14.7 psia with a standard nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere, supporting up to 7 crew for up to 210 hours of free flight
6. The Mark 3 parachute system deploys in two stages: two drogue chutes stabilize the capsule at supersonic speeds, followed by four main chutes that slow descent to approximately 7.6 m/s at splashdown

## Significance
Crew Dragon's certification was a watershed moment for commercial human spaceflight. It validated NASA's strategy of purchasing crew transportation services from private companies rather than owning and operating vehicles directly, fundamentally reshaping the institutional structure of human space access.
