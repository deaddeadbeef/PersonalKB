# Full-Flow Staged Combustion Cycle

## Summary
This source examines the thermodynamic principles, engineering challenges, and historical context of the full-flow staged combustion (FFSC) rocket engine cycle as implemented in SpaceX's Raptor engine. It explains why the cycle offers theoretical performance advantages over gas generator and conventional staged combustion designs, and why it remained unflown for decades until Raptor. The paper provides essential context for understanding Raptor's significance in propulsion engineering.

## Key Claims
- Full-flow staged combustion gasifies both propellants before they enter the main combustion chamber, enabling higher chamber pressures and more complete combustion than any other cycle
- The use of two separate preburners—one oxidizer-rich, one fuel-rich—means no turbine is exposed to the extreme conditions of the opposite propellant's hot gas, reducing turbine thermal stress
- Unlike gas generator cycles, FFSC produces no overboard turbine exhaust dump, meaning all propellant mass contributes to thrust generation and specific impulse
- The Soviet Union's RD-270 engine (1960s) was the first serious FFSC development program but was cancelled without ever reaching flight status
- SpaceX succeeded where prior programs failed primarily through modern computational fluid dynamics, additive manufacturing, and an iterative test campaign unconstrained by traditional aerospace program management

## Atomic Facts
1. In a gas generator cycle (e.g., Merlin), approximately 2-3% of propellant is dumped overboard after driving the turbines, representing a direct specific impulse loss
2. The RS-25 Space Shuttle Main Engine uses oxygen-rich staged combustion but only gasifies the hydrogen side, making it a staged combustion (not full-flow) design with ~206 bar chamber pressure
3. The Soviet RD-270 was designed for the UR-700 heavy-lift vehicle, targeting ~260 bar chamber pressure with N2O4/UDMH propellants, but suffered from combustion instability and was cancelled around 1970
4. Raptor's oxidizer-rich preburner operates at a relatively mild temperature (~700K) because excess oxygen keeps combustion temperatures low, reducing turbine blade erosion
5. The fuel-rich preburner similarly operates at manageable temperatures (~800K), with methane-rich exhaust that is far less corrosive to turbine components than oxygen-rich gas
6. Full gasification of both propellants before injection into the main chamber eliminates injector-face erosion caused by impinging liquid streams, a common failure mode in high-pressure engines

## Significance
The FFSC cycle represents the theoretical apex of chemical bipropellant rocket engine performance. Raptor's successful implementation of this cycle—after decades of failed attempts by other programs—is one of SpaceX's most important technical achievements and underpins the entire Starship architecture's viability.
