## Summary

SpaceX has accumulated a remarkable series of aerospace firsts since Falcon 1 Flight 4 in 2008. Falcon 9 surpassed 300 launches, and the booster recovery program proved first-stage reuse is economically transformative. The 2020s saw dramatic acceleration — 134 launches in 2024 alone, more than any single entity in spaceflight history. Each milestone compounds: the first landing enabled the first reflight, which enabled routine reuse, which enabled the cadence now dominating global orbital access. Starship added its own first with the booster catch by launch tower arms in October 2024.

## Key Claims

- SpaceX achieved the first privately funded liquid-fueled rocket to reach Earth orbit with Falcon 1 Flight 4 on September 28, 2008.
- Dragon became the first commercial spacecraft to dock with the International Space Station on May 25, 2012, during the COTS 2 demonstration mission.
- The first successful orbital-class booster landing occurred on December 21, 2015, when Falcon 9 B1019 returned to Landing Zone 1 at Cape Canaveral after the Orbcomm OG-2 mission.
- Falcon 9 achieved 134 launches in calendar year 2024, setting the record for most orbital launches by a single vehicle type in one year.
- The first successful Starship Super Heavy booster catch by the Mechazilla chopstick arms on the launch tower occurred during IFT-5 on October 13, 2024.

## Atomic Facts

1. First private liquid-fueled rocket to orbit: Falcon 1 Flight 4, September 28, 2008, from Kwajalein Atoll.
2. First private spacecraft to dock with ISS: Dragon C2+ (COTS 2), May 25, 2012, demonstrating autonomous rendezvous and berthing capabilities.
3. First orbital-class booster landing: Falcon 9 B1019, December 21, 2015, Landing Zone 1, Cape Canaveral, after Orbcomm OG-2 mission deployment.
4. First reflight of an orbital-class booster: Falcon 9 B1021, March 30, 2017, launching SES-10 to GTO on a previously-flown booster from the CRS-8 mission.
5. First commercial crew to ISS: Crew Dragon Demo-2, May 30, 2020, carrying NASA astronauts Doug Hurley and Bob Behnken, restoring American crewed launch capability after nine years.
6. 100th successful Falcon 9 booster landing achieved in 2021; fastest booster turnaround reached approximately 21 days between flights; the Super Heavy booster catch during IFT-5 (October 13, 2024) marked the first time a launch vehicle first stage was caught by tower-mounted mechanical arms.

## Significance

Reusability proved to be not just cost reduction but a cadence enabler transforming space economics. The 134-launch year means SpaceX alone accounted for the majority of global orbital launches — unprecedented since Cold War programs. Each record compounds the next: faster turnarounds enable more flights, generating revenue that funds Starship development.
