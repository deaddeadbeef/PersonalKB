# Starship Users Guide

## Summary
The Starship Users Guide is SpaceX's official payload integration document for the fully reusable Starship launch system. It describes the two-stage vehicle architecture—Super Heavy booster and Starship upper stage—along with payload volume, mass capabilities, and both cargo and crew configurations. The guide establishes Starship as a vehicle class capable of delivering unprecedented payload mass and volume to orbit.

## Key Claims
- Starship is designed to deliver 100-150 tonnes to low Earth orbit in fully reusable configuration, far exceeding any existing launch system
- The vehicle's 9-meter outer diameter provides a payload volume of approximately 1,000 cubic meters, the largest of any rocket ever built
- Stainless steel 304L alloy was selected for the airframe due to its favorable strength-to-weight ratio at cryogenic and re-entry temperatures, low cost, and ease of fabrication
- Starship supports both cargo and crew configurations, with the crew variant designed for up to 100 passengers on interplanetary missions
- On-orbit propellant transfer between Starship vehicles enables deep-space missions including lunar landing and Mars transit

## Atomic Facts
1. The full Starship stack stands approximately 121 meters (397 feet) tall, making it the tallest rocket ever built
2. Super Heavy booster is powered by 33 Raptor engines, while the Starship upper stage uses 6 Raptors (3 sea-level + 3 vacuum-optimized)
3. Total vehicle diameter is 9 meters (approximately 30 feet), consistent across both booster and ship stages
4. The payload bay door is a large clamshell design enabling deployment of satellites up to 8 meters in diameter
5. Starship uses an active heat shield system with approximately 18,000 ceramic hexagonal tiles on the windward side for atmospheric re-entry
6. Total propellant capacity for the full stack is approximately 4,600 tonnes of subcooled liquid oxygen and liquid methane

## Significance
The Starship Users Guide defines the technical parameters of what may become the most transformative launch vehicle in spaceflight history. Its combination of full reusability, massive payload capacity, and low projected per-flight cost could reduce launch costs by an order of magnitude and enable permanent human presence beyond Earth.
