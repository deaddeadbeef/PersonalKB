# Starbase Boca Chica Development

## Summary

Starbase evolved from a modest Falcon 9 launch site to the primary Starship development and launch complex. SpaceX obtained FAA launch site operator licenses for Boca Chica in 2014, originally for Falcon 9/Heavy commercial launches. The South Texas location at 25.99°N — the southernmost U.S. continental launch site — offers favorable inclination access, over-water eastward trajectories without overflying populated areas, and lower land costs. SpaceX pivoted the site to Starship development circa 2018. Facilities evolved from open-air fabrication tents to the Starfactory, a large enclosed manufacturing building for high-rate Starship and Super Heavy production. The integration tower (Mechazilla, 146 m) is equipped with mechanical arms ("chopsticks") for catching returning boosters and ships mid-air, eliminating landing legs and their mass penalty — first demonstrated catching a Super Heavy booster in October 2024. The FAA completed a Programmatic Environmental Assessment in June 2022, issuing a Mitigated FONSI with 75+ mitigation measures addressing wildlife, debris, noise, and water quality. The site's proximity to the Lower Rio Grande Valley National Wildlife Refuge creates ongoing environmental tension, particularly regarding nesting shorebirds. SpaceX purchased most Boca Chica Village properties; remaining residents experience road closures during operations.

## Key Claims

- Originally permitted for Falcon 9 (2014), Starbase pivoted to Starship development circa 2018, fundamentally changing site scope
- Location at 25.99°N provides latitude and over-water trajectory advantages with minimal populated-area overflight
- The Starfactory represents a transition from ad-hoc fabrication to industrial-scale Starship manufacturing
- Mechazilla's catching arms eliminate landing legs mass penalty — first caught a Super Heavy booster in October 2024
- The FAA's 2022 PEA imposed 75+ environmental mitigation measures reflecting launch/wildlife refuge tension

## Atomic Facts

1. SpaceX obtained FAA launch site operator licenses for Boca Chica in 2014, originally for Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy commercial launches
2. At 25.99°N, Starbase is closer to the equator than Cape Canaveral (28.5°N), with over-water eastward trajectories avoiding populated areas
3. The Starfactory is a large enclosed manufacturing facility replacing the earlier open-air fabrication approach for high-rate Starship production
4. Mechazilla is a 146 m steel tower with mechanical arms first successfully catching a Super Heavy booster in October 2024
5. The FAA Programmatic EA (June 2022) issued a Mitigated FONSI allowing launches subject to 75+ mitigation measures
6. Starbase is adjacent to the Lower Rio Grande Valley National Wildlife Refuge; operations require U.S. Fish and Wildlife coordination regarding piping plovers and nesting shorebirds

## Significance

Starbase is an unprecedented experiment in private launch site development — full-scale orbital launch complex and manufacturing facility built from scratch on private land. Its evolution from Falcon 9 pad concept to the world's largest rocket production facility illustrates SpaceX's execution speed and the regulatory/environmental complexities constraining operations.
