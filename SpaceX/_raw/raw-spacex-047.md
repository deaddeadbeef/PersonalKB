## Summary

SpaceX has distinguished itself from traditional aerospace companies through an aggressive vertical integration strategy, controlling over 80% of its component manufacturing in-house. This approach, combined with continuous investment in advanced manufacturing technologies, has enabled SpaceX to achieve production rates, cost structures, and iteration speeds unprecedented in the launch industry. From friction stir welding on Falcon 9 tank sections to 3D-printed Raptor engine components, SpaceX treats manufacturing process innovation as a first-class engineering discipline. The construction of "Starfactory" at Boca Chica, Texas, represents the latest escalation of this philosophy, targeting production rates of one complete Starship per week.

## Key Claims

- SpaceX maintains over 80% vertical integration, manufacturing the majority of avionics, engines, structures, and ground support equipment internally—a stark contrast to traditional aerospace primes like Boeing or Lockheed Martin, which rely heavily on subcontractor supply chains.
- Friction stir welding (FSW), a solid-state joining process that produces stronger and more consistent welds than traditional fusion welding, is used extensively in Falcon 9 aluminum-lithium alloy tank manufacturing at the Hawthorne, California headquarters.
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing) is used for critical engine components, including the SuperDraco Inconel combustion chambers on Dragon (printed as a single piece) and various Raptor engine components, reducing part counts and lead times.
- Starship's stainless steel construction enables a simplified fabrication approach: steel sheets are roll-formed into cylindrical sections and welded using automated submerged arc or MIG welding processes, far less complex than carbon composite layup.
- Starfactory, the expanded manufacturing facility at Boca Chica, Texas, is designed to achieve a production target of one complete Starship upper stage per week, with parallel Super Heavy booster production lines.

## Atomic Facts

1. Hawthorne, California serves as SpaceX's primary manufacturing headquarters, housing Falcon 9 and Dragon production lines, Raptor engine assembly, and avionics fabrication across approximately one million square feet of factory floor space.
2. Friction stir welding uses a rotating tool to plastically deform and join metal without melting it, producing welds with superior fatigue life and fewer defects compared to conventional TIG or MIG welding—critical for pressure vessel applications.
3. The SuperDraco engine's Inconel combustion chamber was one of the first flight-qualified, 3D-printed rocket engine chambers, demonstrating that additive manufacturing could meet the stringent quality and performance requirements of human-rated propulsion systems.
4. Raptor engine production rate targets have been publicly stated as one engine per day, a cadence necessary to support the envisioned Starship flight rate of multiple launches per week.
5. Starship's roll-form-weld fabrication of 304L stainless steel can be performed with standard industrial metalworking equipment, dramatically lowering the capital cost of tooling compared to carbon fiber autoclaves or aluminum-lithium friction stir welding fixtures.
6. Traditional aerospace outsourcing models can involve thousands of subcontractors across dozens of countries, introducing supply chain fragility, quality control variability, and communication overhead that SpaceX's vertical integration model explicitly avoids.

## Significance

SpaceX's manufacturing strategy is inseparable from its competitive advantage. By controlling the production process end-to-end, the company can iterate designs rapidly, control quality directly, and maintain cost discipline that would be impossible in a disaggregated supply chain. Starfactory represents the industrialization of spaceflight hardware at a scale never previously attempted, and its success or failure will determine whether Starship achieves the flight rates necessary for orbital refueling, Starlink deployment, and Mars missions.
