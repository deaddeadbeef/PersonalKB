# Booster Recovery and Reflight History

## Summary

SpaceX's booster reuse program evolved from a single proof-of-concept to routine industrial practice. The first reflight occurred on March 30, 2017, when B1021 — previously flown on CRS-8 — launched SES-10, proving orbital-class boosters could be reflown successfully. By 2024, new boosters are the exception, manufactured only to replace retiring cores or expand the fleet. Fleet leaders B1058, B1060, and B1061 have each exceeded 20 flights, far surpassing the original 10-flight Block 5 design target. Turnaround time compressed from over 12 months for the first reflight to under 21 days in the fastest cases. The refurbishment process includes post-flight inspection, engine and structural checks, consumable replacement, and a static fire before clearance. Block 5's design-for-reuse features — durable TPS, improved turbopump coatings, bolted landing legs — reduced refurbishment scope. Customer acceptance shifted decisively: early customers demanded new boosters or discounts, but by 2022 NASA crew missions flew on previously-flown hardware, and flight heritage became a positive reliability indicator. Retirement criteria involve structural fatigue assessments, engine performance trends, and flight count relative to the qualification envelope.

## Key Claims

- B1021's SES-10 reflight (March 2017) was the first reuse of an orbital-class liquid-fueled booster, validating the business case
- Fleet leaders B1058, B1060, and B1061 each surpassed 20 flights, demonstrating Block 5 durability beyond initial design targets
- Turnaround time compressed from 12+ months to under 21 days in the fastest cases
- Customer perception shifted from discount-requiring risk to flight-heritage-positive asset by approximately 2021-2022
- Block 5 design-for-reuse features reduced per-flight refurbishment scope and cost significantly

## Atomic Facts

1. B1021 first reflew on SES-10 (March 2017) after originally flying CRS-8 in April 2016, a turnaround of approximately 11 months
2. B1058 first flew on Demo-2 (May 2020), SpaceX's inaugural crewed ISS mission, then exceeded 20 flights primarily on Starlink missions
3. B1060 entered service on a GPS III mission (June 2020) and became one of the highest-flight-count boosters alongside B1058 and B1061
4. Block 5 (first flown May 2018) incorporated thermal protection improvements, fatigue-resistant engine components, and bolted landing legs for rapid reuse
5. The fastest booster turnaround achieved was approximately 21 days between flights
6. By Crew-4 (April 2022), NASA approved previously-flown boosters for crew rotation missions, signaling full institutional acceptance

## Significance

The progression from one tentative reflight to a fleet of 20+ flight boosters represents a goal that eluded the Shuttle program and every prior launch vehicle. Operational normalization of reuse has driven SpaceX's cadence past 90 missions per year and made new-booster flights the exception in the global launch market.
