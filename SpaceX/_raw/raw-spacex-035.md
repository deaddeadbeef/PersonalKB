## Summary

SpaceX's commercial launch business grew from a tentative GTO foothold to global dominance in roughly a decade. SES-8 (December 2013) was the first GTO delivery. Orbcomm OG-2 (December 2015) combined commercial deployment with the first booster landing. The Iridium NEXT campaign (2017–2019) demonstrated constellation-scale reliability across 10 launches and 75 satellites. Increasingly massive payloads followed: Turksat 5B, Hughes Jupiter 3 (~9,200 kg, among the heaviest commercial GEO sats), and Eutelsat HOTBIRD replacements. BulgariaSat-1 (June 2017) broke the reusability barrier as the first commercial customer on a flight-proven booster (B1029). By 2024, SpaceX held over 60% of the global commercial launch market by revenue.

## Key Claims

- SES-8 (December 3, 2013) was SpaceX's first geostationary transfer orbit mission, demonstrating Falcon 9's capability to serve the high-value commercial communications satellite market.
- The Orbcomm OG-2 mission (December 21, 2015) achieved the first-ever landing of an orbital-class rocket booster while simultaneously completing a commercial satellite deployment.
- The Iridium NEXT campaign comprised 10 Falcon 9 launches between January 2017 and January 2019, deploying all 75 operational satellites for the Iridium constellation replacement.
- BulgariaSat-1 (June 23, 2017) was the first commercial mission to fly on a previously-flown Falcon 9 first stage, breaking the reusability acceptance barrier for commercial customers.
- Hughes Jupiter 3, at approximately 9,200 kg, represented one of the heaviest commercial geostationary communications satellites ever launched, demonstrating Falcon 9's performance envelope for high-mass GEO missions.

## Atomic Facts

1. SES-8 launched December 3, 2013, from SLC-40 at Cape Canaveral, delivering the 3,170 kg SES-8 communications satellite to a supersynchronous transfer orbit for SES of Luxembourg.
2. Orbcomm OG-2 launched December 21, 2015, deploying 11 OG-2 satellites to ~620 km LEO; Falcon 9 B1019 landed at LZ-1, marking the first successful orbital booster recovery.
3. Iridium NEXT: 10 launches, 75 satellites total (plus spares), all from Vandenberg SLC-4E; the campaign demonstrated cadence reliability with a mean interval of approximately 10 weeks between launches.
4. BulgariaSat-1 flew on booster B1029.2 (previously Iridium-1), making Bulgaria Sat the first commercial operator to accept a flight-proven Falcon 9 booster.
5. Hughes Jupiter 3 launched on July 28, 2023, via Falcon Heavy from LC-39A; at ~9,200 kg it was among the heaviest commercial GEO payloads and required Falcon Heavy's additional performance.
6. Eutelsat HOTBIRD 13F and 13G were launched on consecutive Falcon 9 missions in 2022, replacing aging broadcast satellites serving European, Middle Eastern, and North African television markets.

## Significance

The SES-8 to BulgariaSat-1 arc — first GTO to first reused-booster customer in 3.5 years — compressed a transformation observers expected to take a decade. Premium customers entrusting billion-dollar assets to Falcon 9 validated SpaceX's engineering at the highest commercial standard.
