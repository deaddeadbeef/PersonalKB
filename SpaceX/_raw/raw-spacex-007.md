# Dragon Spacecraft Overview

## Summary
This source provides a comprehensive overview of SpaceX's Dragon spacecraft family, from the original Dragon 1 cargo capsule through the redesigned Dragon 2 in both cargo and crew variants. It covers the spacecraft's design evolution, pressurized and unpressurized cargo capabilities, docking systems, and thermal protection. Dragon became the first commercially built and operated spacecraft to deliver crew to the International Space Station.

## Key Claims
- Dragon 1 completed 20 successful Commercial Resupply Services (CRS) missions to the ISS between 2012 and 2020, establishing commercial cargo delivery as routine
- Dragon 2 was a fundamental redesign rather than an incremental upgrade, featuring autonomous docking capability, a modern avionics suite, and crew life support systems
- The PICA-X (Phenolic Impregnated Carbon Ablator) heat shield is a SpaceX-developed variant of NASA's PICA material, enabling reflight of capsules after refurbishment
- Dragon 2 supports both cargo and crew configurations with minimal hardware changes, improving fleet utilization and reducing per-mission costs
- The unpressurized trunk section provides external cargo capacity and houses Dragon's solar arrays, discarded before re-entry

## Atomic Facts
1. Dragon 2 crew variant provides 9.3 cubic meters of pressurized habitable volume, accommodating up to 4 astronauts in standard ISS crew rotation configuration (expandable to 7 for contingency)
2. Dragon 2 cargo variant offers 12.1 cubic meters of pressurized volume by removing crew seats and life support, plus additional unpressurized trunk volume
3. Dragon 1 used a berthing approach requiring the ISS Canadarm2 robotic arm to grapple and attach the capsule to a CBM port
4. Dragon 2 upgraded to autonomous docking via NASA's International Docking Adapter (IDA), eliminating the need for robotic arm capture
5. The first Dragon 1 mission (COTS Demo Flight 2, May 2012) made Dragon the first commercial spacecraft to berth with the ISS
6. Dragon 2 capsules have demonstrated reuse, with individual capsules flying up to 5 or more missions after heat shield and parachute refurbishment

## Significance
Dragon transformed the economics and logistics of ISS operations by proving that a commercial company could reliably deliver cargo and crew to the station. It ended U.S. dependence on Russian Soyuz for crew transport and established the commercial crew paradigm that now defines NASA's human spaceflight access strategy.
