## Summary

Life support for a 6-to-9-month Mars transit and extended surface stay presents challenges that dwarf anything encountered in low Earth orbit operations. The Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) must sustain crew health across two fundamentally different operating regimes: the microgravity transit phase and the partial-gravity (0.38g) Mars surface phase. The central design trade is between open-loop and closed-loop systems. Open-loop ECLSS — carrying all consumables from Earth — is simpler and more reliable but scales catastrophically with crew size and mission duration. Closed-loop systems recycle air, water, and potentially waste, reducing resupply mass but introducing mechanical complexity and failure modes. CO2 removal is the most immediately critical function. Current approaches include lithium hydroxide (LiOH) canisters, which are single-use and mass-intensive, and regenerable amine swing-bed systems that adsorb CO2 and then release it when heated, allowing indefinite cycling. Oxygen generation relies on water electrolysis — splitting H2O into breathable O2 and H2, with the hydrogen either vented or fed into a Sabatier reactor for CO2 processing. Water recycling employs multi-stage distillation and filtration, recovering approximately 90-93% of wastewater including urine and humidity condensate, as demonstrated on the ISS. Radiation represents the most intractable life-support challenge. Solar particle events (SPEs) from coronal mass ejections can deliver acute lethal doses without shielding, but are intermittent and somewhat predictable. Galactic cosmic radiation (GCR) — high-energy heavy ions from outside the solar system — delivers a chronic low dose for which no practical shielding solution exists at reasonable mass budgets. Water walls, polyethylene, and regolith burial on Mars offer partial mitigation. Food supply for early missions will be entirely pre-packaged, with future Mars greenhouse agriculture remaining a long-term aspiration dependent on surface ISRU. Psychological factors compound the physiological challenges: communication delays of 4 to 24 minutes each way eliminate real-time conversation with Earth, crew confinement in limited volume over months creates interpersonal stress, and the absence of abort-to-Earth options after TMI fundamentally alters crew psychology.

## Key Claims

- Closed-loop ECLSS is essential for Mars-duration missions because open-loop consumables mass scales prohibitively with crew size and trip time.
- Regenerable amine swing-bed CO2 removal systems offer indefinite cycling capability compared to single-use lithium hydroxide, trading simplicity for mechanical complexity.
- Water recycling systems currently achieve 90-93% recovery rates, as demonstrated on the ISS, but Mars missions may require higher efficiency.
- Galactic cosmic radiation remains an unsolved shielding problem — no practical mass-budget solution exists to reduce chronic GCR exposure during transit.
- Communication delays of 4 to 24 minutes each way and the absence of abort options create psychological stressors unprecedented in human spaceflight.

## Atomic Facts

1. Lithium hydroxide canisters remove CO2 via irreversible chemical reaction but are single-use and mass-intensive.
2. Amine swing-bed systems regenerably adsorb and release CO2 through thermal cycling, enabling indefinite reuse.
3. Water electrolysis generates breathable O2 by splitting H2O, with hydrogen as a byproduct.
4. ISS water recovery systems achieve approximately 90-93% reclamation from urine, humidity condensate, and wastewater.
5. Earth-Mars one-way communication delay ranges from approximately 4 minutes (closest approach) to 24 minutes (conjunction).
6. Solar particle events can deliver acute radiation doses within hours, while galactic cosmic radiation delivers chronic low-level exposure over months.

## Significance

Life support is where Mars colonization stops being a propulsion problem and becomes a biomedical and psychological one. The radiation challenge in particular has no clean engineering solution — it is a fundamental biological constraint that may ultimately limit transit speed requirements or demand pharmacological countermeasures not yet developed.
