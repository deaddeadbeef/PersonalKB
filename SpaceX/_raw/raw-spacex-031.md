## Summary

Eric Berger's *Liftoff* chronicles SpaceX's Falcon 1 program, from the makeshift launch site on Kwajalein Atoll to Flight 4's triumphant orbit. Falcon 1 used a single Merlin engine on the first stage and a Kestrel on the upper stage. Three consecutive failures nearly bankrupted the company before Flight 4 made SpaceX the first private firm to orbit a liquid-fueled rocket. The book captures engineers working hundred-hour weeks on a remote Pacific atoll, battling corrosion and logistics alongside the ever-present knowledge that each failure edged the company closer to collapse.

## Key Claims

- Flight 1 (March 24, 2006) failed at T+25 seconds when a corroded fuel line nut caused a Merlin engine fire, leading to loss of vehicle shortly after liftoff from Kwajalein's Omelek Island.
- Flight 2 (March 21, 2007) reached T+5 minutes and achieved stage separation, but roll control instability in the second stage caused premature shutdown before reaching orbit.
- Flight 3 (August 2, 2008) suffered a stage separation collision — residual thrust from the upgraded regeneratively-cooled Merlin 1C caused the first stage to re-contact the second stage moments after separation, destroying both stages and their payloads.
- Flight 4 (September 28, 2008) became the first privately developed liquid-fueled rocket to reach Earth orbit, launching a dummy mass payload; the fix for Flight 3 involved a longer coast period before stage separation.
- By mid-2008, SpaceX had nearly exhausted its funding and Musk's personal reserves, making Flight 4 effectively a last-chance attempt before the company would have folded.

## Atomic Facts

1. The Kwajalein Atoll launch site (Omelek Island) was selected because its equatorial location and US military presence provided both performance advantages and regulatory simplicity for early SpaceX launches.
2. Flight 1's engine fire was traced to a corroded aluminum B-nut on a fuel line fitting, exacerbated by the salt-air environment of the tropical Pacific launch site.
3. Flight 2 reached an altitude of approximately 300 km but failed to circularize due to a second-stage roll oscillation caused by sloshing of residual LOX in propellant tanks.
4. Flight 3 carried three small satellite payloads including NASA's NanoSail-D and two Department of Defense experimental satellites, all lost in the staging failure.
5. The staging fix between Flight 3 and Flight 4 was implemented in just seven weeks, requiring a longer pneumatic pusher delay to allow Merlin 1C thrust tail-off before separation.
6. Flight 5 (July 14, 2009) successfully delivered RazakSAT, a Malaysian remote-sensing satellite, to a near-equatorial orbit, marking SpaceX's first paying commercial orbital mission.

## Significance

Falcon 1 established SpaceX's iterative culture — fly, fail, fix, fly again. Flight 4's success validated the low-cost development model and directly enabled the NASA COTS award that funded Dragon and Falcon 9, transforming SpaceX from startup to credible orbital provider.
