# SpaceX Launch Operations Procedures

## Summary

A standard Falcon 9 launch follows a T-38 minute countdown beginning with the launch director's go/no-go poll. RP-1 (rocket-grade kerosene) loads first at ambient temperature. LOX loading follows, with Falcon 9 using deeply subcooled LOX chilled to approximately -207°C — roughly 24°C below its boiling point — increasing density by ~8% for more oxidizer mass in fixed tank volume. This subcooling creates a finite launch window (~30-45 minutes) before warming degrades performance. Engine chill circulates LN2 and LOX through Merlin turbopump bearings and combustion chambers to prevent thermal shock at ignition. At T-3 seconds, the nine Merlin 1D engines ignite in a staggered sequence (~0.5 s), ramping to full thrust while held by launch clamps for health verification. Clamps release at T-0. First stage flight lasts ~2 min 30 sec to MECO; stage separation via pneumatic pushers initiates parallel paths — the second stage MVac engine ignites for orbit insertion while the first stage executes recovery burns: boostback (if RTLS), three-engine entry burn, single-engine landing burn. Fairing deployment occurs ~3 minutes after launch. The MVac supports coast phases and restarts for precise GTO, direct GEO, or interplanetary injection.

## Key Claims

- The T-38 minute countdown integrates propellant loading, engine conditioning, and verification in a compressed timeline driven by subcooled LOX warming constraints
- LOX subcooled to -207°C increases density by ~8% but creates a finite launch window before warming
- Nine Merlin 1D engines ignite in staggered sequence at T-3s with thrust verification on hold-down clamps before T-0 release
- Stage separation at MECO initiates parallel operations: second stage orbit insertion and first stage recovery burns
- Merlin Vacuum supports coast and multiple restarts for LEO, GTO, and interplanetary orbit delivery

## Atomic Facts

1. The T-38 minute countdown is shorter than most launch vehicles due to automated sequences and streamlined procedures
2. Subcooled LOX at -207°C (66 K) is ~24°C below its boiling point, increasing density ~8%; introduced with Falcon 9 Full Thrust (v1.2) in 2015
3. Engine chill circulates LN2 and LOX through Merlin turbopumps and combustion chambers pre-ignition to prevent thermal shock
4. Nine first-stage Merlin 1D engines ignite sequentially over ~0.5 seconds at T-3s, with health verification before clamp release at T-0
5. First stage recovery involves up to three burns: boostback (trajectory reversal), entry (three-engine deceleration), and landing (single-engine precision descent)
6. Merlin Vacuum restart capability enables complex orbit insertions including direct GEO, precise LEO planes, and interplanetary injection

## Significance

The Falcon 9 timeline embodies the tension between performance optimization and operational risk. Subcooled LOX pushes vehicle performance but constrains the launch window. The highly automated countdown — far shorter than legacy vehicles — enables high cadence through redundant automated checks rather than lengthy manual verification.
