# Propulsive Landing Development Timeline

## Summary

SpaceX's propulsive landing program progressed from the Grasshopper test vehicle (2012-2013) through F9R Dev (2014) to operational recovery attempts on orbital missions. Grasshopper completed eight flights at McGregor, reaching 744 m altitude. Early barge landing attempts on CRS-5 (January 2015) and CRS-6 (April 2015) demonstrated the approach was viable but revealed hardware limits — CRS-5 crashed due to grid fin hydraulic fluid depletion, CRS-6 tipped after touchdown. The first successful return-to-launch-site landing came on OG-2 (December 2015) at Landing Zone 1, followed by the first autonomous drone ship landing on CRS-8 (April 2016). The core technical innovation is supersonic retropropulsion guided by convex optimization (G-FOLD algorithm), enabling real-time fuel-optimal trajectory computation. The landing sequence transitions from a three-engine entry burn to a single-engine landing burn, executing autonomously from MECO to touchdown in approximately eight minutes.

## Key Claims

- Grasshopper and F9R Dev provided foundational VTOL data across 12+ test flights between 2012 and 2014
- CRS-5 and CRS-6 barge attempts in early 2015 were near-misses that validated the approach while revealing specific hardware limitations
- Supersonic retropropulsion was demonstrated operationally for the first time in spaceflight history
- The G-FOLD convex optimization algorithm enables real-time computation of fuel-optimal landing trajectories with guaranteed convergence
- The three-engine to one-engine landing sequence balances deceleration requirements against fuel efficiency across descent phases

## Atomic Facts

1. Grasshopper was a modified Falcon 9 v1.0 first stage with fixed landing legs that conducted 8 test flights from September 2012 to October 2013, reaching 744 m maximum altitude
2. F9R Dev added deployable landing legs and grid fins, flew April to August 2014 before the program transitioned to operational recovery attempts
3. CRS-5 (January 2015) was the first ASDS landing attempt; the booster reached the barge but crashed due to hydraulic fluid depletion in grid fin actuators
4. OG-2 (December 2015) achieved the first RTLS landing of an orbital-class booster at Cape Canaveral's LZ-1
5. CRS-8 (April 2016) was the first successful drone ship landing, enabling recovery on high-energy missions that cannot return to the launch site
6. The G-FOLD algorithm, originally developed by Behçet Açıkmeşe at JPL for Mars landing, was adapted by SpaceX for real-time booster landing guidance with guaranteed fuel-optimal solutions

## Significance

The propulsive landing timeline demonstrates SpaceX's iterate-fast philosophy — test articles to operational attempts in under three years, tolerating public failures as learning data. Supersonic retropropulsion and autonomous precision landing created the technical foundation for routine booster reuse and fundamentally altered launch economics.
