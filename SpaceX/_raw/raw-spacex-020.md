# McGregor Test Facility Overview

## Summary

SpaceX's McGregor, Texas test facility is the primary engine and vehicle testing site, where every Merlin engine, Raptor engine, and Falcon 9 first stage is tested before flight. Acquired in 2003 from defunct Beal Aerospace — which had invested ~$200M in rocket development before ceasing operations in 2000 — the site provided existing test infrastructure at a fraction of new-build cost. The facility covers 4,000+ acres of Central Texas land ~20 miles west of Waco, providing buffer zones for simultaneous multi-stand testing. Merlin engines are tested on horizontal stands, simplifying installation, instrumentation, and post-test inspection. Each Merlin 1D undergoes a full-duration acceptance firing before vehicle integration. Raptor engines are tested on vertical stands replicating flight orientation, accommodating higher thrust levels and gimbal actuation under load; hundreds of tests supported iterative full-flow staged combustion development. Every Falcon 9 first stage (nine Merlin engines integrated) undergoes a horizontal static fire acceptance test at McGregor — all nine engines firing simultaneously to verify propellant feed, avionics, and thrust vector control. Dragon thruster testing covers both SuperDraco launch escape engines (~73 kN each) and Draco maneuvering thrusters (400 N each). The facility's expansion over two decades mirrors SpaceX's growth from a startup testing its first engines to processing dozens of stages and hundreds of engines per year.

## Key Claims

- Acquired from Beal Aerospace in 2003, providing existing test infrastructure that accelerated early development at minimal capital cost
- The 4,000+ acre site provides buffer zones for simultaneous multi-stand rocket engine testing operations
- Every Merlin engine undergoes individual horizontal test firing, and every Falcon 9 first stage passes a nine-engine acceptance burn before flight
- Raptor engines are tested on vertical stands replicating flight orientation, supporting iterative full-flow staged combustion development
- The facility expanded from single-engine stands to a comprehensive complex handling Merlin, Raptor, stages, and Dragon thrusters

## Atomic Facts

1. SpaceX purchased the McGregor site from Beal Aerospace assets in 2003; Beal had invested ~$200M in rocket development before ceasing operations in 2000
2. The facility covers 4,000+ acres near McGregor, Texas (~20 miles west of Waco), providing isolation and buffer zones for large engine testing
3. Merlin 1D engines are acceptance-tested on horizontal stands, with each engine firing for a qualification or acceptance duration before vehicle integration
4. Raptor engines are tested on vertical stands accommodating full-thrust testing, gimbal actuation, and extended-duration burns
5. Every Falcon 9 first stage undergoes a horizontal nine-engine static fire verifying propellant feed, avionics, TVC, and engine-to-engine interactions
6. Dragon thruster testing covers SuperDraco launch escape engines (~73 kN each) and Draco maneuvering thrusters (400 N each)

## Significance

McGregor is the quality gate through which every SpaceX flight article passes. The test-everything-before-flight philosophy — individual engine tests, integrated stage burns, thruster qualification — is a reliability cornerstone. The site's Beal Aerospace origin also illustrates how SpaceX leveraged existing infrastructure during capital-constrained early years, a pattern repeated with the LC-39A lease.
