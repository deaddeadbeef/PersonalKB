# Super Heavy Booster Development

## Summary
This source compiles analysis of SpaceX's Super Heavy booster, the first stage of the Starship launch system and the most powerful rocket stage ever flown. It tracks the booster's rapid iterative development from early prototypes through flight-proven hardware, covering its propulsion layout, structural design, and the revolutionary tower-catch recovery concept. Super Heavy represents SpaceX's most ambitious application of its build-test-fly development philosophy.

## Key Claims
- Super Heavy's 33 Raptor engines produce approximately 7,590 tonnes-force (74,400 kN) of total liftoff thrust, roughly double that of the Saturn V
- The booster is designed for recovery via mechanical catch arms ("chopsticks") on the launch tower, eliminating the mass penalty of landing legs
- Hot-staging was adopted starting with IFT-2, where the ship's engines ignite before stage separation while still attached to the booster via a hot-staging ring
- The rapid iterative prototype approach saw SpaceX construct and test boosters B4 through B14 in approximately three years at Starbase, Boca Chica, Texas
- Stainless steel construction allows SpaceX to fabricate booster barrel sections and weld rings at a pace impossible with traditional aerospace aluminum-lithium alloys

## Atomic Facts
1. Super Heavy carries approximately 3,400 tonnes of subcooled LOX/LCH4 propellant, constituting roughly 80% of the full stack's propellant mass
2. The booster stands approximately 71 meters tall and 9 meters in diameter, constructed from rolled 304L stainless steel sheet approximately 4mm thick
3. Booster 9 (B9) became the first Super Heavy to successfully perform a controlled descent and near-catch maneuver during IFT-4 in June 2024
4. Four stainless steel grid fins, each approximately 2 meters by 4 meters, provide aerodynamic steering during descent through the atmosphere
5. The hot-staging ring is a vented interstage structure that redirects ship engine exhaust away from the booster during the staging event, adding approximately 3 seconds of overlap thrust
6. Booster 13 (B13) achieved the first successful tower catch on IFT-5 in October 2024, marking a historic milestone in rocket reusability

## Significance
Super Heavy's development demonstrates that extreme-scale rocket stages can be built and iterated rapidly outside traditional aerospace timelines. The tower-catch recovery concept, if proven reliable, eliminates landing infrastructure mass from the vehicle and enables rapid turnaround—a critical enabler for SpaceX's goal of airline-like launch operations.
