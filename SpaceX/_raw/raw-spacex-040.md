## Summary

SpaceX's failure history illustrates its "test, fail, fix, fly" philosophy. Falcon 1 Flights 1–3 (2006–2008) failed for different reasons — engine fire, roll instability, staging collision — each corrected within weeks. CRS-7 (June 28, 2015) broke apart at T+139s when a defective helium COPV strut failed at ~20% rated strength. AMOS-6 (September 1, 2016) exploded during static fire when solid oxygen between COPV overwrap layers ignited. The Starship prototype campaign (2020–2021) saw SN8–SN14 suffer landing failures before SN15 succeeded in May 2021. IFT-1 (April 2023) triggered FTS after loss of control during ascent. Each failure drove specific fixes: strut testing upgrades, COPV redesign, header tank improvements, and hot-stage separation redesign.

## Key Claims

- CRS-7 (June 28, 2015) lost at T+139s: defective COPV strut failed at ~20% rated strength, causing structural breakup.
- AMOS-6 (September 1, 2016) destroyed during static fire: solid oxygen in COPV overwrap layers ignited.
- Starship SN8–SN14 experienced landing failures (2020–2021); SN15 succeeded May 2021.
- IFT-1 (April 20, 2023) FTS activated ~4 minutes after liftoff due to loss of control.
- Each failure drove specific fixes: strut testing (CRS-7), COPV redesign (AMOS-6), header tank fixes (SN series), hot-staging (IFT-1).

## Atomic Facts

1. CRS-7 failed June 28, 2015, at T+139s; root cause: industrial steel strut securing a helium COPV failed below rated strength; return to flight occurred December 21, 2015 (Orbcomm OG-2), a six-month stand-down.
2. AMOS-6 was destroyed September 1, 2016, during static fire; root cause: solid oxygen (SOX) formed in buckled COPV liner overwrap, igniting under pressure; return to flight January 14, 2017 (Iridium-1), a 4.5-month stand-down.
3. Starship SN8 launched December 9, 2020, to ~12.5 km; crash-landed due to low header tank pressure causing insufficient thrust during the landing flip maneuver.
4. Starship SN9 (February 2, 2021) and SN10 (March 3, 2021) also experienced landing failures; SN10 touched down successfully but toppled and exploded approximately 8 minutes later due to leg/structural issues.
5. Starship SN15 successfully launched and landed May 5, 2021, incorporating improvements to the propulsion system, avionics software, and structural design from previous prototype failures.
6. IFT-1 (April 20, 2023) FTS activation was triggered after multiple Raptor engines shut down and the vehicle began tumbling; subsequent IFT-2 (November 2023) achieved stage separation but lost the booster and ship to separate anomalies.

## Significance

SpaceX's failure record demonstrates tolerance for controlled risk and iterative development. CRS-7 led to 100% acceptance testing of critical struts. AMOS-6 drove COPV redesign and revised loading procedures. The Starship campaign compressed years of traditional development into months of rapid public testing. Zero crew casualties across all crewed missions validates the approach when paired with escalated rigor for human-rated systems.
