## Summary

SpaceX's entry into US national security launches began with a legal battle against ULA's monopoly. In 2014, SpaceX sued over the Air Force's sole-source block-buy of 36 EELV cores for ULA. The settlement opened competitions and led to Falcon 9's NSSL certification in 2015. The first NSSL mission, GPS III SV01 (December 23, 2018), delivered a next-generation GPS satellite to MEO. SpaceX subsequently flew classified NRO payloads under NROL designations. USSF-44 (November 2022) was the first Falcon Heavy military mission, deploying payloads direct to GEO. The X-37B spaceplane launched on Falcon Heavy (USSF-52, December 2023) — its first flight on a commercial vehicle. SpaceX also secured SDA Tranche 0/1 contracts for proliferated LEO military communications and missile-tracking satellites.

## Key Claims

- SpaceX's 2014 lawsuit against the Air Force challenged ULA's sole-source EELV block-buy contract, arguing it violated competitive procurement law and unfairly excluded qualified providers.
- Falcon 9 was certified for national security missions in 2015 following the lawsuit settlement, opening the door for SpaceX to compete for NSSL contracts.
- GPS III SV01 (December 23, 2018) was SpaceX's first NSSL mission, delivering the first modernized GPS satellite to its operational orbit.
- USSF-44 (November 1, 2022) was the first Falcon Heavy national security mission, deploying payloads directly to geostationary Earth orbit for the US Space Force.
- SpaceX launched the X-37B spaceplane on Falcon Heavy (USSF-52, December 28, 2023), transitioning the military's autonomous orbital vehicle from ULA's Atlas V to a commercial launch provider.

## Atomic Facts

1. SpaceX filed suit against the US Air Force in April 2014, challenging the sole-source award to ULA of a block-buy contract for 36 EELV cores worth billions of dollars.
2. The Air Force certified Falcon 9 for NSSL (then called EELV) missions in May 2015, after SpaceX completed a series of required certification flights and demonstrated compliance with mission assurance requirements.
3. GPS III SV01 ("Vespucci") launched December 23, 2018, from SLC-40, Cape Canaveral; the booster was expended to maximize performance for the MEO mission profile.
4. NROL missions (e.g., NROL-76, NROL-108, NROL-87) carry classified NRO payloads; orbital parameters are not publicly disclosed.
5. USSF-44 launched November 1, 2022, from LC-39A on Falcon Heavy; both side boosters were recovered at LZ-1 and LZ-2, while the center core was expended for direct GEO insertion.
6. SDA Tranche 0 satellites launched in April 2023 aboard a Falcon 9 rideshare, establishing the initial demonstrator layer of the Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture (PWSA) for missile warning and data transport.

## Significance

The transition from suing the Air Force in 2014 to preferred NSSL provider within a decade is one of defense contracting's most dramatic shifts. SpaceX's cost advantage enabled SDA's proliferated constellation strategy, cost-prohibitive under legacy pricing. The X-37B's Falcon Heavy transition marked the Atlas V era's end in national security space.
