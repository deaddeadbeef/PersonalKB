# Fairing Recovery Program

## Summary

The Falcon 9 payload fairing costs approximately $6 million per set (two halves), making recovery worthwhile at high cadence. SpaceX initially attempted mid-air net-catching using the vessel Mr. Steven (later renamed Ms. Tree), joined by Ms. Chief — each equipped with a large net on extending arms. Fairing halves deployed parafoils after separation at ~110 km altitude and guided themselves toward the recovery vessel. Net-catching proved operationally unreliable due to wind, parafoil variability, and boat-fairing coordination precision; SpaceX transitioned to controlled water landings by late 2020. Saltwater exposure complicates refurbishment but the reliability improvement was decisive. Fairing 2.0, introduced with Block 5, added improved environmental sealing, modular electronics, and structural reinforcement for water impact loads. The refurbishment process involves freshwater rinsing, structural joint inspection, pyrotechnic bolt replacement, electronics testing, and acoustic liner recertification. The first fairing half reflight occurred in November 2019 on a Starlink mission; by 2022 most Starlink flights used at least one reused fairing half. At 50+ Starlink missions per year, fairing reuse saves an estimated $200M+ annually.

## Key Claims

- Fairing sets cost ~$6M to manufacture, the second most expensive expendable component after the first stage
- Net-catching via Ms. Tree and Ms. Chief demonstrated feasibility but was abandoned for water landing due to operational reliability
- Fairing 2.0 incorporated reuse-enabling features including improved sealing and reinforcement for water impact
- First fairing reflight occurred November 2019; by 2022 reused fairings were standard on Starlink missions
- Fairing reuse saves an estimated $4-5M per mission, translating to $200M+ annual savings at Starlink cadence

## Atomic Facts

1. Each fairing set costs approximately $6 million, representing roughly 10% of total Falcon 9 vehicle cost
2. Mr. Steven / Ms. Tree was a high-speed offshore supply vessel retrofitted with a net for mid-air fairing catches; Ms. Chief joined as a second recovery vessel
3. Net-catching was abandoned circa late 2020 due to low operational reliability from wind, parafoil variability, and coordination challenges
4. Fairing 2.0 introduced improved environmental sealing, water-impact structural reinforcement, and modular electronics for easier refurbishment
5. The first fairing half reflight (November 2019, Starlink mission) was the first operational reuse of a payload fairing on an orbital launch vehicle
6. Refurbishment involves freshwater rinsing, structural inspection, pyrotechnic bolt replacement, electronics testing, and acoustic liner recertification

## Significance

Fairing recovery exemplifies cost reduction across every vehicle component. While individually less impactful than booster reuse, fairing reuse at high cadence generates $200M+ annual savings that directly subsidize Starlink deployment. The evolution from net-catching to water landing demonstrates pragmatic trade-offs — accepting harder refurbishment for dramatically higher recovery reliability.
