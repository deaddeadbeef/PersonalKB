# Raptor Engine Technical Overview

## Summary
The Raptor engine is SpaceX's next-generation methane-oxygen rocket engine employing a full-flow staged combustion cycle, the first such engine to reach flight. It powers both the Super Heavy booster and Starship upper stage, designed for deep-space missions and planetary landing. Raptor represents a generational leap in engine performance and manufacturability over Merlin.

## Key Claims
- Raptor is the first flight-proven full-flow staged combustion cycle (FFSC) engine in history
- Chamber pressure of approximately 300 bar exceeds that of any previously flown rocket engine, including the RS-25 (~206 bar)
- The LOX/CH4 (liquid oxygen/methane) propellant combination was selected for in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) on Mars
- SpaceX's manufacturing simplification path aims to reduce Raptor production cost to well under $1 million per engine
- Raptor 3 targets approximately 280 tonnes-force of thrust with a further simplified, more compact design

## Atomic Facts
1. Raptor 2 produces approximately 230 tonnes-force (2,256 kN) of sea-level thrust, up from ~185 tf for Raptor 1
2. Specific impulse is approximately 327 seconds at sea level and 350 seconds in vacuum configuration
3. The engine uses dual preburners: an oxidizer-rich preburner driving the LOX turbopump and a fuel-rich preburner driving the methane turbopump
4. Chamber pressure of ~300 bar (4,400 psi) is achieved because both propellants enter the combustion chamber fully gasified, improving mixing and combustion efficiency
5. Raptor 3 removes the outer engine heat shield and gimbal actuator cowling, reducing mass and part count significantly compared to Raptor 2
6. SpaceX has produced over 300 Raptor engines as of mid-2024, with a target production rate exceeding one engine per day

## Significance
Raptor is the enabling technology for the entire Starship architecture and SpaceX's Mars colonization ambitions. Its combination of extreme performance, methane propellant for ISRU, and aggressive cost reduction makes it arguably the most consequential rocket engine development program since the RS-25.
