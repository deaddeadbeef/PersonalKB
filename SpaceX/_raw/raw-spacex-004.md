# Falcon Heavy Design and Operations

## Summary
The Falcon Heavy press kit documents the design, capabilities, and debut mission of SpaceX's heavy-lift launch vehicle. Composed of three Falcon 9 first-stage cores strapped together, Falcon Heavy became the most powerful operational rocket at the time of its February 2018 demonstration flight. The document covers the vehicle's architecture, performance figures, and the iconic Tesla Roadster payload.

## Key Claims
- Falcon Heavy generates approximately 5.1 million pounds-force (22,819 kN) of thrust at liftoff from 27 Merlin 1D engines
- The vehicle can deliver 63,800 kg to LEO, 26,700 kg to GTO, and 16,800 kg to Mars transfer orbit in expendable configuration
- SpaceX originally planned propellant cross-feed between boosters and the center core, but abandoned the concept as unnecessary
- The center core was structurally reinforced to withstand asymmetric aerodynamic and thrust loads from the attached side boosters
- Falcon Heavy is the most capable operational launch vehicle by payload mass to orbit, at a fraction of the cost of competing heavy-lift vehicles

## Atomic Facts
1. The demonstration flight launched on February 6, 2018 from LC-39A at Kennedy Space Center, the same pad used for Apollo 11
2. Both side boosters successfully performed return-to-launch-site (RTLS) landings at LZ-1 and LZ-2, touching down nearly simultaneously
3. The center core missed the drone ship Of Course I Still Love You by approximately 100 meters due to insufficient TEA-TEB igniter fluid for re-entry
4. Payload was Elon Musk's personal midnight cherry Tesla Roadster with a spacesuit-clad mannequin named "Starman" in the driver's seat
5. The Roadster was placed into a heliocentric orbit with an aphelion beyond the orbit of Mars, reaching approximately 1.66 AU from the Sun
6. Each side booster separates at approximately T+2 minutes 29 seconds, performing a boostback burn, re-entry burn, and landing burn sequence

## Significance
Falcon Heavy's successful demonstration proved that commercially developed vehicles could achieve heavy-lift capability previously reserved for government-funded programs. It validated SpaceX's propulsive landing approach at unprecedented scale and expanded the company's addressable launch market to include the largest national security payloads.
