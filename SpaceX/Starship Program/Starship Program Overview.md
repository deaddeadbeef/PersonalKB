# Starship Program Overview

Starship is SpaceX's fully reusable super-heavy-lift launch system designed to carry both crew and cargo to Earth orbit, the Moon, Mars, and beyond. With a projected payload capacity exceeding 100 metric tons to LEO in fully reusable configuration, it represents the most ambitious rocket development program in history.

## Starship Architecture

The Starship system consists of two elements: the Super Heavy booster (first stage) and the Starship spacecraft (upper stage/ship). The full stack stands approximately 121 meters tall and 9 meters in diameter, constructed primarily from stainless steel 304L. Both stages are powered by Raptor engines burning liquid methane and liquid oxygen, and both are designed to be fully reusable.

## Super Heavy Booster

Super Heavy is the most powerful rocket booster ever built, equipped with 33 Raptor engines producing roughly 74 MN of liftoff thrust. It performs a boost-back maneuver after stage separation and returns to the launch site for a tower catch by the Mechazilla arms. The booster's massive propellant load and engine count provide both the performance for heavy payloads and the redundancy for engine-out capability.

## Flight Test Campaign

SpaceX has pursued an aggressive iterative flight test approach, accepting hardware losses in exchange for rapid learning. The first integrated flight test (IFT-1, April 2023) ended in a commanded destruction after loss of control. Subsequent flights have demonstrated stage separation, ship reentry survival, booster catch, and progressively longer mission profiles. Each test feeds data directly into the next vehicle iteration.

## Orbital Refueling

Delivering large payloads beyond LEO — particularly to the Moon or Mars — requires on-orbit refueling of the Starship upper stage. SpaceX is developing ship-to-ship cryogenic propellant transfer technology, which NASA considers critical for the Human Landing System contract. This capability, once proven, enables mission architectures that no expendable vehicle could achieve.

## Human Landing System (HLS)

NASA selected Starship as the Human Landing System for the Artemis program, awarding SpaceX a $2.89 billion contract (later supplemented) to land astronauts on the lunar surface. The HLS variant features additional engines for lunar landing, no heat shield (it doesn't return to Earth), and internal volume dwarfing the Apollo Lunar Module. It requires orbital refueling before translunar injection.

## Starship Variants

SpaceX envisions multiple Starship configurations: a standard cargo/tanker variant, the HLS lunar lander, a crew transport variant with cabins and life support, a point-to-point Earth transport vehicle, and potentially a space station or orbital depot. The common airframe and engine set across variants is intended to drive down per-unit costs through high production rates.
