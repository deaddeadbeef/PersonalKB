# Engines and Propulsion Overview

Rocket engines are the most complex and performance-critical components in any launch vehicle, and SpaceX's engine development has been central to its competitive advantage. From the Merlin that powers Falcon 9 to the Raptor that will take humans to Mars, SpaceX designs and manufactures all of its engines in-house.

## Merlin Engine Family

The Merlin is a gas-generator cycle engine burning LOX and RP-1 kerosene. It has evolved from the Merlin 1A on Falcon 1 to the Merlin 1D+ on Falcon 9 Block 5, with sea-level thrust increasing from 340 kN to over 981 kN. Merlin's simplicity, reliability, and high thrust-to-weight ratio made it the workhorse that built SpaceX's launch business.

## Raptor Engine

Raptor is a full-flow staged combustion (FFSC) cycle engine burning liquid methane and liquid oxygen. It achieves chamber pressures exceeding 300 bar, making it one of the highest-performance rocket engines ever built. Raptor powers both the Starship upper stage and the Super Heavy booster, and its methane propellant was chosen for its compatibility with in-situ resource utilization on Mars.

## Draco and SuperDraco Thrusters

Draco thrusters provide on-orbit maneuvering and attitude control for Dragon spacecraft, each producing about 400 N of thrust using hypergolic propellants (MMH/NTO). SuperDraco engines, producing 73 kN each, power Dragon 2's launch escape system and were originally designed for propulsive landing. These smaller engines demonstrate SpaceX's breadth across the thrust spectrum.

## Full-Flow Staged Combustion Cycle

The FFSC cycle used in Raptor is the most efficient and complex rocket engine thermodynamic cycle. Both propellants are fully gasified in separate preburners before entering the main combustion chamber, eliminating the fuel-rich or oxidizer-rich turbine exhaust of simpler cycles. SpaceX is only the second entity to fly an FFSC engine, after the Soviet RD-270 test program.

## Engine Manufacturing

SpaceX manufactures engines at its Hawthorne, California headquarters and is scaling production at Starbase in Boca Chica, Texas. The company produces more rocket engines annually than any other organization in the world. Vertical integration — from casting turbopump components to final acceptance testing — gives SpaceX cost and schedule control that competitors using subcontracted engines cannot match.

## Raptor Evolution and Raptor 3

Raptor has undergone rapid iteration: Raptor 1 flew on early Starship tests, Raptor 2 increased thrust and simplified the design, and Raptor 3 eliminates the outer engine jacket by channeling all cooling through the regeneratively cooled chamber and nozzle. Each generation reduces part count, mass, and cost while increasing thrust and reliability.
