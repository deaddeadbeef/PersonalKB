# Launch Operations and Facilities Overview

SpaceX operates a network of launch sites, test facilities, and manufacturing centers that enable the highest launch cadence in the world. The integration of production, testing, and launch operations under one company is a key enabler of SpaceX's speed and cost advantages.

## LC-39A, Kennedy Space Center

Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center is SpaceX's premier East Coast launch pad, handling both Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy missions. Originally built for Apollo Saturn V rockets and later modified for the Space Shuttle, SpaceX leased and extensively rebuilt it starting in 2014. It supports crew missions to the ISS and high-priority government payloads.

## SLC-40, Cape Canaveral Space Force Station

Space Launch Complex 40 is SpaceX's high-cadence Falcon 9 pad, rebuilt after the AMOS-6 explosion in 2016. It handles the majority of Starlink deployment missions and commercial satellite launches. Its rapid turnaround capability — sometimes launching twice in a single day from adjacent pads — is central to SpaceX's unprecedented launch tempo.

## Vandenberg Space Force Base

SLC-4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California provides access to polar and sun-synchronous orbits that cannot be reached from Florida. SpaceX uses it for Earth observation, reconnaissance, and Starlink polar shell missions. The site includes Landing Zone 4 for booster recovery on West Coast missions.

## Starbase, Boca Chica

Starbase in Boca Chica, Texas is SpaceX's dedicated Starship development and launch facility. It includes production tents, a suborbital and orbital launch pad with integration tower (Mechazilla), tank farm, and testing infrastructure. Starbase serves as the primary site for Starship flight tests and is being expanded for operational launches.

## McGregor Test Facility

The McGregor, Texas facility is SpaceX's primary engine and stage testing site. Every Merlin and Raptor engine is acceptance-tested here before shipment. Full Falcon 9 first stages undergo static fire testing on horizontal test stands. McGregor has been in continuous operation since 2003 and has tested thousands of engines.

## Mission Control and Operations

SpaceX operates its primary mission control from its Hawthorne, California headquarters, with additional facilities at launch sites. The mission control team manages countdowns, flight monitoring, landing operations, and Dragon missions. SpaceX's operations team also manages drone ship fleet operations and booster processing at port facilities.
