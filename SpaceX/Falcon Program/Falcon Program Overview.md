# Falcon Program Overview

The Falcon family of launch vehicles — Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy — transformed the commercial launch market by combining reliable performance with unprecedented cost efficiency. Together they form the backbone of SpaceX's revenue and have established the company as the world's most prolific launch provider.

## Falcon 9 Architecture

Falcon 9 is a two-stage, LOX/RP-1 rocket standing 70 meters tall with a 5.2-meter diameter. The first stage is powered by nine Merlin 1D engines producing roughly 7,600 kN of thrust at sea level. The second stage uses a single vacuum-optimized Merlin engine and is expendable on every flight. This simple, proven architecture has been the key to its reliability record.

## Falcon 9 Evolution

The vehicle has gone through several major iterations: Falcon 9 v1.0 (2010), v1.1 (2013), Full Thrust (2015), and Block 5 (2018). Each version increased performance, improved reusability margins, and refined manufacturing. Block 5 was designed from the outset for rapid reuse with minimal refurbishment, targeting 10 flights per booster with only inspections between missions.

## Block 5 and Rapid Reuse

Block 5 introduced thermal protection coatings on the octaweb, redesigned titanium grid fins, and upgraded Merlin engines rated for higher thrust. Multiple boosters have now exceeded 20 flights each. Turnaround times have dropped from months to weeks, approaching SpaceX's goal of aircraft-like operations for first stages.

## Falcon Heavy

Falcon Heavy consists of three Falcon 9 first-stage cores strapped together with a strengthened center core. It can deliver approximately 64 metric tons to low Earth orbit, making it the most powerful operational rocket until Starship. It debuted spectacularly in February 2018 carrying Musk's Tesla Roadster and has since launched high-energy national security and scientific payloads.

## Launch Cadence and Reliability

SpaceX has achieved an extraordinary launch cadence, exceeding 90 Falcon 9 launches in a single year. The vehicle's reliability exceeds 99% across hundreds of missions. This cadence is enabled by a combination of reusable hardware, streamlined pad operations, and a production line that can deliver new boosters and upper stages on demand.

## Specifications and Performance

Falcon 9 delivers approximately 22,800 kg to LEO and 8,300 kg to GTO in reusable mode. Falcon Heavy extends this to roughly 63,800 kg to LEO expendable. Both vehicles use autonomous flight safety systems (AFTS) instead of range safety officers, supporting rapid launch scheduling from multiple pads.
