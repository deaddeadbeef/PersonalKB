## Founding & Early Funding

**Q1. When was SpaceX founded and where?**
> **A1.** May 2002, in El Segundo, California. (Incorporated 14 March 2002.)

**Q2. How much of his own money did Elon Musk initially invest in SpaceX?**
> **A2.** ~$100 million from his PayPal / Zip2 proceeds, funding the company through the first three (failed) Falcon 1 flights.

**Q3. What was the financial situation heading into Falcon 1 Flight 4?**
> **A3.** SpaceX was nearly bankrupt — Musk has stated Flight 4 was the last launch the company could afford. Its success in September 2008 kept SpaceX alive and helped secure the NASA COTS contract shortly afterward.

## NASA Contracts

**Q4. What was the COTS program and how much was SpaceX awarded?**
> **A4.** Commercial Orbital Transportation Services — NASA funded private companies to develop ISS cargo delivery. SpaceX received ~$396 million (2006) to develop the Falcon 9 and Dragon spacecraft.

**Q5. What are the CRS-1 and CRS-2 contract values?**
> **A5.** CRS-1: ~$1.6 billion for 12 resupply missions (2012–2020). CRS-2: ~$4.9 billion (extended, shared with Northrop Grumman and Sierra Space) for continued ISS resupply through 2026+.

**Q6. What is the CCtCap contract and its value?**
> **A6.** Commercial Crew Transportation Capability — NASA awarded SpaceX ~$2.6 billion (September 2014) to develop and certify Crew Dragon for routine astronaut transport to ISS.

**Q7. What is the HLS Option A contract value?**
> **A7.** ~$2.89 billion (April 2021) for developing a Starship variant as NASA's Human Landing System for the Artemis III lunar mission. A subsequent modification (Option B for Artemis IV) added ~$1.15 billion.

## Pricing & Revenue

**Q8. What is the approximate list price of a Falcon 9 launch?**
> **A8.** ~$67 million (published price for standard missions). Internal marginal cost is estimated far lower due to booster reuse — possibly $15–20 million.

**Q9. What is Falcon Heavy's approximate list price?**
> **A9.** ~$97 million (published price, partially reusable configuration).

**Q10. Approximately how many Starlink subscribers does SpaceX have and what is the estimated annual revenue?**
> **A10.** ~4 million+ subscribers across 75+ countries (as of late 2024). Estimated annual Starlink revenue is ~$6.6 billion (2024), making it SpaceX's largest revenue stream.

## Competition & Industry

**Q13. Name SpaceX's primary launch competitors and their vehicles.**
> **A13.**
> - **ULA** — Vulcan Centaur (replacing Atlas V / Delta IV)
> - **Arianespace** — Ariane 6
> - **Rocket Lab** — Electron (small-sat), Neutron (in development)
> - **Blue Origin** — New Glenn
> - **CASC (China)** — Long March 5/6/7 family
> - **ISRO (India)** — LVM3 / SSLV
> - **Roscosmos** — Soyuz-2 / Angara

**Q14. What is the NSSL program and SpaceX's role in it?**
> **A14.** National Security Space Launch — the U.S. DoD program for assured access to space. In Phase 2 (2022–2027), SpaceX won ~60% of NSSL missions (ULA won ~40%). Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, and (eventually) Starship are NSSL-certified vehicles.

**Q15. How does SpaceX's internal cost advantage compare to competitors?**
> **A15.** SpaceX's reuse model gives it an estimated 2–5× cost advantage per kg to orbit over expendable competitors. Vertical integration (SpaceX manufactures ~80% of components in-house) further reduces costs. This pricing pressure has driven competitors (ULA, Arianespace, Blue Origin) to develop partially or fully reusable next-generation vehicles.
