## Integrated Flight Tests

**Q1. When was IFT-1 and what happened?**
> **A1.** 20 April 2023. Booster and Ship failed to separate; vehicle began tumbling at ~39 km altitude. AFTS triggered at T+4 min. The launch pad suffered severe damage (no flame diverter/water deluge in place).

**Q2. What were the key outcomes of IFT-2?**
> **A2.** 18 November 2023. Successful hot-stage separation (first ever). Super Heavy booster exploded shortly after separation (RUD during boostback burn). Ship reached ~148 km altitude before loss of contact/AFTS at T+8 min (likely LOX leak). Demonstrated upgraded pad with water-cooled steel plate.

**Q3. Describe IFT-3's achievements.**
> **A3.** 14 March 2024. Booster completed boostback burn and soft splashdown attempt (lost control near surface). Ship reached orbital velocity for the first time, coasted for ~40 min, then broke up during reentry over the Indian Ocean. Successfully demonstrated propellant transfer test (header tank to header tank), payload door opening, and Raptor relight in space.

**Q4. What did IFT-4 accomplish?**
> **A4.** 6 June 2024. Booster achieved controlled soft splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico (first successful booster landing profile). Ship survived reentry — reaching the targeted splashdown zone in the Indian Ocean despite a damaged flap. Demonstrated full end-to-end mission profile for the first time.

**Q5. What was the landmark achievement of IFT-5?**
> **A5.** 13 October 2024. First successful tower catch of the Super Heavy booster by the Mechazilla chopstick arms at Starbase. Ship reached splashdown target in Indian Ocean with improved TPS performance. This was the first time a large launch vehicle booster was caught instead of landing on legs or a pad.

**Q6. What milestones did IFT-6 achieve?**
> **A6.** 21 November 2024. Second successful tower catch of Super Heavy. First flight of Raptor 3 engines. Ship reached targeted splashdown in Indian Ocean. Demonstrated further TPS improvements and refined catch sequence.

## Orbital Refueling

**Q7. What is the orbital refueling concept and why is it necessary?**
> **A7.** Starship's Mars/Moon payload capacity requires refilling propellant tanks in LEO after launch. A tanker variant of Starship launches and docks with the depot/mission Ship, transferring LOX and LCH₄. An estimated 5–15 tanker flights are needed per deep-space mission depending on destination. NASA requires an orbital propellant transfer demo before Artemis III.

**Q8. What is the propellant depot Starship variant?**
> **A8.** A modified Starship that remains in orbit as a propellant storage facility with extra insulation and active thermal management to minimize boil-off. Tanker Ships launch and transfer propellant to the depot, which then transfers it to the mission Ship. This decouples tanker launch cadence from the mission timeline.

## Human Landing System (HLS)

**Q9. What is the Starship HLS variant and how does it differ from the standard Ship?**
> **A9.** The HLS (Human Landing System) Starship is modified for lunar operations: it has high-mounted engines (thrusters far from the surface to avoid regolith plume damage), an elevator system for crew surface access from the high crew cabin, additional solar arrays, no heat shield or flaps (it never reenters Earth's atmosphere), and enhanced life-support systems. It is designed to land on and launch from the lunar surface.

**Q10. What Artemis missions are Starship HLS contracted for?**
> **A10.** Artemis III (first crewed lunar landing, contracted under Option A ~$2.89B) and Artemis IV (Option B, ~$1.15B additional). The Starship HLS will be pre-positioned in lunar orbit; crew arrives separately via Orion/SLS.

## Starship Variants & Configuration

**Q11. How many Raptor engines are on each stage?**
> **A11.** Super Heavy booster: 33 Raptors (20 inner ring fixed + 13 outer ring gimbaling). Starship upper stage: 6 Raptors (3 sea-level + 3 vacuum-optimized).

**Q12. Name the planned Starship variants.**
> **A12.**
> - **Crew** — passenger transport (Earth-to-Earth, Mars, Moon)
> - **Cargo** — large-volume payload delivery
> - **Tanker** — propellant transfer to depot/mission Ship
> - **Depot** — orbital propellant storage
> - **HLS** — lunar lander for Artemis (NASA)
> - **Starshield** — national security / military variant

## Tower Catch & Operations

**Q13. How does the Mechazilla tower catch system improve operations?**
> **A13.** By catching the booster with tower arms instead of landing on legs, it: (1) eliminates ~10 t of landing leg dry mass from the booster, (2) positions the booster directly above the launch mount for rapid restacking, (3) enables a target of <1 hour turnaround, and (4) removes the need for a separate landing pad and crane operations.

**Q14. What performance targets does SpaceX have for Starship?**
> **A14.** 100–150 t to LEO (reusable), up to 200+ t expendable; $10/kg to orbit target cost; 1,000+ flights per vehicle; aircraft-like turnaround cadence (hours, not weeks); full and rapid reusability of both stages.

**Q15. What is the significance of orbital refueling for Mars colonization?**
> **A15.** Without refueling, Starship cannot carry meaningful payload beyond LEO due to the tyranny of the rocket equation. Orbital refueling breaks this constraint by allowing the vehicle to launch nearly empty (maximizing structural payload to orbit), then top off propellant for the high-delta-v trans-Mars injection. This architecture makes 100+ tonne payloads to Mars feasible with a reusable system — the cornerstone of SpaceX's Mars colonization plan.
