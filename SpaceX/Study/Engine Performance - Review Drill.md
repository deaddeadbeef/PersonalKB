# Engine Performance — Review Drill

> 15 Q&A pairs · Covers every SpaceX engine from Draco to Raptor 3
> **How to use:** Cover the **A** column, answer from memory, then check.

---

## Merlin Family

**Q1. What is the sea-level thrust of a single Merlin 1D engine?**
> **A1.** ~845 kN (~190,000 lbf) at sea level.

**Q2. What is the vacuum thrust and Isp of the Merlin Vacuum (MVac) engine?**
> **A2.** ~981 kN thrust; Isp ~348 s in vacuum.

**Q3. What propellant combination and engine cycle does Merlin 1D use?**
> **A3.** LOX/RP-1 (kerosene), gas-generator cycle.

**Q4. What is the sea-level Isp of Merlin 1D?**
> **A4.** ~282 s at sea level.

**Q5. What is a pintle injector and why does SpaceX use it?**
> **A5.** A single-element injector where propellants meet via a central post and annular gap. SpaceX uses it because it is simpler to manufacture, inherently combustion-stable (resists destructive oscillations), and easy to throttle over a wide range. Heritage from the Apollo LM descent engine (TRW).

## Raptor Family

**Q6. What is the sea-level thrust of a Raptor 2 engine?**
> **A6.** ~230 tf (~2,256 kN) at sea level.

**Q7. What propellant combination and engine cycle does Raptor use?**
> **A7.** Sub-cooled LOX/liquid methane (CH₄), full-flow staged combustion (FFSC) cycle.

**Q8. What is Raptor 2's approximate chamber pressure?**
> **A8.** ~300 bar (~4,400 psi) — among the highest of any operational rocket engine.

**Q9. What is the vacuum Isp of Raptor (vacuum-optimized variant)?**
> **A9.** ~380 s (RVac).

**Q10. Describe the Raptor 1 → 2 → 3 progression in key terms.**
> **A10.**
> - **Raptor 1:** ~185 tf thrust, ~250 bar chamber pressure, ~1,600 kg mass. Proof of FFSC concept.
> - **Raptor 2:** ~230 tf thrust, ~300 bar, mass reduced to ~1,525 kg. Simplified manufacturing (fewer parts, reduced welds).
> - **Raptor 3:** Target ~280 tf thrust, ~350 bar, ~1,470 kg. Nozzle-less (film-cooled open nozzle extension on some variants), further part-count reduction. First flew on IFT-6.

## Reaction Control & Auxiliary Engines

**Q11. What are the thrust and propellant of a single Draco thruster?**
> **A11.** ~400 N (~90 lbf) thrust, using MMH/NTO (hypergolic). Used for on-orbit maneuvering on Dragon.

**Q12. What are the thrust and role of SuperDraco engines?**
> **A12.** ~73 kN (~16,400 lbf) each, eight per Crew Dragon (in four pairs). They power the launch escape system (LES). They are 3-D printed (Inconel) and hypergolic (MMH/NTO).

## Cycle Comparison & Concepts

**Q13. What is the key difference between a gas-generator cycle and a full-flow staged combustion cycle?**
> **A13.** In a **gas generator** cycle, a small amount of propellant is burned to drive turbopumps, then dumped overboard — reducing overall efficiency. In **FFSC**, *all* oxidizer drives one turbopump (oxygen-rich preburner) and *all* fuel drives another (fuel-rich preburner); both streams then enter the main chamber. This yields higher Isp, higher chamber pressure, and less turbine thermal stress since each preburner runs cooler.

**Q14. Why is methane preferred over RP-1 for Raptor/Starship?**
> **A14.** Methane burns cleaner (less coking/soot), enabling rapid engine reuse; has higher Isp than RP-1 at similar mixture ratios; is easier to sub-cool for density advantages; and is theoretically producible on Mars via the Sabatier reaction using CO₂ and H₂O — enabling ISRU propellant production.

**Q15. Raptor is the first operational FFSC engine. What was the only prior FFSC engine to reach test-stand level?**
> **A15.** The Soviet RD-270, developed in the 1960s for the UR-700 program. It used N₂O₄/UDMH and reached full-power test firings but never flew. (The Integrated Powerhead Demonstrator by Aerojet in the 2000s also demonstrated the cycle at component level.)
