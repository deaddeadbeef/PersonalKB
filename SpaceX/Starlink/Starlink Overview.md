# Starlink Overview

Starlink is SpaceX's satellite internet constellation designed to provide high-speed, low-latency broadband connectivity worldwide. With thousands of satellites in low Earth orbit, it is both the largest satellite constellation ever deployed and a critical revenue engine funding SpaceX's Mars ambitions.

## Constellation Design

Starlink operates in multiple orbital shells between roughly 340 km and 570 km altitude, with orbital inclinations chosen to maximize coverage of populated areas. The low altitude minimizes signal latency to 20–40 ms — competitive with terrestrial broadband — and ensures that defunct satellites deorbit naturally within a few years. The constellation is designed for continuous global coverage with inter-satellite handoffs.

## Satellite Generations

Starlink satellites have evolved rapidly across generations. The v1.0 satellites weighed approximately 260 kg and provided Ku-band connectivity. The v1.5 generation added laser inter-satellite links. The v2 Mini satellites are larger, more capable, and fit within the Falcon 9 fairing. Full-size v2 satellites, designed for Starship's larger fairing, will dramatically increase per-satellite capacity.

## Laser Inter-Satellite Links

Beginning with the v1.5 generation, Starlink satellites carry laser terminals enabling direct communication between satellites without routing through ground stations. This mesh network reduces latency for long-distance connections, enables coverage over oceans and polar regions without ground infrastructure, and creates a space-based backbone competitive with fiber for intercontinental routes.

## Ground Infrastructure

The Starlink user terminal ("Dishy McFlatface") is a phased-array antenna that automatically orients itself toward overhead satellites. SpaceX has iterated on terminal design to reduce cost from an estimated $3,000 to under $600 per unit. Ground stations (gateways) distributed worldwide connect the constellation to the terrestrial internet backbone.

## Direct-to-Cell

Starlink's direct-to-cell service, developed in partnership with T-Mobile and other carriers, enables unmodified smartphones to connect directly to Starlink satellites for text, voice, and eventually data service. This eliminates dead zones in remote and rural areas without requiring specialized hardware, representing a massive addressable market expansion beyond traditional satellite broadband.

## Business Model

Starlink generates revenue through consumer and enterprise subscriptions, government contracts (including military and disaster response), and aviation/maritime connectivity. Analyst estimates place Starlink's annual revenue in the multi-billion dollar range, making it SpaceX's largest revenue source. This cash flow is essential for funding Starship development and the Mars program.
