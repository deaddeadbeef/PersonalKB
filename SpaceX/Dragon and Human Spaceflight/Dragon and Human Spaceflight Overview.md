# Dragon and Human Spaceflight Overview

Dragon is SpaceX's spacecraft family for cargo delivery and crew transport to the International Space Station and beyond. The Crew Dragon variant restored American human orbital launch capability in 2020, ending a nine-year dependence on Russian Soyuz vehicles after the Space Shuttle retirement.

## Cargo Dragon

Cargo Dragon (originally Dragon 1, now Dragon 2 in cargo configuration) delivers supplies, experiments, and hardware to the ISS under NASA's Commercial Resupply Services (CRS) contracts. It is the only active cargo vehicle capable of returning significant mass to Earth, enabling retrieval of science samples and equipment. SpaceX has completed dozens of cargo missions since the first berthing in May 2012.

## Crew Dragon

Crew Dragon is a human-rated variant featuring life support systems, a launch escape system powered by eight SuperDraco engines, and a touchscreen-based cockpit. It can carry up to four astronauts and autonomously dock with the ISS. The vehicle was developed under NASA's Commercial Crew Program, which awarded SpaceX a $2.6 billion contract to develop and certify it.

## Commercial Crew Program

NASA's Commercial Crew Program (CCP) was a paradigm shift in how the agency procured human spaceflight services. Instead of owning the vehicle, NASA bought seats on commercially developed spacecraft. SpaceX's Demo-1 (uncrewed, March 2019) and Demo-2 (crewed, May 2020, astronauts Hurley and Behnken) validated the approach and certified Dragon for operational missions.

## Operational Crew Missions

Following certification, SpaceX has flown regular crew rotation missions (Crew-1 through Crew-9+) to the ISS, typically carrying four astronauts for six-month stays. These missions have established a reliable cadence and proven the vehicle's systems across dozens of crew-days in orbit. Dragon has also served as a lifeboat during crew overlaps on the station.

## Private and Commercial Missions

Beyond NASA, Dragon has enabled a new market for private human spaceflight. Inspiration4 (September 2021) was the first all-civilian orbital mission, flying four private citizens on a multi-day free-flight. Axiom Space has contracted Dragon for private astronaut missions to the ISS, and Polaris Dawn (2024) pushed the envelope with the first commercial spacewalk.

## Life Support and Safety Systems

Dragon's Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) manages atmosphere composition, temperature, humidity, and CO₂ removal for up to seven crew members. The integrated launch escape system can pull the capsule away from a failing rocket at any point from pad to orbit. These systems underwent extensive testing, including an in-flight abort demonstration in January 2020.
