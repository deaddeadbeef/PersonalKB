# Origins and History Overview

SpaceX was founded in 2002 by Elon Musk with the audacious goal of reducing space transportation costs and enabling the colonization of Mars. Understanding the company's origins reveals the risk tolerance, iterative engineering philosophy, and long-term vision that continue to define every program SpaceX undertakes.

## Founding and Early Vision

In 2001, Elon Musk attempted to purchase refurbished ICBMs from Russia to send a greenhouse to Mars as an inspirational project. When the Russians quoted exorbitant prices, Musk concluded he could build rockets cheaper himself. He incorporated Space Exploration Technologies Corp. in May 2002 with $100 million of his own money from the PayPal sale.

## The Falcon 1 Era

Falcon 1 was SpaceX's first orbital launch vehicle — a small, two-stage rocket powered by a single Merlin engine. The first three launches between 2006 and 2008 all failed, each for different reasons. The fourth flight in September 2008 became the first privately developed liquid-fueled rocket to reach orbit, saving the company from bankruptcy.

## Key Milestones

SpaceX's trajectory from startup to dominant launch provider followed a remarkable pace. The company won its first NASA contract (COTS) in 2006, successfully launched Falcon 9 in 2010, berthed Dragon with the ISS in 2012, and landed a booster in 2015. Each milestone built credibility and unlocked the next tier of contracts and ambition.

## Company Culture and Philosophy

SpaceX operates with a flat hierarchy, rapid iteration, and vertical integration that sets it apart from traditional aerospace. Engineers are expected to own problems end-to-end, hardware is tested to failure rather than analyzed to perfection, and the company manufactures roughly 80% of its components in-house. This culture enables a development speed that legacy primes cannot match.

## Failures and Resilience

The company has faced existential crises multiple times — three consecutive Falcon 1 failures, the CRS-7 in-flight breakup in 2015, and the AMOS-6 pad explosion in 2016. In each case, SpaceX conducted transparent investigations, implemented fixes, and returned to flight faster than industry norms. This resilience is a core organizational trait.

## Long-Term Vision

From its inception, SpaceX has been oriented toward making humanity a multiplanetary species. Every vehicle, engine, and business decision is evaluated against this north star. Revenue from Falcon 9, Starlink, and government contracts funds the Starship program, which is the vehicle Musk believes will ultimately carry settlers to Mars.
